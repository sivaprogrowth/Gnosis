The room was full of founders less than three years into their journey (in other words, born in the AI era), building services companies across construction/design, semiconductors deign, recruitment, tax advisory, media services and more. Based on this anecdotal data as well as the volume of companies we are seeing in the past 18 months, AI-led services is a huge interest area for founders in India.

In late 2024, we published ***[AI Will Eat Services](https://lsvp.com/stories/ai-will-eat-services/)***. Its argument: AI is expanding the market for software from productivity tools (roughly $300B of SaaS spend) to the work itself - the $2T+ spent on outsourced digital work. A large set of services industries are facing existential risks - think law firms, creative agencies, SEO firms, management consulting firms, accounting firms, IT services firms and BPOs. Our view is that AI-led services will be one of the defining business models of this shift and has the potential to create several new venture-backed winners.

Lightspeed has led early-stage financings in several companies in this thesis, including **[Aqqrue](https://www.aqqrue.com/)**, **[Bridgetown](https://www.bridgetownresearch.com/)**, **[Gush](https://www.gushwork.ai/)**, **[Level3](https://www.level3.ai/)** and **[Pepper](https://www.pepper.inc/)**.

Here is what we learnt that could be actionable for founders building AI-led services companies, particularly those building toward top-tier venture funding.

## Strategy

**Pick which of the three forms you are building.**

- A1) Selling and delivering AI-powered services to enterprises: Pepper for GEO/SEO outcomes, Pixis in performance marketing, Bridgetown in commercial diligence for PE firms. **Organic buildout**.
- A2) Selling and delivering AI-powered services to enterprises. **Inorganic buildout or roll-up strategy**. Lightspeed partner Isaac Kim has written about services rollups **[here](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kimisaac_over-the-last-20-years-ive-been-involved-activity-7425238525219897344-mvRX?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAAABeoB9aa8jXW1jaOjIZmmPYnYOamGVxs)**, **[here](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kimisaac_ai-roll-ups-are-not-hard-because-the-idea-activity-7420519781734359040-DIAX?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAAABeoB9aa8jXW1jaOjIZmmPYnYOamGVxs)** and **[here](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kimisaac_most-ai-roll-ups-are-doomed-to-fail-before-activity-7417352968783597568-0Wwc?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAAABeoB9aa8jXW1jaOjIZmmPYnYOamGVxs)**.
- B) Selling an AI-native tool to services providers. I’ve seen this work mostly by selling into SMBs (as a scale up strategy) or to larger service providers (as an adjunct growth strategy or channel strategy but not the main one). Large service providers will likely, IMO, try their own hand at building out these technologies/products.
- C) Selling an AI-native tool to enterprises to replace what they used to outsource: Harvey in legal, Sierra and Decagon in support and many others. This is the most heavily financed segment of the market.

**Speak the language of outcomes to oust incumbent service providers.**

Incumbent agencies (think law firms, creative agencies, SEO firms, management consulting firms, accounting firms, IT services firms, BPOs), some sitting on $50-500mm contracts, all speak the language of inputs: hours times people/seats. They are mostly structurally unable to shift to speaking the language of outcomes, for fear of cannibalizing their own revenues and profit pools. This is a key advantage for new AI-led services startups. Wedge in the way - ask for two lines of business out of those contracts and commit to results instead. Even if you promise a 5x improvement and delivers 3x, the customer stays. They still got a 3x lift, and no other vendor was willing to be measured on results in the first place.

Price against the cost of a human doing the work. This is selling work, not software: sell a 95% improvement rather than a marginal productivity gain. And don’t put all your revenue on pay-for-results terms; pick a deliberate slice. These customers churn faster when targets slip, but the ones who stay become your strongest references.

**Go US-first.**

Selling to US companies is an ROI conversation. You spend $10, show $13 in results, and the org pays $120 next year for $100 of good work this year. In India, target only the thin tier with real willingness to pay: large banks and financial services companies and IT services firms with US exposure plus the top ~30 FMCG (CPG) companies.

## Process Automation/Delivery

**Run three tests to measure whether you are genuinely AI-native.**

In a pre-AI services company, humans were the production engine and technology the efficiency layer; in an AI-native one, the system produces and humans judge. Ask your own company and fix any “no” before you scale.

1. Does operational leverage sit in systems rather than people?
2. Is revenue decoupled from headcount?
3. Is it the system’s learning that compounds, rather than individual experience?

**Turn “learning” into a metric.**

Every AI-native services company claims its system learns, so make the claim concrete. The way Pepper’s systems learn about ways of working while serving Unilever makes the P&G client far easier to serve next. Your time-to-value should fall with every new customer you onboard.

**Rebuild your delivery pod around agents.**

Pepper’s pod went from 8-10 people on a $100-200k account to roughly 3 people and 5 agents by stripping the analyst layer and flattening the hierarchy from 3-4 levels to 1-2.

The key human role that remains is the forward-deployed engineer (FDE): one person who sits with the customer like a consultant and also writes production code, instead of handing requirements down a chain. Companies run this role differently: at Intercom, FDEs push code straight to production; at Outreach, they work mainly as consultants. Decide early how much engineer versus consultant yours will be, and start building the talent pipeline now, because the persona barely exists: ANSR is manufacturing them in-house.

**Diagnose churn by segment before you blame the product.**

Split your churn by segment (enterprise, mid-markte, SMB) before touching the roadmap.

## GTM, Budget, and Economics

**Take over an existing budget line.**

Don’t ask the customer to create a new budget for you; capture one that already exists. For Rocketlane, their wedge is to land a few seats alongside the incumbent tool, build the connectors, and expand once you reach the data.

**Go upmarket (or way downmarket!).**

Sell where speed is worth money: there seems to be a barbell here - enterprises and SMBs pay for it, while mid-market tries to in-house the work. On the SMB side, Avoca, EvenUp, Gush have successfully targeted SMBs.

**Underwrite ~60-70% gross margins, with a durability plan.**

Set the benchmark there: pre-AI services ran 20-30%, and if you’re not near 60-70%, relook at the business. According to Karthik, every industry is susceptible to compression over time so track margins by customer cohort, show them improving as automation rises, and explain why you keep 60%+ when pricing compresses.

## Team

While we did not discuss this during the session, I think this is the most important aspect of any startup, including in AI-led services. My view is that a strong AI-pilled technology founder is key in addition to a strong operator/domain expert. In case a roll-up strategy is part of the plan, then it helps to have an M&A expert on the team. It’s tempting to assume that insiders from the large incumbents will be the natural founders for new AI-led services startups - not always the case (but certainly can be).

\--

AI-led services will be a fragmented market, not a winner-take-all, and that’s fine: in a market this large, even a non-winner can be enormous. Venture firms have historically funded technology companies more often than services companies, so build to clear a high bar: margins that reach ~70%, revenue that can reach hundreds of millions of dollars within a decade, and growth that is fast and isn’t local and relationship-bound.

Clear it, and the market you are playing for is the one we described in AI Will Eat Services. Not the few hundred billion dollars a year the world spends on software, but the trillions it spends on work itself.
