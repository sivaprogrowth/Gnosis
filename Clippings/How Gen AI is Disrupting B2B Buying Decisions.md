Consider this scenario: Company A’s new cancer drug receives FDA approval based on a Phase 3 trial showing a 3.2-month improvement in progression-free survival. The company’s medical affairs team publishes the trial results in a top-tier journal, corporate communication issues a press release, and its commercial teams train sales representatives on key messages to communicate to doctors. However, Company B’s competing drug, approved six months earlier, has generated substantially more clinical discussion, real-world evidence in publications, and treatment guideline citations.

When an oncologist queries a gen AI assistant about treatment options for that specific cancer, Company B’s drug appears more prominently in the synthesized answer, accompanied by richer contextual evidence. Company A’s $2 billion investment in the new drug’s development program and $200 million annual investment in physician engagement has become secondary to algorithmic retrieval patterns shaped by publicly available, machine-readable content—something Company B excelled at creating, but Company A didn’t even consider when it designed its materials.

This scenario is no longer hypothetical. OpenEvidence, a clinical decision-support AI assistant designed for physicians, is now used daily by more than 40% of U.S. physicians, according to the company’s internal numbers. It handled over [20 million physician queries](https://www.openevidence.com/announcements/sutter-health-collaborates-with-openevidence-to-bring-evidence-based-ai-powered-insights-into-physician-workflows) in January 2026, up from approximately 2.6 million in December 2024. This sevenfold increase signals durable workflow integration rather than experimentation. OpenAI and Anthropic both doubled down on healthcare in early 2026, effectively positioning large language models (LLMs) such as [ChatGPT for Healthcare](https://openai.com/index/openai-for-healthcare/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [Claude for Healthcare and Claude for Life Sciences](https://www.anthropic.com/news/healthcare-life-sciences?utm_source=chatgpt.com) at the center of clinical processes.

Across B2B sectors, gen AI is fundamentally restructuring how businesses discover information, evaluate options, and make decisions. While much has been written about how gen AI is affecting brands in the B2C space, it’s also fundamentally reshaping complex buying journeys in B2B categories. Modern B2B buyers are increasingly using AI tools to guide discovery, compare vendors, and evaluate fit long before they even engage with salespeople. International Data Corporation (IDC) predicts that [62% of traditional B2B demand-generation will be AI-led by 2028](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/inside-the-ai-led-buyer-journey/).

## From Channel Management to Answer Engineering

Traditional B2B go-to-market strategies relied on controlled channels: sales representatives, distribution networks, partner ecosystems, and owned media properties. Awareness depended on trusted peer input, industry experts, and legacy vendor relationships, followed by a quick leap into evaluation. The evaluation phase, however, was often slow and resource intensive as buyers worked to understand offerings, explore use cases, compare prices, and build internal alignment.[Reimagining the Future of Business](https://hbr.org/insight-center/reimagining-the-future-of-business)

Gen AI has introduced a fundamentally different dynamic. Buyers access a much broader set of potential vendors, but they also eliminate options far more quickly. Among B2B technology buyers in the U.S., for example, [75% now complete their purchase journey in 12 weeks or less](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/12/05/google-survey-of-b2b-buyers/) compared to [11 months in 2024](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/#2025-findings-the-song-remains-the-same-but-the-rhythm-has-been-disrupted). The funnel widens at the top and narrows earlier. A purchasing manager may bypass product sites and sales representatives, relying instead on AI‑synthesized specs. A low‑impact press release can become a persistent data point in AI training sets. And a competitor’s framing can become the industry’s default if it is more retrievable and easier for AI to distill. Yet, traffic originating from gen AI tools remains largely invisible to companies (we’ve come to call it the “dark funnel”), leaving sellers with limited insight into how, where, and to what extent their content is influencing buyer decisions.

The implications are substantial. B2B messaging, content, and influence strategies must be redesigned for AI-driven discovery—optimized for prompts, jobs to be done, and decision support tools. Search engine optimization, or SEO, is being replaced by generative engine optimization, or GEO. Indeed, many firms may now need to rethink their entire go-to-market strategies, including how much they invest in sales force training, channel partnerships, and marketing. Yet B2B leaders have been slow to respond. Yet [McKinsey’s 2025 B2B Pulse Survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/unlocking-profitable-b2b-growth-through-gen-ai) finds that only 19% of respondents are implementing use cases involving gen AI tools for B2B buying and selling.

## Where the Disruption Hits Hardest

The pharmaceutical industry provides the clearest example of AI-driven channel displacement. [Major pharmaceutical companies collectively invest tens of billions annually](https://uspharmamarketing.com/) in physician engagement through medical science liaisons, sales representatives, continuing medical education programs, and speaker bureaus. These investments aim to ensure that physicians understand the clinical evidence supporting prescription medications and can make informed treatment decisions. Clinical AI assistants are now intermediating this relationship at scale.

Industrial manufacturing is yet another industry experiencing AI-driven channel disruption. AI-powered procurement platforms like Arkestro, Fairmarkit, and Keelvar use AI to analyze technical specifications, compare suppliers, evaluate total cost of ownership, and recommend optimal sourcing strategies. As a result, industrial engineering companies are increasingly forced to ask: How do they show up in the dark funnel where LLMs and industry specific AI tools shape early decision making? IMI, a UK-based global engineering company that specializes in fluid and motion control technology, discovered a consequential shift in its residential building business. In interviews with us, IMI executives explained how HVAC installers are no longer relying on Google search; instead, they have started researching products and looking for recommendations on ChatGPT or Gemini. As Marcella Colombino, IMI’s marketing and commercial excellence director of climate control, told us, “Industrial projects work with specifications. Today it’s much easier to find alternatives, compare features and prices, and equip yourself to push the conversation with B2B suppliers even if you don’t have the knowledge.”

The same dynamic is now emerging in banking. Even though banks have been early adopters of gen AI, most deployments remain internal and operational rather than customer facing. JPMorgan Chase, for example, reports that AI driven payment validation has [reduced fraud and lowered account validation rejection rates by roughly 20%](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/payments/security-trust/ai-payments-efficiency-fraud-reduction), delivering substantial cost savings. Similarly, a study by the Hong Kong Institute for Monetary and Financial Research found that [75% of financial institutions primarily view gen AI as a tool for productivity and operational efficiency](https://www.aof.org.hk/docs/default-source/hkimr/applied-research-report/genairep1.pdf?sfvrsn=7b0261f_0). Yet the far greater opportunity lies in using gen AI to reshape go-to-market strategy and drive revenue growth by meeting the right customers with the right message at the right time. In commercial banking and wealth management, customers are increasingly using AI tools to analyze cash management options, compare borrowing structures, and optimize treasury decisions, gradually moving away from the high touch, relationship led models anchored by relationship managers and physical branch networks.

These industries have several aspects in common: complex products and services, extensive purchasing negotiations, multi-stakeholder management, and siloed organizational structures. Ironically, the very features that once insulated these industries from disruption now make them especially exposed to GEO.

## The 4C Framework for Building Generative Readiness

So, how should organizations respond? We propose a 4C framework:

### 1\. Coordination of the narrative across functions.

Most organizations manage content through functional silos. Marketing owns promotional materials; the research team owns scientific communications; product teams own technical specifications; corporate communications handles press releases, investor relations, and public statements; and legal owns disclosures. These separations exist for valid reasons: protecting scientific integrity, mitigating promotional risk, and maintaining regulatory compliance. But GEO depends on consistent cross-functional signals such as customer behavior, product data, content performance, sales feedback, and market context combined into one view. Companies need to move away from silos to create a coordinated, cross-functional narrative focused on three outcomes:

- Accuracy: To ensure that genAI tools surface correct, consistent, and complete information from the company’s content corpus to differentiate factual claims from opinions.
- Consistency: To maintain alignment in language, framing, and emphasis across press releases, websites, technical documentation, and educational materials.
- Governance: AI systems equally weigh medical publications, press releases, corporate websites, or even blogs and sources like Reddit, making it essential to establish clear accountability when outputs contain errors or dangerous misrepresentations.

When providing content that will be digitally consumed, every function must consider how it will be pulled from a machine learning perspective.

### 2\. Citability of AI-friendly content.

Traditionally, principal investigators at large pharma prioritized top-ranked peer-reviewed journals like the *New England Journal of Medicine* where much of the content sits behind strict paywalls. However, in the GEO world, citability requires that the novel up-to-date pharma findings are “discoverable” to open source LLMs. This might mean crafting the abstract with machine-readability in mind or publishing in lower-tier journals with no paywalls or smaller disease area journals—none of which is ideal from the perspectives of the researchers. Large pharma companies have started engaging with medical journal publishers to expand access by “skipping paywalls” but with limited success. Stephen Griffiths, GSK’s publication head, specialty and general medicines, told us: “Publishers are aware of the impact of AI‑driven content access. There is growing concern that publisher platforms are being bypassed, with implications for content utilization and revenue models, driving increased discussion around linkages through MCPs (model context protocol) and copyright licensing.”

Similarly, IMI has been actively restructuring product knowledge for an AI-mediated world. A strategic pivot is underway to build “prompt authority so that LLMs consistently generate outputs aligned with IMI’s brand voice, marketing goals, and growth strategy. As told us, “AI systems rely on structured, trustworthy, machine-readable data, so the way we publish and organize information must be engineered for LLM ingestion, not just human reading. In simple terms, it’s about controlling the input to control the output.” IMI operationalized this shift by focusing on:

- Implementing product schema markup: Embedding rich, standardized schema for products, applications, components, specifications, and use-cases enable LLMs to parse relationships, performance characteristics, and intended applications with zero ambiguity.
- Developing the website content to be AI digestible: Ensure technical content reflects the questions that installers, consultants, and end-users ask LLMs; this involves highlighting problem-diagnosis scenarios. Incorporate a library of precise “AI snackable” micro-answers. Utilize lists, comparison tables, pros/cons, and step-by-step guides with an explicit structure.
- Building trust signals: AI systems weigh content credibility using external signals. Strengthen these by increasing Google Reviews / Trustpilot / Amazon-style verified feedback, media coverage, public relations, third-party blogs, comparison sites and independent product testers.

### 3\. Credibility of AI answers.

A battle for trust is emerging in a highly fragmented LLM landscape. With multiple competing models, credibility hinges on the authority of the sources it can credibly reference. This has significant implications in terms of “who” is the authority. Across industries, LLMs draw on different knowledge bases and signal trust as authoritative sources.

In pharma, high-end journals and medical guidelines emerge as the authority. Christof B. Wyss, global medical director, specialty care at GSK, told us: “We know what a high-quality publication looks like for human readers. Now we need to understand what a good publication looks like for the API and the LLM. What are the basics to ensure? What metadata is missing? And what potential Q&A documents should we produce?” GSK has engaged with OpenEvidence to understand what pharma companies could collectively do to improve the quality and reliability of information surfaced by AI systems. The objective was not to influence output but learn how publication standards must evolve in a compliant manner.

A pilot study conducted by Digitas UK, a subsidiary of Publicis Groupe, examined B2B fintech payment solutions in the UK and U.S. markets. The findings revealed that more than 80% of the sources leveraged by LLMs originated directly from the brands themselves, such as Stripe, Adyen, PayPal, and Visa. Caitriona Gallagher, strategy partner at Digitas, told us: “This makes sense because these brands have significant amounts of content on their sites to help support B2B buying journeys, including product comparison content and sector/audience led content. But more importantly, these brands are also the leaders within their categories in terms of R&D and technological progress.” For a small hospitality business owner looking for a payment solution, such a recommendation from the LLM would be regarded as credible.

For industrial manufacturers, credibility means ensuring that product specifications appear in industry databases, certifications are registered with relevant standards bodies, and case studies include verifiable customer references. These make legitimate technical information discoverable through AI procurement tools without artificial manipulation. Additionally, [nascent research](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/youtube-surpasses-reddit-as-primary-social-source-for-ai-models-adweek-reports-4465710) suggests that sites like Reddit and YouTube may currently have an outsized influence on answers provided by LLMs. Historically, IMI had not viewed YouTube as a serious channel for communicating product or performance insights. The company moved quickly to refocus on YouTube content, particularly short, authoritative formats such as the CFO explaining quarterly results or R&D leaders outlining innovation priorities. These were far more likely to be surfaced in gen AI outputs versus traditional corporate reports. Companies can create lots of content, but which ones will really help move the needle has to be ascertained.

### 4\. Calibration through generative listening systems.

Generative listening systems monitor how and how often the company’s content appears in AI-synthesized answers across relevant use cases. GSK’s audit of one of its treatment brands for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) in 2025–26 is a case in point. The audit tested 6,000 prompts across nine nodes in the healthcare practitioner’s decision-making process. For a broad prompt (“which pharmacotherapy available in the U.S. is best for managing moderate COPD patients”) GSK’s Brand X (anonymized for confidentiality) ranked first across multiple LLMs. However, for a specific prompt focused on patients with no previous COPD treatment, the brand dropped to fourth place. That is consequential. Brand X is positioned as a best-in-class option for initial maintenance treatment, and pharmacotherapy naïve patients should be its strongest segment. Yet this is exactly where the brand underperforms in AI answers, revealing a clear disconnect between brand positioning and AI visibility.

A reassuring finding, however, was that all the LLMs accurately anchored their answers in the [Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease](https://goldcopd.org/) (GOLD), the leading global clinical guideline. However, GSK found it was consistently citing the 2024 version, even though GOLD updates its recommendations annually. This was a crucial finding—and a significant issue for GSK given that the latest updates included important changes for its portfolio. Further investigation revealed that the GOLD website had altered how its guidelines were published: Instead of opening as embedded PDFs within the webpage, the files downloaded on click, meaning they were no longer machine readable. With thousands of local, regional, and global clinical guidelines governing hundreds of conditions worldwide, even small changes in how authoritative information is structured can propagate outdated or incomplete medical guidance at scale, increasing patient safety risk.

IMI’s generative listening surfaced a more subtle, and more concerning, insight: Users tended to accept a GEO answer as accurate. Emma Fox, head of communications and engagement at IMI, told us, “Customers don’t probe the answers deeply. Through this LLM audit, IMI aims to safeguard brand visibility, strengthen commercial pull-through over time, reduce misinformation risk, and build long-term influence by being early to shape how LLMs understand the category.” IMI identified key imperatives: 1) Ensure that its products showed up in the GEO results; 2) Enhance the quality and framing of the GEO recommendation; and 3) Ensure that its brand influence came from sources external to the brand itself. IMI’s LLM audit helped safeguard its brand visibility, increased commercial pull-through, reduced misinformation risk, and built strong long-term influence by being the first to shape how LLMs understand the category.

### ...

B2B go‑to‑market strategies are entering a new phase. Much of this process now unfolds in a dark funnel, beyond firms’ direct visibility or control. Executives must ask themselves four critical questions: Do we know what our AI-mediated presence looks like today? Do we have the right organizational structure for this? Are we reallocating resources to match where decisions are actually being made? And are we prepared for a legal crisis?

Strategic vision, rather than technical expertise, will separate the winners from the losers in this arena.
