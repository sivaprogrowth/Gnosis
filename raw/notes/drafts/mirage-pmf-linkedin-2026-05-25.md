Revenue growth used to be the PMF signal in SaaS. In AI-native services, it isn't.

The failure pattern: an AI-services company is growing 4x year over year, retention looks healthy, the board deck is glowing. Under the hood, the team is growing linearly with the customer base. The "AI leverage" is a slide. Each new customer is another human added to delivery. Gross margin is flat or quietly declining — but everyone's looking at the top line.

Emergence Capital named the trap: [[mirage-pmf|Mirage PMF]]. The work isn't to scale revenue. It's to scale AI's contribution to delivering that revenue.

> "Firms chasing automation may unlock short-term gains, but those using AI to orchestrate complex systems will unlock entirely new forms of value, creating competitive advantage." — Sangeet Paul Choudary, *Reshuffle*

The diagnostic is honest COGS accounting. If inference spend, model API costs, and human-in-the-loop labor are quietly migrating from COGS to opex to lift gross margin on paper, the firm is in Mirage PMF. Real [[ai-native-services|AINS]] PMF requires gross margin to expand as the business grows, not stay flat.

And the buyer side of this is changing too:

> "Getting the product-market fit is still very important. Listening to customers' feedback is the best way to create future products. Even in a data-driven, AI-powered world, there is still a human at the other end making the buying decision." — Rajesh Jain, *Startup to Proficorn*

The PMF question for any AI-services company:

ARR per FTE. Gross margin trend. A [[north-star-product-metric|north-star product metric]] that actually measures AI doing the work.

If you can't name those, you're growing on revenue, not on leverage. And the market eventually prices the difference.
