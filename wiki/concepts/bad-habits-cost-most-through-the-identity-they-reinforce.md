# Bad habits cost most through the identity they reinforce

The real damage of a bad habit is that it trains you to be a certain kind of person, such as someone who skips. You don't need perfection, but you should plan to get back on track quickly and do something small each day that fits the person you want to be.

> The most expensive part of a bad habit is rarely the action
> itself, but rather the identity it quietly reinforces underneath.
> The issue isn't skipping once. It's that you practiced being
> someone who skips.
>
> Now, that doesn't mean you need to be perfect. Everyone falls off
> from time to time. But it is good to have a plan for getting back
> on track quickly. And try to find a small way to practice being
> the type of person you want to be today.
>
> — James Clear
