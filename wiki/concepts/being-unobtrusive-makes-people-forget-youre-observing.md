# Being unobtrusive makes people forget you're observing

Joan Didion's strength as a reporter came from standing back, listening, and fading into the background so people forgot she was there.

> Observation isn’t just about looking—it’s about disappearing.
> Joan Didion grasped this better than anyone.
>
> — [[shane-parrish|Shane Parrish]], The World According to Joan Didion

> “My only
> advantage as a reporter is that I am so physically small, so
> temperamentally unobtrusive, and so neurotically inarticulate
> that people tend to forget that my presence runs counter to their
> best interests.”
>
> — Joan Didion, Slouching Towards Bethlehem
