# Connect and Dominate

> Humans are driven to connect and dominate.
> — Will Storr, *The Science of Storytelling*

Storr's tightest synthesis of status psychology. Humans pursue two status moves simultaneously, in every social context: **connect** (signal that you belong to the tribe, are trustworthy, will sacrifice for the group) and **dominate** (signal that you are better than other members of the same tribe, deserving of higher rank, worth listening to). Neither alone is sufficient; both pulled too hard at once look hypocritical. The tension between them is the engine of most human social behaviour — and of most B2B buying behaviour.

## Why this is a single drive, not two

Pure connection without domination produces invisibility. Pure domination without connection produces ostracism. The healthy operator runs both at calibrated intensity: connect strongly enough to be inside the tribe, dominate strongly enough to be moving up inside it. This is also why **heroism** is universally compelling — heroes pay a personal cost (connect: I sacrifice for the group) in a way that simultaneously raises their rank (dominate: this is why I deserve higher status). Costly signalling is the connect-and-dominate move played simultaneously on both axes.

The corollary: **villains** are characters whose status-seeking is read as pure domination at the tribe's expense. Their punishment by the story is rapturous to audiences because watching tribal-norm-violators lose status is itself a status-affirming experience for the in-group.

## Why this matters for B2B buying

A B2B purchase is rarely just a procurement decision. It is also a status move *within the buyer's organisation*. Every buyer is simultaneously asking:

- **Will this make me look like a competent, careful, in-the-tribe insider?** (Connect.) Will my CFO, my board, my peers see this purchase as the right kind of choice for someone in my role?
- **Will this make me look smarter, more ahead, more deserving of the next role than my peers who didn't buy it?** (Dominate.)

The vendor who only sells on connect ("Industry leaders trust us") feels safe but boring. The vendor who only sells on dominate ("Get an unfair advantage") feels risky and potentially career-limiting. The vendor who lands both ("This is what the most credible operators in your category are quietly doing first") wins.

For [[progrowth]] selling to founders or fractional CMOs, the connect-and-dominate frame looks like:

- **Connect:** name-drop adjacent operators who are doing this; cite credible third-party sources; show you understand the buyer's category and its conventions.
- **Dominate:** explicitly position this as something the buyer's peers haven't yet figured out, and the buyer is being shown ahead of the curve.

## Why this matters for content and brand voice

The "thought leader" position is a connect-and-dominate move at the brand level. Connect: *I am of your tribe; I read what you read, attend what you attend, talk the way you talk.* Dominate: *and I see one specific thing inside our tribe that the rest of you haven't seen yet.* The brand whose content lands consistently on both axes accumulates status faster than competitors who only run one.

The failure mode of most B2B content is pure connect — recycled best practices, conference recaps, "10 trends" lists — which signals belonging but no rank. The opposite failure (pure dominate — contrarianism for its own sake) signals rank but no belonging, which the tribe will eventually expel.

## Humiliation as the inverse

If connect-and-dominate is the constructive drive, **humiliation** — the public removal of any ability to claim status — is its inverse. Storr cites it as "an annihilation of the self" and the underlying mechanism behind disproportionate violence (spree shootings, honour killings, vendettas). It is also why category-shaming content (e.g., dunking on a competitor) is risky: the spectacle of humiliation is gripping, but the audience attaches the violation to the brand doing the humiliating as often as to the target.
