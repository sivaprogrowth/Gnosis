# Equilibrium never lasts; the endless adjustments are life

Systems constantly move toward balance but rarely stay there, and we wrongly expect to reach a permanent steady state once some event happens. The trick is to seek balance where needed but know when to embrace the imbalance that drives progress.

> Equilibrium is a  double-edged sword, both stability and
> stagnation. In our lives, we often act like we can reach an
> equilibrium: once we get into a relationship, we’ll be happy;
> once we move, we’ll be productive; once X thing happens, we’ll be
> in Y state. But things are always in flux. We don’t reach a
> certain steady state and then stay there forever. The endless
> adjustments are our lives. The trick is to find the right
> balance, to strive for equilibrium where it’s needed, but to also
> know when to break free, to embrace the dis-equilibrium that
> drives progress.
>
> — [[shane-parrish|Shane Parrish]], The Great Mental Models v3: Systems and Mathematics
