# Everyone writes a personal narrative constantly revised

Each person carries a life story that they keep revising, and its meaning shifts with age. All children create, so the real question is why so many people stop.

> "Everyone writes in a way; that is, each person has a "story," a
> personal narrative which is constantly being replayed, revised,
> taken apart, and put together again. The significant points in
> this narrative change as a person ages—what may have been tragedy
> at twenty is seen as comedy or nostalgia at forty. All children
> write. (And paint, and sing.) I suppose the real question is why
> do so many people give it up?"
>
> — Margaret Atwood, Women at Work: Interviews from the Paris Review
