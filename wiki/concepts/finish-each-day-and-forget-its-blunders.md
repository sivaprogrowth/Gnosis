# Finish each day and forget its blunders

Once a day is done, let go of its mistakes and begin tomorrow fresh and serenely. Each new day is too valuable to waste dwelling on the past.

> Finish every day and be done with it. For manners and for wise
> living it is a vice to remember. You have done what you could;
> some blunders and absurdities no doubt crept in; forget them as
> soon as you can. Tomorrow is a new day; you shall begin it well
> and serenely, and with too high a spirit to be cumbered with your
> old nonsense. This day for all that is good and fair. It is too
> dear, with its hopes and invitations, to waste a moment on the
> rotten yesterdays.
>
> — Ralph Waldo Emerson, Letter to his daughter Ellen, reprinted in The Letters of Ralph Waldo Emerson
