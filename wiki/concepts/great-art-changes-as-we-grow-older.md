# Great art changes as we grow older

Real works of art seem different each time we return to them, because we bring more life experience. Recording your impressions of a great work over the years would amount to writing your autobiography.

> There is one peculiarity which real works of art possess in
> common. At each fresh reading one notices some change in them, as
> if the sap of life ran in their leaves, and with skies and plants
> they had the power to alter their shape and colour from season to
> season. To write down one’s impressions of Hamlet as one reads it
> year after year, would be virtually to record one’s own
> autobiography, for as we know more of life, so Shakespeare
> comments upon what we know.
>
> — Virginia Woolf, Genius and Ink: Virginia Woolf on How to Read
