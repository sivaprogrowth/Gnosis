# Having no backup plan forces you to succeed

Schwab bet everything on a failing tire shop with no experience, and total commitment forced him to figure it out. Half-measures guarantee half-results.

> At 34, Les sold his house, borrowed against
> his life insurance, and scraped together \$11,000 to buy a failing
> tire shop with no running water. He’d never changed a tire. His
> competitors had decades of experience. But Les had something they
> didn’t: no backup plan. That total commitment forced him to
> figure it out. One year later, he’d quintupled revenue.
> Half-measures guarantee half-results.
>
> — [[shane-parrish|Shane Parrish]], The Knowledge Project [Outliers]
