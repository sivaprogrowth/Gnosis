# Improvement stops once a skill becomes automatic

Learning a skill moves through attention, coordination, and finally automatic performance. Once a skill is automatic, improvement slows and eventually stops.

> When we learn to do anything new—how to drive, for example—we go
> through three stages. The first stage demands a lot of attention
> as we try out the controls, learn the rules of driving, and so
> on. In the second stage we begin to coordinate our knowledge,
> linking movements together and more fluidly combining our actions
> with our knowledge of the car, the situation, and the rules. In
> the third stage we drive the car with barely a thought. It’s
> automatic. And with that our improvement at driving slows
> dramatically, eventually stopping completely.
>
> — Geoff Colvin
