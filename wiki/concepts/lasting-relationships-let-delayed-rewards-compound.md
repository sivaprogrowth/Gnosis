# Lasting relationships let delayed rewards compound

Life's greatest rewards are delayed and usually involve other people, so being pleasant and trustworthy keeps relationships intact long enough for results to compound. Many relationships fall apart before the rewards arrive.

> The greatest rewards in life are often delayed. The financial
> benefits of work and investing. The emotional benefits of
> marriage and friendship. The psychological benefits of creating
> something that matters. Meaningful outcomes take a long time to
> grow and compound.
>
> Furthermore, most pursuits involve other people: coworkers,
> spouses, children, peers. If you want your results to continue to
> grow, then you need relationships that last.
>
> One of the essential ingredients for success is being pleasant
> and trustworthy because it allows whatever you are building—a
> business, a relationship, a project—to continue. Many
> relationships disintegrate before the rewards accumulate.
>
> — James Clear
