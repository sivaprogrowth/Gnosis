# Leaders should take the blame, then fix the problem

By owning responsibility for failures as the leader, you end the blame game and redirect the team toward fixing and learning. Blaming others is unproductive and unkind, and freedom to fail is needed for people to take appropriate risks.

> “'I know there's been a lot of talk about what went wrong last
> week. I know everyone's been at least somewhat focused on who's
> to blame. I want you to know that I've searched long and hard and
> finally discovered who's responsible.' I paused. 'I am.' One
> could see varying looks of surprise and relief around the table.
> I continued, 'I'm the chairman and it's my fault when things go
> wrong. Now, having gotten that out of the way, let's try to
> figure out how to fix this and learn from what happened, so we
> don't repeat it.'
>
> It's tempting to criticize others, especially when someone else
> actually did do something wrong. It's also unproductive and
> unkind. If someone repeatedly makes mistakes, he or she is in the
> wrong job. But firing someone won't solve an immediate problem -
> and sometimes making mistakes is a necessary adjunct to trying
> new things: without the freedom to fail, many people won't take
> an appropriate risk.”
>
> — Strauss Zelnick
