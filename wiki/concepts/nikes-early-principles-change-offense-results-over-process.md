# Nike's early principles: change, offense, results over process

An early Nike memo set out principles of constant change, staying on offense, valuing results over perfect process, holding people to promises, finishing the job, and avoiding bureaucracy and overcommitment. It held that doing the right things makes money nearly automatically.

> 1. Our business is change.
>
> 2. We're on offense. All the time.
>
> 3. Perfect results count — not a perfect process. Break the
> rules: fight the law.
>
> 4. This is as much about battle as about business.
>
> 5. Assume nothing. Make sure people keep their promises. Push
> yourselves push others. Stretch the possible.
>
> 6. Live off the land.
>
> 7. Your job isn't done until the job is done.
>
> 8. Dangers:
>
> - Bureaucracy
>
> - Personal ambition
>
> - Energy takers vs. energy givers
>
> - Knowing our weaknesses
>
> - Don't get too many things on the platter
>
> 9. It won't be pretty.
>
> 10. If we do the right things we'll make money damn near
> automatic.
>
> — Rob Strasser, Principles – For Collective Understanding, Focus and Orchestration (Nike internal memo)
