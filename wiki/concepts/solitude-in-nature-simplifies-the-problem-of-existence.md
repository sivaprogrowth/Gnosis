# Solitude in nature simplifies the problem of existence

Thoreau describes walking into the stillness of the woods as opening a window that lets him see out and around himself. There he feels accompanied by an invisible, serene, encouraging presence.

> I come out to these solitudes, where the problem of existence is
> simplified. I get away a mile or two from the town into the
> stillness and solitude of nature, with rocks, trees, weeds, snow
> about me. I enter some glade in the woods, perchance, where a few
> weeds and dry leaves alone lift themselves above the surface of
> the snow, and it is as if I had come to an open window. I see out
> and around myself.
>
> ...
>
> This is what I go out to seek. It is as if I always met in those
> places some grand, serene, immortal, infinitely
> encouraging—though invisible—companion, and walked with him.
>
> — Henry David Thoreau, In Wildness is the Preservation of the World
