# The second idea is always the important one

Your first idea mainly serves to get you started. After finding its flaws and struggling through denial and regret, you arrive at a better second idea that matters.

> Over the years, I've learned that the first idea you have is
> irrelevant. It's just a catalyst for you to get started. Then you
> figure out what's wrong with it and you go through phases of
> denial, panic, regret. And then you finally have a better idea
> and the second idea is always the important one.
>
> — Arthur van Hoff, Founders at Work
