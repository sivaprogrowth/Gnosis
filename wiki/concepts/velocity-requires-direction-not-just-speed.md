# Velocity requires direction, not just speed

Progress is speed in the right direction; moving fast in circles goes nowhere. You can increase velocity by adding force or by removing friction that doesn't serve your goal.

> Velocity isn’t just about raw speed. Direction matters as much. A
> car moving at high speed in circles goes nowhere, while a slow,
> steady walk in a straight line can cross continents.
>
> Velocity is progress. Sometimes progress comes from adding more
> force, and sometimes, progress comes from removing friction. Once
> you have a destination, you can improve your velocity by working
> harder and eliminating things that aren’t contributing toward
> reaching that goal.
>
> — [[shane-parrish|Shane Parrish]], The Great Mental Models v2: Physics, Chemistry, and Biology
