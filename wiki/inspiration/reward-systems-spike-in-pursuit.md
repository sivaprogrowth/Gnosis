# "Our reward systems spike not when we achieve what we're after but when we're in pursuit of it."

> When we push ourselves towards a tough yet meaningful goal, we thrive. Our reward systems spike not when we achieve what we're after but when we're in pursuit of it. It's the pursuit that makes a life and the pursuit that makes a plot.

— Will Storr, *The Science of Storytelling*, Kindle loc. 2298

## Why it's filed

The neuroscience underneath every long-horizon goal system — including Harada Method, 12 Week Year, and any honest goal-pursuit framework. The mistake most people make is to treat goals as deferred reward (*"once I get there, I'll be happy"*). The empirical reality is the inverse: dopamine spikes during the pursuit, and post-achievement is mostly anticlimax. The implication is severe — **the design of the pursuit is the design of the life**, not the destination.

Useful for:

- **Goal design.** Pick goals whose pursuit is intrinsically rewarding, not whose attainment is. The Harada-Method "tough but meaningful" bar isn't sentimental; it's biomechanical.
- **Founder energy.** Companies whose founders set goals only at the exit level eventually burn out. Companies whose founders set goals at the pursuit level (this quarter's hard problem, this month's specific bet) keep firing the reward circuitry.
- **Client narratives.** The B2B engagements that compound are the ones where the *operator's pursuit* gets better, not just the dashboard at the end.
