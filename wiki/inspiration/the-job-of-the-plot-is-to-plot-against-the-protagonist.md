# "The job of the plot is to plot against the protagonist."

> The job of the plot is to plot against the protagonist. Its causes and effects always revolve around some sort of story event — an episode that brings the character into a new psychological realm. Once they're in this hostile and alien place, their flawed theory of control is tested and retested, often to breaking point and beyond.

— Will Storr, *The Science of Storytelling*, Kindle loc. 2313

## Why it's filed

The cleanest one-line statement of the entire book's argument. Plot is not a sequence of events the writer arranges; plot is **active opposition** to the protagonist's [[theory-of-control]]. Anything else is incident, not story.

Equally portable outside fiction:

- **In customer engagement:** the "plot" of a worthwhile B2B engagement is whatever forces the client's [[theory-of-control]] to update. If the engagement isn't doing that, it isn't a story — it's an activity.
- **In a founder's own life:** the events that produce real change are the ones that plot against the operator's current self-model. Nothing else compounds.
