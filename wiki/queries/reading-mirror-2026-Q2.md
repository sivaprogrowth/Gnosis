## Surprising overlap

Three emergent cross-book themes worth flagging:

1. **The discipline of solitary work over time** — Murphy on performance-under-pressure routines, Rubin on the artist's daily practice, Dillard on the work-as-feral-beast requiring daily visits. Three authors from three traditions converging on the same operating principle: *show up daily, the work itself is the reward, treat your mood as noise.* This is the same cluster the §4.7 weekly synthesis caught last week with the Dillard mosquito metaphor. Across the full quarter it's the dominant emergent theme. **Candidate for a `wiki/concepts/` page** — provisionally `daily-discipline-of-solitary-work` or `the-pursuit-makes-the-life` (extending [[reward-systems-spike-in-pursuit]]).
2. **Belief-engineering as the founder's craft** — Pitch on persuasion architecture, Book of Elon on reality-distortion field, Wright Brothers on selling against expert opinion. The cross-book pattern: *the founder's primary skill is making other people believe in the work before the evidence is in.* Adjacent to `[[connect-and-dominate]]` and `[[wants-vs-needs]]` but distinct enough to warrant its own concept — provisionally `belief-engineering` or `pre-evidence-conviction`.
3. **Subtraction as intelligence** — Dillard's "life of the spirit requires less and less," Manu Joseph's "austerity is a form of intelligence," and last week's synthesis brief already caught the Vedic *aparigraha* overlap. At quarter-scale this is now the *second* surface of the same theme — the case for promoting `wiki/concepts/aparigraha` (or `austerity-as-intelligence`) gets stronger.
