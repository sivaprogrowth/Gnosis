# Resurface — KOG bid bond outreach campaign (2026-05-25)

**Work context:** `[[kog-bid-bonds-outreach]]` — pain-points research, JTBD analysis, TPSA ads, and the 2-stage email sequence are built. Ready to send to bonded contractors. Pending: final send list confirmation + live A/B subject-line check.

**Why these seven:** the campaign is operational, not strategic — sending happens this week. The hooks below are tactical guidance to apply *before* the first send and during the live A/B, not to reconsider the strategy. Source: `mcp__readwise__readwise_search_highlights` vector-similarity pass, re-ranked for applicability per `CLAUDE.md` §4.8.

> "Remember, the point of the subject line is to get the recipient to open the email. In this case, the subject is simple: [Current employer/undergraduate school]. Wouldn't you open an email with that subject?" — Mark Roberge, *The Sales Acceleration Formula*
> **Why this matters for the A/B test:** Use this format as one of your subject-line variants — `[Bonded contractor in <state>]` or `[<Specific surety carrier they use>]` — a recognition-trigger subject. Almost always outperforms benefit-stated subjects on first-touch.

> "These emails should look as if they are a single email that came from a salesperson. They should be text-based, not fancy HTML. State simply and clearly why you are reaching out. Make the email easy to read and respond to on a mobile phone." — Aaron Ross, *Predictable Revenue*
> **Why this matters for the send:** Confirm the 2-stage sequence is plain-text from a human-sounding sender, not from `marketing@progrowth.services` with a templated header image. Contractors check email on phones; HTML-heavy emails get filed as junk reflexively.

> "The goal of every mass email should be to establish and close a prospect on a next step. That next step should be either one of two things—but NOT both: Who is the best point of contact for …? (to get a referral); …" — Aaron Ross, *Predictable Revenue*
> **Why this matters for the sequence:** Each stage should ask for *one* thing — either the meeting, OR the referral to the right person, but not both in the same email. The 2-stage design is the right structural choice; check each stage has exactly one CTA.

> "Effective ABM messaging requires an understanding of customer pain points. That insight comes from the sales reps who are having conversations with prospects every day." — Sangram Vajre, *ABM for Dummies*
> **Why this matters for the message:** Your pain-points research is the load-bearing input — but the message should make the prospect *recognize* the pain, not state it. "Most bonded contractors we talk to are running quotes through three carriers manually" works; "your quoting process is inefficient" doesn't.

> "Dominate a niche, then once you own it, do the same with another and then another. But never do so all at once. Doing so dilutes your message and your marketing power." — Allan Dib, *The 1-Page Marketing Plan*
> **Why this matters for the campaign:** Bonded-contractors-only positioning is the correct tactical bet. **Resist** the temptation to broaden to "construction services + bonded contractors" if early reply rates are slow. The niche is the asset.

> "in a B2B approach, you must convince someone to spend someone else's money. (And usually more than one person.)" — T2d3
> **Why this matters for the sequence:** Bonded contractors typically have a CFO + owner + insurance-agent triangle on the buying side. The 2-stage sequence should address at least two roles — the operator (whose pain it solves) and the financial owner (whose money it costs). Single-persona messages get socialized and stalled. Connects to [[connect-and-dominate]] — different buyers in the same org weigh status signals differently.

> "Cold approaches are a different beast and I haven't found a magic bullet or reliable cold email format. But remember, the point of cold calls is to stop having them. If you're banging your head against the wall trying to get people to answer your cold emails, then you're probably taking the hard road." — Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*
> **Why this matters for the campaign:** Honest expectation-setting before the first send: cold-contractor reply rates of 2–3% are decent on the first wave. The *real* goal is the few who reply, plus the larger group who now know ProGrowth exists for when their situation cracks (their [[theory-of-control]] hits ignition point). Don't over-tune on aggregate open rate.
