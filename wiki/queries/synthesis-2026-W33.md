## Collisions and tensions

**Direct contradiction — within Dyson himself: experience is a hindrance / experience is everything.** He devalues accumulated knowledge as a hiring and thinking criterion:

> "At Dyson, we don't particularly value experience. Experience tells you what you ought to do and what you'd do best to avoid. It tells you how things should be"
> — James Dyson, *Invention*

Two pages earlier in the same argument, the thing he credits for his method is *precisely* accumulated experience — thousands of prototypes, and Honda's "addiction to the continuous improvement of products":

> "Research is about conducting experiments, accepting and even enjoying failures, but going on and on, following a theory garnered from observing the science. Invention is often more about endurance and patient observation than brainwaves."
> — James Dyson, *Invention*

The disagreement is real, not merely verbal: a theory "garnered from observing" is an experience-derived prior, and continuous improvement is compounding experience. What he actually rejects is *inherited* experience (other people's conventions); what he prizes is *self-generated* experience (his own failure log). The book conflates the two under one word, and the highlights caught it.

**Cross-domain rhyme — failure-welcoming and the older detachment-from-fruits tradition.** "It is more about failure than ultimate success... I even thought of calling this book James Dyson: Failure" is, structurally, a control-locus claim: attach identity to the process you control (prototype n+1) and not to the outcome you don't (the breakthrough). That is the karma-yoga move — right to the action, never to the fruits — arrived at from a vacuum-cleaner workshop rather than a scripture. It also rhymes with [[concepts/anti-mimetic-life]]: "the bloody-mindedness not to follow convention, to challenge experts and to ignore Doubting Thomases" is a description of removing external models from one's desire-system, which is the same manoeuvre [[concepts/mimetic-desire]] and [[concepts/internal-vs-external-mediation]] describe from the other end. Dyson's rejection of "virtue signallers" in favour of "doers" is, read charitably, an anti-mimetic ethic: virtue signalling is desire mediated externally; making a thing is desire mediated internally.

**Cross-domain rhyme — "experience can be a hindrance" against the AI-discovery stack.** This week's ingested pages describe systems that are, definitionally, experience machines: they rank and recommend on the accumulated past. [[concepts/e-e-a-t]] elevates *Experience* to a first-class trust signal; [[concepts/big-brand-bias]] and [[concepts/brand-age]] document that AI answer engines systematically over-weight the established. Dyson's claim is the exact inverse: in the realm of the genuinely new, "experience can be a hindrance." The rhyme is uncomfortable and useful — the discovery layer now being built is structurally conservative in precisely the way Dyson says kills invention. A Dyson launching today would be invisible to it by construction, not by accident.

**Cross-domain rhyme — collapsing roles, then and now.** "I dared to dream that I could be engineer, designer and manufacturer at one and the same time" is the 1970s analogue of this week's ingested [[rise-of-million-dollar-one-person-companies]] and [[ai-native-companies-tiny-staffs-fewer-bosses]]. Same claim — one person can hold the whole value chain — different enabling technology (personal bloody-mindedness vs. tooling). Worth noting the asymmetry: Dyson's version required 5,127 prototypes of *his own* labour; the AI-native version outsources the labour but not the taste.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/long-horizon-r-and-d]], strengthened.** The page's claim that breakthrough technology requires tolerating long unrewarded intervals gets a first-person mechanism here: "going on and on, following a theory garnered from observing the science," plus the sporting corollary "when everyone else feels exhausted, that is the opportunity to accelerate." Dyson supplies the *motivational* substrate the page currently treats as a capital/structure question.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/manual-then-automate]], strengthened.** "prepared to slog through prototype after prototype searching for the breakthrough" is the physical-goods case of the same principle: do the expensive manual iteration until the shape of the solution is known.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/sacred-flaw-approach]], strengthened obliquely.** The father-loss cluster — "Dad died in 1956. I was nine" alongside the Ironside statistic — is Dyson doing narrative self-diagnosis: locating a wound as the engine of the drive. That is the sacred-flaw structure applied to a memoir rather than a character. He also hedges it honestly ("It would be wrong to say the loss of a father is some sort of macabre ticket to success"), which is the correct epistemic posture the page should borrow.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/procrastination]] and the ingested [[how-to-stop-procrastinating]], qualified.** Dyson's account offers no technique, no [[concepts/mcii-mental-contrasting-implementation-intentions]]-style implementation intention, no environment design. His entire answer is *obsession supplied by the objective*: "you become engaged, hooked and even one-track-minded." This doesn't refute the procrastination literature so much as suggest the literature is solving for the case where the objective isn't gripping enough — a scope limit worth writing onto the page.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/e-e-a-t]], undermined.** See above. The Experience pillar's implicit claim (accumulated domain experience is a reliable proxy for quality of output) is directly contradicted by "If you want to pioneer and invent new technology you need to step into the unknown and, in that realm, experience can be a hindrance." Worth adding as a named counter-case: E-E-A-T is a *trust* heuristic, and trust heuristics are optimised against the median, not the frontier.

## What this week contradicted

- [[concepts/e-e-a-t]] — the Experience pillar. Dyson: "experience can be a hindrance" in the realm of the new. The page should be scoped to trust-signalling for established categories, not treated as a general quality proxy.
- [[concepts/repeatability]] — the page assumes codified experience ("how things should be done") is the asset. Dyson explicitly inverts this: "we are much more interested in how things shouldn't be done." Repeatability is a scaling virtue and an invention vice; the page currently doesn't mark that boundary.
- [[concepts/procrastination]] — weakened as a general theory of non-action. Dyson's model is that a sufficiently gripping objective makes the problem dissolve ("you become engaged, hooked and even one-track-minded"), which relocates the intervention from the person's habits to the objective's quality.
- [[concepts/pretty-good-at-scale]] — qualified by "everything about it has to have a purpose. There has to be a reason." Dyson's whole case is that purposeful design beats adequate-at-volume; the page should carry the counter-position rather than assume the trade is settled.
