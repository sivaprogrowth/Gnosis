## Collisions and tensions

**Direct contradiction — the customer is both oracle and idiot.** Within a single paragraph Dyson holds two incompatible positions:

> "He talked of the need to listen to your customers, aiming to improve products wherever necessary"

against

> "This is not to say we at Dyson ask our customers what they want and then build it. That type of focus-group-led designing may work in the very short term, but not for long."

And the same tension runs across the cluster: selling is the final criterion of worth ("of little use unless they can be translated… into products that… can sell"), yet the market's *stated* judgement is worthless in advance ("The hugely successful Sony Walkman was dismissed"). The unresolved question is epistemic, not rhetorical: **the market is the only valid verdict but cannot be polled before the fact.** Dyson never says how you tell "ahead of its time" from "wrong," and the highlights don't either. That's the load-bearing gap in the whole chapter.

**Direct contradiction — anti-expertise smuggles in its own orthodoxy.** "He loathed smugness and experts" and hired "curious, unsullied and open minds… *as long as they didn't sport beards or smoke pipes*." The stated principle is openness; the operative filter is a grooming prejudice. And it collides with the book's central demand: you must "be more than a designer or engineer" and control "the technology, engineering and design of a product and making and marketing it, too" — which is a demand for *more* expertise than anyone reasonably has, concentrated in one person. The doctrine is not anti-expertise; it is anti-*credentialled* expertise, plus a bet that a single unsullied mind can out-learn a committee of specialists. Worth flagging as an unexamined premise rather than a proven one.

**Cross-domain rhyme — "things that might seem strange, and not things you know will sell" is a manufacturing statement of anti-mimetic desire.** Compare against [[concepts/mimetic-desire]] and [[concepts/internal-vs-external-mediation]]: focus-group-led design is desire borrowed from a model — the customer's existing wants, which are themselves borrowed. Dyson's rejection of it is precisely Girard's *internal* mediation refused in favour of a self-generated object. [[concepts/anti-mimetic-life]] usually reads as a personal-discipline concept; this week supplies its industrial form — an *organisational* anti-mimetic posture with a P&L attached. That the strange-product bet is financed by "debt, risk and potential penury" is the price of non-imitation, stated plainly.

**Cross-domain rhyme — the Deirdre passage against karma yoga, with one telling deviation.** "It is something you get caught up in. You have to do it, believe in it and trust in a successful outcome." The first two clauses are almost verbatim *svadharma*: the action is compulsory because it is *yours*, not because the arithmetic works — the Gita's "your right is to the action alone." But then Dyson adds the clause the Gita explicitly forbids: *trust in a successful outcome*. Where the older tradition detaches from the fruit in order to act without paralysis, Dyson attaches to the fruit *as the mechanism* for enduring "a world of debt, risk and potential penury." Same problem — how to act under total uncertainty — opposite solutions: renounce the outcome, or believe in it hard enough. The Western entrepreneurial version needs the optimism because it lacks the metaphysics.

**Cross-domain rhyme — "news is fast while making" is [[concepts/makers-schedule]] at industrial scale.** Graham's argument was about a programmer's afternoon; Dyson's is about a national economy's attention. Same structure: the fast-cadence medium (meetings, news cycles) systematically undervalues the slow-cadence activity because the slow one produces nothing legible in a news-cycle interval. The rhyme suggests [[concepts/makers-schedule]] is a special case of a more general attention-bandwidth mismatch, not just a calendar problem.

**Wiki-impact — strengthens [[concepts/founder-led-sales]].** Claim: early selling works because the founder can transmit conviction and technical depth a hired rep cannot. Highlight: "He sold new and intelligent things to people not through smooth and well-rehearsed sales patter but because… he enjoyed talking to prospective customers about engineering, for which his enthusiasm was infectious." Direction: **strengthens**, and adds a mechanism the page doesn't have — enthusiasm is credible precisely *because* it is unrehearsed, so professionalising the pitch destroys the asset.

**Wiki-impact — strengthens [[concepts/long-horizon-r-and-d]].** "Licensing… putting them at the mercy of other companies that may or may not have a long-term commitment to a particular new idea" names a specific failure mode: long-horizon R&D dies not from technical failure but from ownership changing hands mid-horizon. Direction: **strengthens**, with a governance argument the page can absorb.

**Wiki-impact — undermines [[concepts/core-vs-context-framework]] and [[concepts/build-vs-buy]].** Both pages rest on the claim that you concentrate on the differentiating layer and outsource the rest. Dyson's whole position is that for a genuinely new product *there is no context layer* — manufacturing, marketing and engineering are all core, because each is a place where the idea can be diluted by someone with no commitment to it. Direction: **undermines**, or at minimum bounds the frameworks' applicability to incremental rather than radical products.

**Wiki-impact — qualifies [[concepts/mirage-pmf]] and [[concepts/levels-of-pmf]].** The Walkman being "dismissed" pre-launch is the mirror image of mirage PMF: a *mirage of no-PMF*, where every available early signal reads negative on something that later works. The PMF pages handle false positives well and false negatives not at all. Direction: **extends by inversion** — the false-negative case deserves a section.

**Tension with this week's ingested pages.** Nothing in the AI/B2B ingest touches Dyson thematically, but the postures collide: [[future-of-b2b-sales-with-ai-mckinsey-2026]] and [[b2b-growth-economics-2026-pulse-survey]] rest on rewiring the go-to-market around aggregated buyer-signal data — the systematised, instrumented descendant of the focus group. Dyson's verdict on that method — "may work in the very short term, but not for long" — is a live objection to the entire genre, and to [[concepts/hyper-personalisation]] and [[concepts/next-best-experience]], both of which optimise against *revealed existing* preference. Neither side is obviously right; the honest framing is that signal-driven GTM is a machine for exploiting demand that already exists, and has no theory of the product nobody has asked for yet.

## What this week contradicted

- [[concepts/core-vs-context-framework]] — the claim that non-differentiating layers should be outsourced. Dyson's position is that for radical products every layer is differentiating, because every handoff is a place the idea gets diluted by a party with no commitment to it.
- [[concepts/build-vs-buy]] — the licensing/partner branch is reframed not as a capital-efficiency choice but as a loss of control with a predictable failure mode ("at the mercy of other companies").
- [[concepts/hyper-personalisation]] and [[concepts/next-best-experience]] — both optimise against revealed existing preference; "focus-group-led designing may work in the very short term, but not for long" is a direct challenge to that as a durable strategy, though Dyson offers no alternative measurement, only conviction.
- [[concepts/jobs-to-be-done]] — *partially*. JTBD's warning that customers describe solutions badly is strengthened; any reading of JTBD as a research procedure you run *on* customers is weakened. Worth splitting the page's claim into these two halves.
- [[concepts/b2b-pulse-survey]] — survey-derived buyer preference as a planning input. Dyson treats stated preference as a lagging indicator of the past; the page treats it as forward guidance. Unresolved, and probably resolvable only by scoping: incremental vs. genuinely new.
