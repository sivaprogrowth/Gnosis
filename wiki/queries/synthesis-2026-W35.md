## Collisions and tensions

**Direct contradiction — expertise kills ideas / expertise *is* the edge.** Within the same book, twenty pages apart:

> "they have all the answers and, because of this trait, they can kill new ideas. But when you are trying to break new ground, you have no interest in getting stuck in engineering conventions or intellectual mud."

against

> "His approach to winning investment, he wrote, was to become a leading authority on a 'clearly defined and narrow area of knowledge'. Following the Zulu Principle, this can be achieved remarkably quickly."

Dyson is cautious of experts and simultaneously endorses manufacturing yourself into one, fast. The reconciliation he leaves implicit — and doesn't defend — is that *acquired-by-doing* narrow expertise is generative while *inherited-by-credential* expertise is sclerotic. That's a load-bearing claim he never argues for. Worth pressing on.

**Direct contradiction — ignore market research / the market is why you failed.** "Don't worry about market research... Follow your own star" and the transparent-bin story ("we ignored the market research") sit against the Ballbarrow post-mortem: "The product was good, but the commercial proposition was a bad i[dea]" — a failure diagnosed entirely in market terms (wrong price, wrong comparison set, wrong distribution, wrong export economics). And Ross in Australia succeeds by selling *against* the market's shape: "It was a slightly odd thing to be selling an upright cleaner in Australia in what is a cylinder − or 'barrel' as they call it – market." Dyson's actual position seems to be: ignore *stated preference* about products that don't exist yet, but never ignore *structural* market facts. He doesn't separate the two, and the book's own moral ("follow your own star") flattens them.

**Cross-domain rhyme — "one thing at a time" is the eval discipline.** "changing only one thing at a time and to see what difference that one change made" is, precisely, the ablation logic underneath [[concepts/ai-evals]]. The rhyme is that both are counter-cultural in the same way: Dyson is arguing against the spark-of-genius myth, and eval practice argues against the demo-and-vibes myth. Two industries, four decades apart, same enemy — the belief that a brainwave can be shipped without being isolated and measured.

**Cross-domain rhyme — Dyson's "don't copy the opposition" and Girardian mimesis.** [[concepts/anti-mimetic-life]] holds that desire is borrowed and that freedom comes from refusing the model. Dyson supplies an unusually clean commercial instance: Kirk-Dyson failed *because* "I wasn't following my own star," i.e. because the firm's shape was borrowed. The interesting extension is that Dyson locates the anti-mimetic move not in personal asceticism but in *cap table structure* — you cannot refuse the model if shareholders are the model. That's a mechanism the concept page currently lacks.

**Cross-domain rhyme — believe totally, plan for nothing.** "you have to believe that you'll get there in the end" placed immediately beside "Never for a moment, though, had I thought, I know, one day I'll set up a business making and selling vacuum cleaners and if it takes me fifteen years to make a profit" is a detachment-from-fruits structure: full commitment to the work, no attachment to the timeline or the outcome-as-specified. This is the same shape as [[a-goal-isnt-a-mission]], ingested this week — the mission (obsolete the bag) is directional and unbounded, the goal (a profitable vacuum company) was never held at all. It also rhymes with [[internal-locus-of-control-economist-2026]]: agency located in effort rather than in results.

**Cross-domain rhyme — Fuller's obsolescence and the modular-build literature.** "You never change things by fighting the existing reality. To change something, build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete" sits against [[economist-why-big-projects-go-wrong-2023]] and [[how-big-things-get-done-review-wsj-2023]] from this week's ingest. Both argue big things succeed through slow modular iteration, not heroic novelty. Dyson is the reconciliation case: the *ambition* was Fuller-scale obsolescence, the *method* was 5,000-odd prototypes with one variable changed. Vision maximal, increment minimal.

**Cross-domain rhyme — the Zulu Principle and vertical AI.** "become a leading authority on a 'clearly defined and narrow area of knowledge'" is the thesis of [[harvey-builds-ai-around-domain-expertise]], ingested this week, almost word for word — narrow domain depth as the defensible position when general capability is commoditised. Slater was describing stock-picking in the 1990s; Harvey is describing legal AI in 2026.

**Wiki-impact — [[companies/zoho-corporation]], strengthened and then complicated.** The page's claim that staying private and refusing outside capital is what enables decade-horizon R&D is directly corroborated: "Without shareholders to hold the company back, we are free to take long-term and radical decisions." But [[zoho-vembu-divorce-ip-transfer-dispute]], ingested this week, is the counter-evidence on the same structure — concentrated personal ownership makes the firm's fate contingent on one individual's personal life. Dyson's "all decisions are your own... This certainly sharpens the mind" reads differently next to it. The concept isn't wrong; it's under-specified about failure modes.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/b2b-pulse-survey]] and [[concepts/b2b-buyer-archetypes]], undermined at the edges.** Both rest on stated-preference instruments. "I was also told that no one would want to see dust sucked up by a cleaner inside a transparent container. Simple market research confirmed this" is a clean falsification for *novel categories* — respondents cannot report preferences over experiences they've never had. Direction: qualifying, not fatal. The scope condition ("survey research degrades as category novelty rises") should be added.

**Wiki-impact — [[concepts/big-brand-bias]], strengthened.** "It was the last of these qualities, in fact, that took us a very long time indeed to get across in shops and retail warehouses. For many years, products were distinguished, looks" — even with a genuinely superior product, the retail channel defaulted to familiarity and appearance. Performance took years to become legible. That is the bias the page describes, measured in years by someone who was on the wrong side of it.
