## Abstract

Menlo Ventures' fourth annual State of Enterprise AI report analyzes the explosive growth of enterprise AI deployment spending, which surged from $33B to $177B in a single year (5.4×). The central argument is that AI agents—not just human-driven usage—are the primary engine of this growth, having overtaken human token consumption and decoupled spend from headcount. The report finds that far from triggering a "SaaSpocalypse," AI is expanding the overall software market, with 55% of incremental AI spend coming from net-new budgets. [[companies/anthropic]] and [[companies/openai]] together now command 86% of enterprise LLM API spend, with [[companies/anthropic]] growing its share from 40% to 55%, while AI-native startups capture 77% of application-layer spend.

## TL;DR

- Enterprise AI deployment spend hit $177B in 2026 (5.4×), driven by AI agents that now account for 80% of all tokens on OpenRouter and have decoupled spend from headcount.
- [[companies/anthropic]] and [[companies/openai]] command 86% of enterprise LLM API spend; capability—not price—is the decisive buying criterion, with buyers consistently trading up to frontier models.
- AI is growing the software market rather than cannibalizing it: 55% of incremental AI spend comes from net-new budgets, and there is limited evidence of enterprises replacing core SaaS systems of record.

## Key claims

- Enterprise AI deployment spending grew from $33B to $176.8B (5.4×) in one year, reaching more than 30% of public cloud revenue; every $1 spent building models generates $1.70 of deployment revenue.
- AI agents now account for 80% of all tokens on OpenRouter (up 37× since February), having overtaken human-driven usage after the launch of Anthropic's Opus 4.5 in November 2025.
- [[companies/anthropic]], [[companies/openai]], and [[companies/google]] capture 69% of all enterprise AI deployment dollars ($122.7B), up from 50% the prior year; [[companies/anthropic]] alone holds 55% of enterprise LLM API spend.
- Actual enterprise AI spend averaged 4.7× prior-year levels vs. a planned 1.9×, with the gap explained by agents running independently, in parallel, and outside business hours—scaling with work rather than headcount.
- 55% of incremental AI spend came from net-new budgets; only 9% ($12.6B) moved from incumbent SaaS to AI-native vendors, representing roughly a 2-point growth headwind for the $600B+ application SaaS market.
- Coding ($34.0B) and enterprise chatbots ($25.0B) account for 76% of the $77.3B AI application market, because they provide environments in which agents can act, complete context to learn from, and verifiable results.
- AI-native providers now capture 77% of application-layer spend and 90% of infrastructure-layer spend; coding is 96% AI-native, while incumbents retain dominance in IT (91%), data science (88%), and HR (68%).
- Open-source models rose to 19% of token share but only 4% of spend; Chinese open-source models (Kimi, [[companies/deepseek]], GLM, Qwen) represent 89% of open-source spend, with Meta's Llama the leading Western model at 7%.

## Key passages

> Enterprise AI deployment spending surged from **$33 billion** to **$177 billion** in just one year. That's over **$140 billion** in new annual spending, more than **four times** the size of the entire market a year ago.

> Reported spending averaged **4.7x** last year's level, against a planned **1.9x**—roughly **2.5x** plan... agents explain the gap. They work independently, run in parallel, and keep going even after people step away, so spending scales with work rather than headcount.

> Far from being a zero-sum game, AI is creating new value across the economy, expanding markets far more than it's displacing existing revenue.

> Mostly new budgets, which funded **55%** of incremental AI spend, compared with **22%** from software budgets... Our study also found limited evidence of enterprises replacing core systems such as [[companies/salesforce]]'s CRM, Workday's HRIS, or Oracle's NetSuite with AI.

> Distribution wins when AI enhances an existing workflow; capability wins when AI redefines it. Incumbents hold the departments where AI has to fit inside a system of record, while AI-natives are winning where agents are rewriting the work itself.

> Open-source now handles about **one in five** tokens but accounts for only about **one in 25** dollars of model spending, driven by a significant delta in price between closed and open-source models.
