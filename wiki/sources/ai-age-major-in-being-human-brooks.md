## Abstract

In this New York Times opinion column, David Brooks argues that the rise of AI should prompt individuals — especially students — to double down on distinctly human capabilities rather than compete with machines on their own terrain. Using an AI-generated artwork that won a Colorado State Fair prize as a launching point, Brooks contends that AI output tends to be bland and lifeless because it lacks the passion, pain, and personal experience that animate genuine human creativity. He identifies six core human skills — distinct personal voice, presentation ability, childlike creativity, unusual worldviews, empathy, and situational awareness — that AI is structurally unlikely to replicate. The broader implication is that AI's greatest gift may be clarifying what it cannot do, thereby revealing the irreducible value of humanistic knowledge and education.

## TL;DR

- AI excels at impersonal, pattern-based thinking, which means humans should invest in exactly the skills AI cannot replicate: creativity, voice, empathy, and situational awareness.
- Brooks recommends students seek out courses — in sciences or humanities — that cultivate these distinctly human capacities rather than ones that train linear, generalized reasoning.
- The deepest form of education remains relational and embodied: great teachers model virtue, passion, and judgment in ways no bot can transmit.

## Key claims

- AI-generated art and prose tends to feel "bland and vague" because it lacks a humanistic core rooted in individual passion and lived experience.
- The most important insight AI offers may be negative: it reveals what machines cannot do and therefore what humans uniquely bring.
- A distinct personal voice — contrasted with the impersonal bureaucratic prose AI produces — is one of the most valuable skills in an AI-saturated economy.
- [[companies/google-deepmind|AI]] text-prediction systems are good at predicting the conventional next word; therefore, being genuinely unpredictable and holding idiosyncratic worldviews becomes more valuable.
- Economist [[Tyler Cowen]] argues that AI favours extroverts over introverts, reversing the dynamic of the previous generation of information technology.
- Developmental psychologist [[Alison Gopnik]] observes that children find a "creative sweet spot between the obvious and the crazy" that current AI systems cannot reach.
- Situational awareness — an intuitive, embodied feel for unique circumstances — flows from experience, historical knowledge, and a reflective life, and is unlikely to be replicated by machines.
- The best teachers transmit humanistic knowledge by modelling passion, moral virtues, and ways of being — a form of knowledge never available from a bot.

## Key passages

> "This is what many of us notice about art or prose generated by A.I. It's often bland and vague. It's missing a humanistic core. It's missing an individual person's passion, pain, longings and a life of deeply felt personal experiences."

> "The most important thing about A.I. may be that it shows us what it can't do, and so reveals who we are and what we have to offer."

> "'The prior generation of information technology favored the introverts, whereas the new A.I. bots are more likely to favor the extroverts,' the George Mason University economist Tyler Cowen writes. 'You will need to be showing off all the time that you are more than one of them.'"

> "'When you interact for a while with a system like GPT-3, you notice that it tends to veer from the banal to the completely nonsensical,' Alison Gopnik, famed for her studies on the minds of children, observes. 'Somehow children find the creative sweet spot between the obvious and the crazy.'"

> "Situational Awareness... flows from experience, historical knowledge, humility in the face of uncertainty, and having led a reflective and interesting life. It is a kind of knowledge held in the body as well as the brain."

> "That's my hope for the age of A.I. — that it forces us to more clearly distinguish the knowledge that is useful information from the humanistic knowledge that leaves people wiser and transformed."
