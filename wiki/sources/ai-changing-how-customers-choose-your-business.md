## Abstract

As AI tools increasingly mediate how customers research, evaluate, and select suppliers, competitive advantage is shifting away from direct customer observation toward managing AI-shaped interactions. Drawing on three disguised small-to-medium business (SMB) case studies — a precision manufacturer (Meridian Mouldings), a heritage wedding venue (Ashcombe Manor), and a B2B SaaS firm (Runtime Harbor) — the article argues that firms must qualify AI-generated inbound demand more rigorously, audit how AI tools describe them in the wild, and move from episodic to continuous customer co-creation. The piece contends that the baseline against which customers evaluate a business now shifts constantly because the AI tools mediating those comparisons are themselves continuously retrained, making periodic strategy cycles structurally inadequate. Notably, all three firms implemented their adaptations manually and cheaply, without bespoke software.

## TL;DR

- AI intermediaries have inserted a new, largely invisible layer between customers and businesses, making raw inbound volume and polished reviews unreliable signals of true demand or sentiment.
- Firms should respond by qualifying AI-generated enquiries more selectively, auditing their own AI-described reputation regularly, and building continuous (not episodic) feedback loops.
- Specific, differentiated public content is now a strategic asset because it is what AI tools draw on when describing a business to prospective customers.

## Key claims

- Around 50% of consumers now use AI for researching products and services, and shopping-related usage is the third most popular application of generative AI (McKinsey).
- AI-assisted procurement lets B2B buyers mass-generate polished RFQs in minutes, flooding specialist suppliers with low-quality demand that looks credible but lacks the context needed for a responsible quote.
- For specialist firms, competing by quoting faster (rather than qualifying better) is structurally self-defeating because their value lies in diagnosing the customer's problem before proposing a solution — a direct application of [[concepts/jobs-to-be-done]].
- Between 10% and 30% of reviews on platforms such as Capterra, G2, and TrustRadius posted after ChatGPT's launch are estimated to be AI-generated, making review pools less reliable as strategic signals.
- AI-generated fake reviews tend to be more fluent and easier to understand but less specific than authentic reviews, meaning polish is an inverse indicator of signal quality.
- How an AI tool describes a business to a prospective customer now carries as much strategic weight as a critical public review, yet most firms have no system for monitoring it.
- The baseline for "good" in any category drifts continuously as AI tools are retrained and competitors update their public content, making annual strategy cycles structurally insufficient.
- Generic public content (e.g. "attentive staff," "stunning setting") gives AI tools nothing distinctive to say; concrete, specific content is what produces differentiated AI-generated descriptions — a practical dimension of [[concepts/generative-engine-optimization]].

## Key passages

> "AI, in other words, can manufacture a race to quote faster rather than better—and faster is the race a specialist least wants to win, because its value-added lies in helping the customer improve their own value through the expertise they bring to the customer's job-to-be-done."

> "Rising inquiry volume no longer automatically signaled rising opportunity, and leadership gained a far more accurate read of real demand."

> "An entire layer of customer opinion-formation about the venue was happening in conversations that the venue could not see."

> "If your public content sounds like every other venue's public content, AI tools will describe you like every other venue. Specific content gives the AI something specific to say."

> "The same product that delighted a customer in March may underwhelm a prospective customer in November. This is not because the product has changed but because the AI that describes it has been retrained, the competitors it is compared against have updated their content, and the implicit standard for 'good' has drifted."

> "Design for this deliberately—define customers as actors of human-AI interaction, weight voices by authenticity rather than polish, and make listening continuous rather than episodic."
