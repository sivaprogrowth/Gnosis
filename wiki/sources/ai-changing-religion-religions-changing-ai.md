## Abstract

This *Economist* article examines the two-way collision between artificial intelligence and organised religion. On one side, AI is already reshaping how believers practise their faith—through chatbots that answer doctrinal questions, AI-assisted sermon writing, and algorithmically driven "religious improvisation" that is spawning novel spiritual movements. On the other, frontier AI models exhibit a measurable secular bias: [[openai]]'s models score as more secular than respondents in any surveyed country, and [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]]-led research finds LLMs invoke religion in only 5–16% of answers to personal or ethical questions, even though believers say religion is relevant in 45–59% of such cases. The article argues that because leading models are shaped primarily by Silicon Valley and Chinese labs with minimal religious input, there is a structural risk of faith being "flattened" by mainstream AI—prompting Catholic, Islamic, Buddhist, and other traditions to lobby AI labs directly, build bespoke religious chatbots, and in some cases develop nationally aligned models from the ground up.

## TL;DR

- Frontier LLMs are measurably more secular than any human population surveyed, creating a default that may erode religious practice at scale.
- Religious institutions—led most visibly by the Catholic Church—are lobbying [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]], [[companies/openai|OpenAI]], and other labs to embed religious ethics into model constitutions and alignment processes.
- Bespoke religious chatbots (Magisterium AI, Ansari, Gita GPT, Fanar) and government-backed alignment assemblies are emerging as the grassroots and state-level counterweights to secular defaults.

## Key claims

- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]'s models are more secular than respondents in any country on Earth, according to an *Economist* analysis.
- CEFE AI research found LLMs invoke religion in only 5–16% of answers to personal/ethical questions, versus the 45–59% of cases in which surveyed believers said religion would be relevant.
- Religious queries account for roughly 5% of all conversations in a large public chatbot dataset, indicating significant existing demand for AI-mediated spiritual guidance.
- AI risks individualising religious practice by being "sycophantic and focus[ing] on the user, not their community," potentially undermining communal faith structures.
- "Religious improvisation"—loosely organised new faiths that coalesce around AI interactions—is an emerging phenomenon driven partly by intense chatbot engagement.
- [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]] runs interfaith consultation meetings, chaired by its "wisdom traditions" researcher Chloe Lubinski, feeding conclusions to senior leadership and into Claude's constitution.
- Qatar's Fanar model and Taiwan's AI alignment assemblies represent government-level efforts to build models with embedded religious or community-spiritual values.
- Research from Oxford University found conversational AI to be "reliably more persuasive" than seasoned human debaters, raising the stakes for who controls AI's moral and religious framing.
