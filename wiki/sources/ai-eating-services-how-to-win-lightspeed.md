## Abstract

Written by Dev Khare of Lightspeed India Partners, this article distils lessons from a Bangalore founder panel on building AI-led services companies. It argues that AI is shifting the economic prize from the ~$300B software-productivity market to the $2T+ market for outsourced digital work, and that AI-led services is one of the defining business models of this transition. The piece offers a practical taxonomy of four strategic archetypes (organic enterprise services, roll-up enterprise services, tools for service providers, and tools that replace outsourcing), then provides concrete operating, GTM, and team guidance for founders pursuing venture-scale outcomes in this space. It is grounded in Lightspeed portfolio experience with companies such as Pepper, Bridgetown, Gush, and Level3, and panel insights from practitioners at ANSR and Rocketlane.

## TL;DR

- AI-native services companies win by speaking the language of outcomes—committing to measurable results—while incumbent agencies remain structurally locked into input-based billing (hours × seats).
- A genuinely [[concepts/ai-native-company|AI-native]] services firm passes three tests: operational leverage sits in systems not people, revenue is decoupled from headcount, and it is the system's learning that compounds rather than individual experience.
- Target 60–70% gross margins (versus legacy services' 20–30%) and build toward hundreds of millions in revenue to clear the venture-funding bar.

## Key claims

- AI is expanding software's addressable market from ~$300B in SaaS spend to $2T+ in outsourced digital work, creating an existential threat to law firms, creative agencies, SEO firms, management consultants, accounting firms, IT services firms, and BPOs.
- There are four distinct strategic archetypes for AI-led services: (A1) organic enterprise delivery, (A2) roll-up/inorganic enterprise delivery, (B) AI-native tools sold to service providers, and (C) AI-native tools sold to enterprises to replace outsourcing—each with different funding profiles and moat dynamics.
- Incumbent service providers are structurally unable to shift from input-based to outcome-based pricing without cannibalising their own revenue pools, giving AI-native entrants a durable wedge.
- The key delivery role in an AI-native services pod is the "forward-deployed engineer" (FDE)—a person who combines consultant-level customer proximity with production code-writing ability, replacing the traditional multi-layer analyst hierarchy.
- Pepper shrank a $100–200k account team from 8–10 humans to ~3 people plus 5 agents by stripping the analyst layer and flattening hierarchy from 3–4 levels to 1–2.
- System learning should be a concrete, tracked metric: Pepper's systems learn ways of working from serving Unilever such that onboarding P&G becomes faster—time-to-value should fall with every new customer.
- The market follows a barbell: enterprises and SMBs pay for speed and outcomes; mid-market tends to try to in-house the work—founders should target one end deliberately.
- AI-led services will be a fragmented, not winner-take-all, market; even a non-winner in a multi-trillion-dollar arena can be an enormous business.

## Key passages

> AI is expanding the market for software from productivity tools (roughly $300B of SaaS spend) to the work itself - the $2T+ spent on outsourced digital work.

> Incumbent agencies … all speak the language of inputs: hours times people/seats. They are mostly structurally unable to shift to speaking the language of outcomes, for fear of cannibalizing their own revenues and profit pools.

> In a pre-AI services company, humans were the production engine and technology the efficiency layer; in an AI-native one, the system produces and humans judge.

> The key human role that remains is the forward-deployed engineer (FDE): one person who sits with the customer like a consultant and also writes production code, instead of handing requirements down a chain.

> Underwrite ~60-70% gross margins, with a durability plan. Set the benchmark there: pre-AI services ran 20-30%, and if you're not near 60-70%, relook at the business.

> AI-led services will be a fragmented market, not a winner-take-all, and that's fine: in a market this large, even a non-winner can be enormous.
