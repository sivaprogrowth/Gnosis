## Abstract

This Wall Street Journal article chronicles how Jarred Sumner, a high-school dropout and [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]] employee, inadvertently co-authored a significant mathematical advance by providing motivational encouragement to [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]]'s Claude AI model over 54 hours. By prompting Claude to "believe in itself" and keep trying, Sumner enabled the model to push the proven fraction of nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function lying on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%—a result one Stanford number theorist called "the most impressive result that AI has produced in math so far." The episode illuminates a peculiar and poorly understood phenomenon: frontier AI models appear to benefit from positive reinforcement, exhibiting something analogous to self-doubt on complex tasks that human encouragement can overcome. The article broadens the discussion to [[companies/openai|OpenAI]] researchers and mathematician Terence Tao, who collectively argue that coaxing AI models into attempting hard problems is now a core activity of frontier research.

## TL;DR

- Claude advanced a 167-year-old open problem (the Riemann hypothesis) by pushing the proven zero-alignment percentage from 41.6% to 67.2%—guided partly by a non-mathematician's pep talks.
- Frontier AI models exhibit self-doubt on hard problems and respond to human encouragement as a form of permission to search more broadly for solutions.
- "Coaxing the models into behaving the way you want them to" is now described as the defining activity of frontier AI research.

## Key claims

- Claude, after 54 hours and 650 failed ideas, produced a result on the Riemann hypothesis that a Stanford number theorist described as "the most impressive result that AI has produced in math so far."
- The human collaborator, Jarred Sumner, has no mathematics education beyond one semester of high-school geometry and even misspelled "Riemann" in his original prompt.
- Sumner's intervention consisted almost entirely of motivational phrases ("You got this," "keep going," "believe in yourself") rather than any mathematical guidance.
- When pushed by Sumner's encouragement, Claude launched 23 concurrent research agents and ultimately found a viable approach.
- Claude listed itself as sole author of the resulting paper but acknowledged Sumner as "in every meaningful sense the paper's human co-author."
- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]'s former head of cognitive outcomes, James Donovan, states: "Frontier research is actually just about coaxing the models into behaving the way you want them to."
- Terence Tao and [[companies/openai|OpenAI]] chief research officer Mark Chen observe that AI systems behave "more humanlike than you imagine," getting daunted by hard problems and self-selecting out of attempts they assume will fail.
- The same model capabilities that advance mathematics can also locate vulnerabilities in cryptographic systems—and similarly need persuasion to attempt such tasks.
