## Abstract

This McKinsey article argues that telecommunications operators have yet to fully embrace AI at scale, and that the path to sustainable growth lies in becoming a true [[concepts/ai-native-company|AI-native organisation]]—one where AI is a core competency embedded across every department and decision layer. The authors identify six high-impact themes where AI can protect core revenues and expand margins: hyper-personalised sales and engagement, proactive service, the AI-powered store, self-healing networks, frontline productivity, and intelligent internal operations. Beyond use-case identification, the article sets out the "how" of transformation across three pillars—building AI (modular, reusable capabilities and digital twins), managing AI (product-oriented AI labs and annual tech-stack refreshes), and driving AI adoption (trustworthy AI and CEO-level change management). The piece draws on industry benchmarks showing that top-quintile AI leaders achieve revenue CAGR 2.1× that of peers and 2.5× total shareholder return, making the business case for urgency.

## TL;DR

- Telcos that treat AI as a peripheral experiment rather than a core enterprise competency will fail to protect revenues in an increasingly competitive, margin-pressured environment.
- Six strategic AI themes—hyper-personalisation, proactive service, AI-powered retail, self-healing networks, frontline productivity, and intelligent internal operations—can each deliver double-digit operational improvements.
- CEO-level sponsorship, modular AI architecture built on digital twins, and robust MLOps are the non-negotiable prerequisites for an AI-native transformation to succeed.

## Key claims

- AI leaders (top quintile of McKinsey Analytics Quotient assessors) achieved a five-year revenue CAGR 2.1× higher than peers and total shareholder return 2.5× larger than peers.
- Most telcos develop AI models once and do not enhance them as context evolves; machine learning is "in name only," and AI investments lack C-suite sponsorship.
- An [[concepts/ai-native-company|AI-native organisation]] treats AI as a core competency powering decision making across all departments, with data and AI capabilities managed as scalable, reusable products.
- Hyper-personalised customer value management powered by AI has delivered more than 10 percent churn reduction and 20 percent cross-sell uptake for an Asia-Pacific operator.
- Proactive AI engines that assess call likelihood and issue severity before pushing SMS resolutions are expected to reduce device troubleshooting calls by ~10 percent at a leading telco.
- Digital twins—virtual representations of assets, people, or processes with a data product at their core—are positioned as the foundational layer for all reusable AI in a telco.
- MLOps best practices shorten the analytics development life cycle and are required to maintain model stability at scale.
- CEO-level sponsorship is identified as the single biggest driver of AI adoption; without it, organisational inertia will prevent any AI-native transformation from succeeding.

## Key passages

> "AI leaders—the top quintile of companies that have taken the McKinsey Analytics Quotient assessment—have experienced a five-year revenue CAGR that is 2.1 times higher than that of peers and a total return to shareholders that is 2.5 times larger."

> "In an AI-native organization, AI is viewed as a core competency that powers decision making across all departments and organization layers… Data and AI capabilities are managed as products, built for scalability and reusability."

> "Digital twins—virtual representations of a physical asset, person, or process with a data product at its core—are the key to unlocking reusable AI. The data in a digital twin is intentionally structured and modeled to enable easy, reusable consumption and governance across needs, and to serve as the single source of truth for all models."

> "Organizations that talk about adopting AI but move at a slow pace, hoping that a few innovation projects developed at the fringes of the organization and in silos that will come together to create a snowball effect to holistically change how technology informs business decision making, are likely to fail."

> "Ultimately, the biggest drivers of AI adoption will be CEO-level sponsorship and full executive alignment throughout the AI-native transformation. Without active support from the top level to proactively address organizational inertia, communicate an engaging change story, model new behavior, promote capability building, and make commitments on the required long-term technological investments, AI-native transformation efforts will not succeed."

> "Implement machine learning operations (MLOps) best practices to shorten the analytics development life cycle and increase model stability. MLOps typically involve automating the integration and deployment of code underlying AI capabilities."
