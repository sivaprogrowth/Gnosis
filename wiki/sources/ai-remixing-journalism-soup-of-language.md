## Abstract

This *Economist* article, published from the Global Investigative Journalism Conference in Perugia (May 2023), surveys how artificial intelligence is reshaping newsroom workflows — from automated earnings reports and social-media event detection to generative tools that write, proofread, and reformat stories. It profiles early adopters including the Associated Press, Reuters, Semafor, and Radar AI, and quotes researchers and editors who argue that generative models are dissolving the article as journalism's fundamental unit, replacing it with a personalised "soup of language." The piece situates AI disruption within a broader crisis for digital media — illustrated by [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]-adjacent hype alongside BuzzFeed's closure and Vice's bankruptcy preparations — and concludes that job losses from AI rank lower than pre-existing structural threats to journalists' employment.

## TL;DR

- AI is moving beyond rote automation (earnings reports, comment moderation) into creative newsroom roles: newsgathering, writing, editing, and cross-format reformatting.
- Generative AI threatens to replace the discrete article with a fluid, personalised "soup of language," fundamentally altering what journalism *is*, not just how it is produced.
- Industry leaders frame AI as saving journalism (the institution) rather than journalists (the workers), against a backdrop of severe structural decline in digital media.

## Key claims

- The Associated Press began publishing AI-generated company earnings reports as early as 2014, marking one of the earliest mainstream uses of automated journalism.
- Reuters uses machine-pattern recognition on large data sets for newsgathering; AP deploys AI for "event detection" by scanning social media.
- Nick Diakopoulos of Northwestern University demonstrated that [[companies/openai|ChatGPT]] could assess the newsworthiness of research papers with a human-editor correlation coefficient of 0.58.
- Radar AI's five human journalists have filed more than 400,000 partly automated stories for local papers since 2018.
- Schibsted launched an AI tool in November 2022 to condense long articles into short Snapchat packages, exemplifying format-reshaping use cases.
- David Caswell of BBC News predicts AI will change journalism more in the next three years than journalism changed in the prior thirty.
- Caswell argues generative models are undermining the article as "the fundamental unit of journalism," turning the news into a personalised "soup of language."
- Lisa Gibbs of AP contends that AI is not currently the highest threat to journalists' employment compared with existing structural pressures; BuzzFeed and Vice are cited as evidence.

## Key passages
