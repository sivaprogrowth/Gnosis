## Abstract

China is entering a third era of internet development driven by AI super-apps with agentic capabilities — systems that can autonomously choose, purchase, and deliver goods and services on behalf of users. Building on sprawling logistics networks and mobile super-app portfolios accumulated during the smartphone era, tech giants Alibaba, ByteDance, and Tencent are racing to integrate their AI chatbots (Qwen, Doubao, and Hy3 respectively) with dominant consumer platforms (Taobao, Douyin, and WeChat). The article argues that the competitive pressure is ferocious, monetisation paths remain unclear, and new hardware entrants like Xiaomi and Huawei could disrupt the incumbents. The outcome will reshape China's digital economy and determine which company controls the next platform layer.

## TL;DR

- China's AI super-apps are moving from chatbots to full agentic services that autonomously transact on users' behalf, with 600m+ people already using some form of agentic app.
- Alibaba, ByteDance, and Tencent are integrating their LLMs into existing super-apps, but monetisation remains unresolved as most services are currently free.
- Hardware makers Xiaomi and Huawei represent a credible threat to displace app-layer incumbents by embedding agentic AI at the OS level.

## Key claims

- More than 600 million people in China are estimated to have already used some form of agentic app, signalling unusually rapid mass adoption.
- China has cycled through two prior internet eras — Baidu-dominated web search, then smartphone super-apps — and is now entering a third defined by agentic AI.
- Alibaba fully integrated its Qwen chatbot with Taobao on 11 May 2026, enabling AI-driven procurement across products and services.
- ByteDance is preparing a comparable integration between its Doubao chatbot and Douyin (which already incorporates shopping).
- Tencent's Hy3 model is in testing and being slowly integrated with WeChat's ecosystem of millions of business mini-programs.
- AI super-apps are currently ad-free, but the article suggests that profitability will likely require ad-driven recommendations — replicating the dynamic that drove users away from Baidu.
- A ByteDance–ZTE AI smartphone launched in December failed partly because Alibaba and Tencent blocked access to their payment platforms, illustrating how incumbent payment rails act as a moat.
- Xiaomi and Huawei, with hundreds of millions of users each, are embedding agentic AI in smartphones and EVs, and regulatory intervention could prevent incumbents from blocking them.

## Key passages

> "Already more than 600m of its people are thought to have used some form of so-called agentic app. China is speeding towards a future in which AI chooses, purchases and delivers many of the goods and services people consume, upending its digital economy in the process."

> "On May 13th Pony Ma, founder of Tencent, warned that a messy 'land grab' for AI services was in the offing."

> "On May 11th Alibaba announced that it had fully integrated Qwen, its chatbot, with Taobao, its shopping app. With a few simple commands the AI can now procure all manner of products and services."

> "In private China's tech giants say that their AI super-apps do not recommend products based on ad spending. But to make these profitable, they may eventually have to."

> "Some in the industry worry that the emergence of a device powered by AI with agentic capabilities embedded in its operating system could displace existing super-apps."

> "Auto executives reckon that many young Chinese will access agentic services through voice discussions with their car."
