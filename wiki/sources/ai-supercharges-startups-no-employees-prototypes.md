## Abstract

This Financial Times article explores how AI agents are fundamentally reshaping start-up culture by dramatically compressing costs, timelines, and headcounts. Drawing on examples from Helsinki biotech labs to San Francisco drug-development ventures, the piece documents founders who are building companies with zero or near-zero employees, using tools such as [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]]'s Claude Opus 4.5 and Claude Code to generate prototypes in days and automate entire operational functions. Academic research and business registration data from both the US and UK corroborate a measurable surge in new company formation since the launch of [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]'s ChatGPT in late 2022. The article also flags emerging risks — "slop companies" producing mediocre AI-generated output, founder burnout from supervising always-on agents, and an accelerated competitive cycle where a company's rise and fall can both happen at unprecedented speed.

## TL;DR

- AI agents now enable founders to build companies with no employees, no VC funding, and prototype-to-customer cycles measured in days rather than months.
- New tools like Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.5 have compressed 18 months of development into three months for some start-ups.
- Weekly US business-formation applications have consistently exceeded 100,000 since ChatGPT's launch, up from a pre-2022 ceiling of ~80,000.

## Key claims

- Entrepreneurs Claudio Reiter and Leon Busch have instructed an AI agent to autonomously manage the construction of a fully automated biotech lab in Helsinki, coordinating a ~$500,000 budget including procurement and contractor management.
- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]] CEO Sam Altman noted in 2024 a "betting pool" among tech CEOs for the first year a one-person company reaches a billion-dollar valuation.
- Toby Mather, co-founder of AI start-up Rig, reported that after Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.5 launched on 24 November 2025, his team completed 18 months of "old world" development in just three months.
- Isabel Greenslade of Structured AI described using Claude Code to mock up a full product prototype within two days of a customer conversation.
- A joint University of British Columbia / Stockholm School of Economics study found that AI-era innovative companies are more likely to be founded outside traditional tech hubs like San Francisco and New York.
- Weekly new US business-formation applications have almost always exceeded 100,000 since ChatGPT's November 2022 launch, versus a historic ceiling of ~80,000 in the prior 15 years (per data analysed by [[companies/apollo-global-management|Apollo]]).
- UK application-software company incorporations jumped 40 per cent to 28,100 in H1 of the year, making it the most popular sector for start-ups (per Beauhurst and NatWest analysis of Companies House data).
- Venture investor Helena Gagern warns that while AI dramatically lowers the floor, "slop companies" producing generic AI output risk commoditisation, and human salespeople remain essential to cut through AI-driven spam.

## Key passages

> "We thought it was a bit arrogant to assume humans know best how to build the lab that will teach AI wet-lab science. Why not let AI build its own? So we captured what we learnt and now an agent can manage a lab build itself." — Claudio Reiter

> "He left his company with the insight, 'I think I can rebuild every single thing that humans are doing in my company right now with AI.' Everything — in terms of disease to work on, the research, the trial design — was driven by AI agents. That was for me the eye-opening moment in terms of how far you can push building a company with practically zero humans working on it." — Helena Gagern

> "Opus 4.5 appeared on November 24 [2025]. We have since completed 18 months' of 'old world' development in three months." — Toby Mather

> "The overall level of the playing field has risen, but once you've reached the basic level, the difference will be made by the people in your team." — Vincent Huguet
