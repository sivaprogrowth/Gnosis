## Abstract

This *Economist* briefing examines how [[companies/anthropic]]'s rapid commercial ascent — driven by its early bet on enterprise coding tools and [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]] — has made it both a financial juggernaut and a political target. The firm's annual recurring revenue has surpassed [[companies/openai]]'s, its valuation reached $965 bn in May 2026, and it filed for an IPO expected to value it at around $1 trn. Yet its CEO Dario Amodei's insistence on AI safety guardrails and calls for binding regulation have brought it into repeated conflict with the Trump administration, which has demanded export controls, blocked foreign access to its most capable models (Fable 5 and Mythos 5), and briefly declared it a "supply-chain risk." The article frames the clash as a structural tension between a quasi-ecclesiastical safety-first lab acting as an "unelected arbiter of safety" and a sovereign government that views frontier AI as a core instrument of national power — a tension that will persist until Congress legislates.

## TL;DR

- [[companies/anthropic]] has overtaken [[companies/openai]] on annual recurring revenue and valuation, with four-fifths of its revenue coming from enterprise customers, driven largely by its Claude Code coding agent.
- The Trump administration has twice intervened in Anthropic's operations on national-security grounds, restricting foreign access to its most capable models and threatening its IPO prospects.
- The underlying conflict is structural: without clear AI legislation, the government is forced to use blunt tools while Anthropic's CEO acts as a de facto unelected safety regulator.

## Key claims

- Anthropic's ARR has surpassed [[companies/openai]]'s and its valuation ($965 bn, May 2026) has exceeded OpenAI's ($852 bn, March 2026), with the firm projecting an operating profit in Q2 2026 — which would make it the first AI lab to do so.
- Approximately 80% of Anthropic's revenue comes from business customers, compared with ~40% for [[companies/openai]], giving Anthropic superior monetisation and revenue durability.
- Claude Code, a year-old coding agent, has been the primary growth driver, benefiting from step-by-step reasoning and tool use that give Anthropic an advantage in building [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]].
- The Trump administration has used the national-security threat posed by Mythos 5 — described as "superbly good at hacking" — as justification for demanding export licences for every foreign sale, effectively forcing Anthropic to shut the models down entirely for non-Americans.
- The administration lacks calibrated regulatory tools for AI: the executive order creating a voluntary pre-release framework for AI labs was issued only in early June 2026 and is not yet operational.
- Advisor David Sacks warned that Anthropic's "exponential" growth could give it "unprecedented control over the most important technology of our time," drawing a parallel with John D. Rockefeller's domination of the oil industry.
- The article argues that AI model commoditisation pressure is pushing labs like Anthropic to expand into agentic software that could supplant SaaS companies, threatening [[concepts/saas-cannibalisation]].
- Dario Amodei's preferred regulatory model is narrowly technocratic — standardised technical tests and audits modelled on the FAA — rather than broad governmental control.

## Key passages

> "For more than two years, Anthropic was in Open AI's shadow. Although it released its own chatbot, Claude, shortly after Open AI released Chat GPT, it was Open AI's offering that won most attention. Anthropic's slow start forced it to focus, however. Rather than doubling down on chatbots, which have many non-paying users and require subscriptions or advertising to make profits, it took a narrower bet on corporate customers."

> "Anthropic is getting the better of most comparisons. [...] Anthropic's ARR has raced past Open AI's [...] Anthropic has told investors it will generate an operating profit in the second quarter, which would make it the first AI lab to do so."

> "Mr Sacks recently warned that if Anthropic's 'exponential' growth continues for long, it could gain 'unprecedented control over the most important technology of our time'. He drew an unflattering parallel with John D. Rockefeller's domination of the oil industry in the 19th century."

> "Without clear rules, says Dean Ball, a former Trump administration official at the Foundation for American Innovation, a think-tank, the risk is that America winds up with government 'dominance over AI' rather than American 'dominance in AI'."

> "Although Mr Amodei sometimes talks in alarmist terms about the risks of AI, his favoured approach is a narrowly technocratic form of regulation. He calls for rules modelled on regulatory agencies such as the Federal Aviation Administration, whereby frontier AI models would be put through standardised technical tests and audits."

> "No company gets to shake the foundations of state sovereignty while staying blithely above the raw reality of politics."
