## Abstract

The Economist examines the competitive dynamics between [[companies/anthropic]] and [[companies/openai]] following Anthropic's record-breaking IPO, which raised $107bn and valued the company at $1.5trn — surpassing OpenAI's most recent private valuation of $1.3trn. Anthropic's ascent was driven primarily by the explosive growth of coding agents, with its Claude Code product propelling projected 2026 revenues to $53bn against OpenAI's $35bn. The article argues that while OpenAI dominated the first phase of AI competition through chatbots and retains a massive consumer base, Anthropic seized the second phase by focusing narrowly on code generation and long-horizon agentic tasks. A third phase — centred on broad knowledge-work automation — is now beginning, and the two firms are pursuing meaningfully different strategies to win it. The piece concludes that OpenAI's breadth of ambition risks spreading it too thin, while Anthropic's focus has delivered its first quarterly operating profit.

## TL;DR

- [[companies/anthropic]] completed the largest IPO of all time ($107bn raised, $1.5trn valuation), overtaking [[companies/openai]]'s private valuation on the back of coding-agent dominance.
- Anthropic is narrowly focused on code and enterprise agentic work; OpenAI is pursuing consumers, advertising, devices, and humanoid robots simultaneously.
- Both firms are projecting hundreds of billions in annual revenue by 2030, but Anthropic has already turned its first quarterly operating profit while OpenAI remains deeply loss-making.

## Key claims

- Anthropic's IPO raised $107bn — the largest in history — valuing the company at $1.5trn, ahead of OpenAI's $1.3trn private-round valuation.
- [[companies/goldman-sachs]] (lead bank on the IPO) projects Anthropic's 2026 revenue at $53bn; OpenAI expects $35bn for the same year, though accounting differences complicate direct comparison.
- Goldman Sachs projects Anthropic's annual revenue will reach $636bn by 2030; William Blair forecasts $414bn; OpenAI has told investors it targets $446bn by 2030.
- Coding-agent revenue currently follows an "extreme power law," with the heaviest users — almost always software engineers — generating a disproportionate share of spending.
- On the MirrorCode long-horizon benchmark, OpenAI's most recent model (released last month) is still outperformed by Anthropic's top model from July 2026.
- Anthropic's consulting arm Ode, backed by [[companies/blackstone]] and Hellman & Friedman, reports that more than half of clients who redesigned processes boosted profits by at least $10m.
- OpenAI's consumer segment faces competition from [[companies/google]]'s Gemini and [[companies/meta]]'s Muse personal agent, as well as a broad internal portfolio including an upcoming doughnut-shaped speaker device and a humanoid robot.
- Anthropic recorded its first quarterly operating profit in Q2 2026; OpenAI remains deeply in the red and will need to pursue a public listing soon.

## Key passages

> "It is Anthropic, founded by Openai secessionists, that has raised the largest sum of all time through an initial public offering (ipo), with a total haul of $107bn. Before trading began on November 19th, after we went to press, the firm was worth $1.5trn, topping the $1.3trn at which the still unlisted Openai was valued in a recent private fundraising round."

> "Revenue from agents currently adheres to an 'extreme power law', says one Anthropic investor. The biggest customers contribute an unusually high share of sales, and the heaviest users within those companies—who are nearly always software engineers—are behind much of the spending."

> "Aaron Levie, chief executive of Box, a cloud-storage company that works with both ai labs, says that having 'the right kind of product experiences with the right sales efforts' could prove even more important than having the best models."

> "On MirrorCode, one long-horizon benchmark, Openai's leading model, released last month, is still outperformed by the top offering from Anthropic as far back as July."

> "Unlike Anthropic, which generated its first quarterly operating profit in the second three months of this year, Openai remains deep in the red. It will need to follow its rival into the public markets as soon as it can."

> "Anthropic's ipo prospectus barely mentions consumers. It has sworn off advertising, worrying it could muddy Claude's ability to give helpful answers and incentivise the company to build addictive products."
