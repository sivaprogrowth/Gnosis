## Abstract

This article reports findings from a field experiment in which 2,400 job applications were sent to employers across the U.S. using three candidate profiles — a failed startup co-founder, a successful startup co-founder, and a non-founder tech employee — to test how former founders fare in the hiring process. The study found that former founders were 43% less likely to receive a callback than non-founders, and counterintuitively, successful founders were 33% less likely to be invited to interview than failed founders. Follow-up interviews with 20 technical recruiters revealed that concerns about cultural fit and commitment — not capability — primarily drive this bias. The article also identifies a structural misalignment between senior leaders' stated preference for entrepreneurial talent and recruiters' short-term performance incentives, and offers practical guidance for both former founders and employers.

## TL;DR

- Former startup founders are significantly less likely to receive job interview callbacks than equivalent non-founder candidates, with successful founders penalised more than failed ones.
- Recruiters deprioritise founders not because of doubts about capability but because of fears about poor cultural fit and flight risk (leaving to start another company).
- There is a systemic misalignment between executive-level hiring aspirations and the short-term retention metrics that actually govern recruiter behaviour.

## Key claims

- In a 2,400-application field experiment, former founders were **43% less likely** to receive a first-round interview callback than non-founder candidates with otherwise identical profiles.
- Successful startup founders were **33% less likely** to be invited to interview than founders whose ventures had failed, inverting the intuitive expectation that success signals competence.
- Recruiter interviews confirmed that concerns about **fit and commitment** — not capability — are the primary driver: recruiters worried successful founders were "looking for a paid vacation" before launching their next startup.
- The penalty for founder experience was strongest at **older, more-established firms**, consistent with those firms having more-rigid cultures and more-hierarchical structures.
- Some recruiters viewed **failed founders positively**, reasoning that external factors often cause startup failure and that failed founders would be more motivated to prove themselves in a traditional role.
- Despite the overall bias, founders still received callbacks **11–16% of the time**, reflecting sustained demand for technical talent.
- A structural incentive problem exists: recruiters are evaluated on short-term metrics such as retention rate, making them risk-averse toward founder candidates even when senior leaders want entrepreneurial hires.
- Former founders can partially offset the bias by proactively addressing fit and commitment concerns, leveraging referrals, and targeting younger firms.

## Key passages

> We found that all else being equal, former founders were 43% less likely to receive a "callback" (or first-round interview) than non-founders.

> Former founders with a successful venture were 33% less likely to be invited to interview than founders with a failed venture. This suggests that recruiters may be more concerned with candidates' fit and commitment than with their capabilities.

> "We are scared [former successful founders] won't settle into their role. Few [founders] even get an interview but the ones that have are 'failures.'" — Recruiter at a large, LA-based tech company

> No one will know if you failed to offer an interview to a candidate who could have been the firm's next big thinker, they explained, but no one will forget it if you fight for a successful former founder, only to have them leave the company within a year to begin a new startup, maybe even with another employee in tow.

> Recruiters are the gatekeepers of the top of the hiring funnel: While a positive initial evaluation from a recruiter doesn't mean a bad candidate will get a job, a negative initial evaluation will remove them from the hiring process completely.
