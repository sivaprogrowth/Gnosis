## Abstract

McKinsey's 2026 Global B2B Pulse Survey — drawing on nearly 4,000 decision-makers across 13 countries — finds that digital and omnichannel capabilities that once differentiated market leaders have become the minimum threshold for competing in B2B markets. A decisive performance divide is opening between leaders and laggards, driven by three reinforcing engines: hyperpersonalization at one-to-one precision, scaled deployment of generative and [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]], and disciplined sales-led account-based marketing (ABM) governance. Market leaders — defined as those with >10% market share growth — are four times more likely to deploy true one-to-one personalization, twice as likely to have adopted gen AI, and significantly more likely to anchor ABM ownership in sales rather than shared or marketing-led structures. Together, these capabilities form a self-reinforcing commercial operating system that compounds advantage over time, making integration — not individual tool adoption — the defining differentiator of this era.

## TL;DR

- Omnichannel and e-commerce are no longer differentiators — they are the new survival floor; what separates leaders from laggards is the integration of hyperpersonalization, scaled AI, and disciplined ABM governance into a single, compounding commercial system.
- Market leaders are 4× more likely to deploy one-to-one personalization and 2× more likely to have fully adopted gen AI, producing a 60% vs. 21% gap in double-digit revenue growth.
- B2B buyers cluster into three recurring archetypes (Adapters, Seekers, Innovators) and use an average of ten channels across the buying journey, following a stable "rule of thirds" split among in-person, remote, and digital interactions.

## Key claims

- **Survival threshold shift**: E-commerce (offered by 71% of respondents) and omnichannel engagement are now baseline requirements, not differentiators; ~⅓ of B2B revenue flows through digital channels among companies with e-commerce.
- **Performance divide is wide**: 60% of market leaders report double-digit revenue growth vs. 21% of laggards; 90% of leaders report improved sales effectiveness vs. 55% of laggards.
- **Hyperpersonalization gap**: Leaders are 4× more likely to deploy one-to-one personalization (20% vs. 5%); the largest differences appear in high-impact moments — social media, chatbots, and events.
- **AI flywheel**: Gen AI adoption is accelerating (22% fully implemented, up from 19%); leaders are 2× more likely to have adopted it and 71% increased AI budgets by double digits YoY vs. 25% of laggards — creating a self-reinforcing enthusiasm→usage→implementation→success→reinvestment cycle.
- **ABM governance matters**: Sales-led ABM ownership correlates with 5–10 percentage-point higher representation in top revenue growth bands; 40% of laggards rely on joint governance vs. 32% of leaders.
- **Buyer archetypes are stable**: Adapters (53%, up from 44% in 2024), Seekers (29%), and Innovators (18%) persist across industries and geographies; each requires a distinct personalization approach.
- **Gen AI now a top-5 discovery channel**: Gen AI has entered the top five channels for supplier discovery and evaluation, alongside supplier websites, in-person interaction, web search, and videoconferencing.
- **Switching drivers have shifted upstream**: Inconsistent information across teams is now the #1 reason buyers switch suppliers (cited by 52%), followed by inability to access knowledgeable representatives — reflecting buyers judging suppliers as integrated commercial systems.

## Key passages

> "What once separated market leaders from the rest of the field—digital tools, omnichannel engagement, and e-commerce capabilities—has become the threshold for survival."

> "Sixty percent of market leaders report double-digit revenue growth in 2025, compared with just 21 percent of laggards. Ninety percent of leaders report improved sales effectiveness, versus 55 percent of their lower-performing peers."

> "Leaders are four times more likely than their peers to deploy one-to-one personalization (20 percent versus 5 percent). They are twice as likely to report adopting gen AI (44 percent versus 22 percent) and significantly more likely to have increased AI investment by double digits year over year (71 percent versus 25 percent)."

> "If hyperpersonalization provides precision and gen AI provides amplification, governance provides control. Without clear accountability in the revenue engine, even advanced capabilities struggle to translate into consistent performance."

> "The question is no longer whether to invest in AI, personalization, or account-based marketing. Most organizations already have. The question is whether those investments are integrated into a coherent commercial architecture capable of sustained growth."

> "A decade ago, in 2016, McKinsey's B2B Pulse Survey found that early adopters of B2B e-commerce significantly outperformed peers, achieving five times faster growth, 30 percent higher acquisition efficiency, and 13.5 percent EBIT growth."
