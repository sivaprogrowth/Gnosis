## Abstract

This article by Michael Lombardi, Global Consulting Principal at [[companies/ogilvy|Ogilvy Consulting]], argues that while B2B marketers have rightly focused on Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) to ensure brand visibility in AI-generated answers, the real challenge is what happens after the answer. Drawing on 2025–2026 research from Forrester, [[companies/gartner|Gartner]], LinkedIn's B2B Institute, and TrustRadius, Lombardi identifies five emergent tensions reshaping the B2B buyer decision process: the irrelevance of generic AI capability claims, the gap between fast AI answers and genuine buyer confidence, the risk that hyper-personalised messaging fractures buying-group consensus, the opportunity to listen rather than broadcast through Account Based Marketing (ABM), and the growing premium on authentic peer experience over vendor-produced proof. The piece is significant because it reframes the AI disruption challenge for B2B CMOs from a discoverability problem into a full buyer-journey redesign problem.

## TL;DR

- Getting into the AI answer is necessary but insufficient — brands must now redesign the entire post-answer buyer experience to convert AI-informed prospects into confident purchasers.
- Tailored messaging to individual stakeholders actively harms buying-group consensus; a shared messaging spine that the whole room can agree on outperforms role-specific personalisation.
- Authentic peer community and customer-owned proof are becoming more valuable than vendor case studies, because AI can synthesise claims but cannot replicate lived experience.

## Key claims

- 42% of buyers who recalled a GenAI ad remembered only a generic message about AI's importance, and just 18% recalled a business benefit (LinkedIn B2B Institute & Ehrenberg-Bass, 2025), making "we have AI" messaging increasingly undifferentiated.
- 94% of business buyers use AI during their buying process, yet the same proportion fact-check AI answers at least some of the time (Forrester 2026; TrustRadius 2026), creating a "validation gap" that brands must fill downstream of the answer engine.
- 73% of B2B purchases now span three or more departments, and buying groups double in size when the purchase includes generative AI features (Forrester, 2026).
- 74% of B2B buying teams demonstrate unhealthy conflict during the decision process ([[companies/gartner|Gartner]], 2025), making consensus-building a primary marketing objective.
- Content tailored to individuals had a 59% negative impact on consensus, while content built for the group as a whole improved consensus by 20%; groups reaching consensus were 2.5× more likely to call the deal high quality ([[companies/gartner|Gartner]], 2025).
- ABM adoption has grown from 46% (2022) to 72% (2026) of B2B marketers running some form of ABM, yet most implementations remain broadcast-oriented rather than signal-driven (Visionary Marketing, 2026).
- Brands that move from publishing case studies to opening genuine peer access — connecting buyers with customers willing to discuss failures — can compound trust because today's buyer becomes tomorrow's reference.

## Key passages

> Nearly every B2B company is racing to prove it's in the AI game. In the process, they risk making similar-sounding claims, using the same language, often written by the same tools.

> Fast answers aren't the same as *understanding*; buyers with an AI answer still go looking for the brands that can help them make sense of it.

> When the CEO, CFO and CIO each hear a different version of you, you've handed them something to argue about. Gartner found that content tailored to individuals had a 59% negative impact on consensus, while content built for the group as a whole improved it by 20%.

> The easiest move then is for B2B marketers to use AI to churn out more personalized and more frequent account outreach. Instead of an ABM barrage, a better use of AI is to build a brand that listens.

> AI can tell a buyer what a solution does, how it compares, and what it should deliver. What it can't tell them authentically is what it was like to live through it: where it broke down, who had to be convinced, and whether they'd make the same call again.

> Today's buyer becomes the person the next buyer trusts, and what they say may even shape the next AI answer.
