## Abstract

In a March 2023 interview at the Wall Street Journal's CIO Network Summit, [[andreessen-horowitz|Andreessen Horowitz]] general partner Martin Casado argues that generative AI represents a qualitatively different technology shift from traditional machine-learning AI, because it drives the marginal cost of *creation* toward zero — analogous to how the microchip zeroed out the marginal cost of compute and the internet zeroed out the marginal cost of distribution. Casado reflects on early experiments with [[openai|OpenAI]]'s GPT-4 and finds that large language models already rival junior analysts on summarisation tasks, while excelling at brainstorming and code-assist use cases via tools like GitHub Copilot. He warns that much current corporate adoption is "ChatGPT-washing" — bolt-on integrations with little substance — but notes that a cohort of genuinely net-new businesses driven purely by generative quality are already reaching hundreds of millions in annual recurring revenue. The piece is notable for its intellectual honesty: Casado repeatedly admits uncertainty about which companies will ultimately capture the value and what zero-cost creation will mean for society at large.

## TL;DR

- Generative AI drives the marginal cost of content creation toward zero, a shift Casado rates as comparably historic to the microchip and the internet.
- Consumer behaviour is leading enterprise adoption, mirroring the pattern seen with the PC and the internet.
- Most corporate integration is superficial "ChatGPT-washing"; genuine net-new generative businesses are the meaningful signal.

## Key claims

- The cost gap between AI-generated images and human-equivalent production is four to five orders of magnitude — a rarity in Casado's 20-year technology career.
- The right analogy for generative AI's economic impact is: microchip → zero marginal cost of compute; internet → zero marginal cost of distribution; generative AI → zero marginal cost of creation.
- Neither Casado nor anyone else yet knows what zero-cost creation will mean at civilisational scale ("we don't have a clue").
- The foundation-model landscape poses the same structural question as early coprocessors vs. CPUs: will specialised models persist as independent businesses, or be consumed by a general-purpose model?
- GPT-4's errors in summarising company write-ups mirror the errors junior human analysts make, suggesting the technology is already a credible, if imperfect, cognitive co-processor.
- Behavioural adoption is overwhelmingly consumer-led (coding assistants, brainstorming, summarisation) before it reaches enterprise workflows at scale.
- Casado distinguishes three adoption tiers: (1) ChatGPT-washing bolt-ons, (2) a small number of genuinely improved legacy businesses, and (3) net-new generative-native companies with real revenue — the last being the most consequential signal.
- Large-scale integration of generative AI into existing organisational operations has not yet arrived and organisations lack the frameworks to consume the technology.

## Key passages

> "I think there's a chance that with these generative things, not traditional AI, the marginal cost of creation can converge on zero. And I don't think we have a clue what that means."

> "If you compare that generation, that one one-hundredth of a penny, to hiring a designer or going on Photoshop or, God forbid, flying to the Alps, you're talking about four or five orders of magnitude in difference in cost, the time to create that."

> "It's all consumer-driven. And I remember this is kind of how the PC happened. And this is definitely how the internet happened, too."

> "I think a lot of the things that we tend to hate about these language models, they're kind of stuff we hate about ourselves."

> "There's a lot of use that, I think, is like ChatGPT-washing... The least mature, in my opinion, is existing organizations introducing it into their operations. I don't think that we're at that point at a large scale yet."

> "I really think this is cold-fusion powerful, but we as organizations don't know how to consume it."
