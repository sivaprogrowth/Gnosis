## Abstract

Writing in the *Wall Street Journal*, Henry Kissinger, Eric Schmidt, and Daniel Huttenlocher argue that generative artificial intelligence — exemplified by [[companies/openai|ChatGPT]] — poses a philosophical and civilisational challenge comparable in scale to the Enlightenment. Unlike Enlightenment science, which accumulated knowledge through testable, explicable steps, generative AI produces outputs whose reasoning processes remain opaque even to their creators, creating a dangerous gap between human knowledge and human understanding. The authors warn of compounding risks: automation bias, AI hallucination, concentration of power in the hands of few model operators, and the atrophy of human critical faculties. They call urgently for new philosophical, educational, and governance frameworks — and for humans to cultivate the confidence to interrogate, rather than uncritically obey, machine outputs.

## TL;DR

- Generative AI advances human knowledge while undermining human *understanding*, because its reasoning is opaque and unexplainable even to its inventors.
- The concentration of enormous training costs in a small number of actors risks a new form of epistemic and political power concentration domestically and internationally.
- Humanity must develop new dialectical skills, educational models, and governance norms before defaulting to machine arbitration of reality — or risk permanent cognitive and moral dependency.

## Key claims

- The printing press spread human thought broadly; generative AI reverses that by distilling and elaborating existing thought, creating a gap between knowledge and understanding.
- Enlightenment science built certainty through replicable, step-by-step reasoning; generative AI solves riddles through processes that remain unknown, generating "cumulative ambiguities" rather than certainties.
- [[concepts/ai-hallucination-legal-filings|Hallucination]] (also called "stochastic parroting") is an inherent risk: ChatGPT sometimes fabricates plausible-sounding facts, citations, and sources.
- Automation bias — over-reliance on automated systems — is likely to be especially acute because generative AI produces authoritative-sounding text, reinforcing uncritical acceptance.
- The enormous cost of training large language models (upward of $1 billion per model) means the number of high-quality models will remain small, concentrating design and control even as their influence becomes diffuse.
- Generative AI could enable new forms of imperialism based on monopolising data and AI capability rather than territory.
- The authors argue that a "third way of knowing" — neither human reason nor religious faith — may emerge from AI, with profound implications for democracy and moral authority.
- Urgent adaptation is required in philosophy, education, and international governance; global harmonisation must emerge "by perception or by catastrophe," echoing Immanuel Kant.

## Key passages

> "Enlightenment science accumulated certainties; the new AI generates cumulative ambiguities… In the Age of AI, riddles are solved by processes that remain unknown. This disorienting paradox makes mysteries unmysterious but also unexplainable."

> "AI's outputs remain difficult to explain. The truth of Enlightenment science was trusted because each step of replicable experimental processes was also tested, hence trusted. The truth of generative AI will need to be justified by entirely different methods, and it may never become similarly absolute."

> "ChatGPT possesses a capacity for analysis that is qualitatively different from that of the human mind. The future therefore implies a collaboration not only with a different kind of technical entity but with a different kind of reasoning—which may be rational without being reasonable, trustworthy in one sense but not in another."

> "Leadership is likely to concentrate in hands of the fewer people and institutions who control access to the limited number of machines capable of high-quality synthesis of reality."

> "The arrival of an unknowable and apparently omniscient instrument, capable of altering reality, may trigger a resurgence in mystic religiosity… A third way of knowing the world may emerge, one that is neither human reason nor faith. What becomes of democracy in such a world?"

> "As we become *Homo technicus*, we hold an imperative to define the purpose of our species. It is up to us to provide the real answers."
