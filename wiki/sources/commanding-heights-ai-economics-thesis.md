## Abstract

*The Commanding Heights* is a sweeping macro-analytical essay arguing that artificial intelligence is following the classic pattern of world-changing technologies—where the originary inventors capture little of the ultimate wealth while value accrues downstream to whoever controls the means of diffusion and conduction. The essay argues that the current "Genesis" phase of AI (labs, chips, data centers, model training) will yield diminishing returns as frontier model costs rise ~3.5× per year while token prices collapse ~47% per quarter, creating a structural tension between rational firm-level investment and aggregate capital destruction. It further contends that the US dollar's global reach will be extended—not weakened—by AI-native stablecoin payment rails (the "Neo-Dollar"), and that only two sovereigns, America and China, will ultimately be capable of maintaining true frontier intelligence, with the state increasingly backstopping the entire AI economy through retirement savings, equity stakes, and political will. The piece concludes that durable wealth will be captured not by model makers but by owners of the "commanding heights"—the scarce distribution rails, data networks, and financial infrastructure through which intelligence diffuses to the world.

## TL;DR

- AI is repeating the railroad/Standard Oil pattern: the inventors rarely capture the wealth; it accrues to whoever owns the means of conducting the technology to the rest of the economy.
- Frontier model training costs (~3.5× per year) will outrun commercial returns, forcing the US sovereign to backstop the intelligence economy through retirement capital and equity stakes, making AI and the S&P 500 effectively the same line.
- The "Neo-Dollar"—dollar-pegged stablecoins riding global messaging/AI rails—will extend US monetary reach further than at any time since Bretton Woods, even as AI displaces hundreds of millions of workers worldwide.

## Key claims

- **Genesis capital destruction is systemic:** What is rational capital allocation for any individual hyperscaler or lab may constitute aggregate capital destruction industry-wide, because the economics of staying at the frontier create a coercive, arms-race logic that overrides normal capital-cycle discipline.
- **Token price collapse vs. training cost explosion:** Frontier model training costs are rising ~3.5× annually while the price of a unit of model output falls ~47% per quarter—the fastest cost decline of any transformative technology in history.
- **Hyperscaler cash flow crunch circa 2027:** Hyperscaler capex is growing at ~70% per year vs. ~23% for operating cash flow; the gap is projected to peak around 2027, requiring external financing that draws in pension funds, annuities, and retail private credit—making AI exposure systemic.
- **The Neo-Dollar as geopolitical instrument:** Dollar-pegged stablecoins delivered through AI-native global messaging apps will expand US dollar dominance at the individual (not just central-bank) level, creating a new form of monetary hegemony.
- **Sovereign bifurcation of AI:** Intelligence will split into commodity (open-weight, Chinese-floor-priced), commercial frontier (American labs), and a classified sovereign frontier held in air-gapped facilities—accessible only to the US and China as the two capable sovereigns.
- **China's open-weight strategy is deliberate:** By releasing capable open-weight models (e.g., Kimi K3) globally, China sets a commodity floor, compresses American lab margins, and exports its intelligence stack without needing Western-style capital returns, because it is not beholden to American 401(k) investors.
- **Distribution rails, not models, are the commanding heights:** As with railroads and Standard Oil pipelines, the durable returns of the AI age will accrue to owners of distribution networks, payment infrastructure, and data conduits—not to model builders.
- **K-shaped second act:** The first K (monetary—asset owners vs. wage earners post-QE) is largely complete; the second K (technological—AI owners vs. everyone else) is now beginning, with inflation and deflation arriving simultaneously and the Fed unable to brake it.

## Key passages

> "In the world to come, one either owns the rails or pays those who do. The power and wealth of this brave new world will be defined by the commanding heights."

> "The result was a miracle recovery for those who owned assets, and a slow strangulation for those who earned a wage."

> "Standard Oil was not broken up for finding oil. Standard Oil was destroyed for the sin of owning the means of moving it across the country."

> "In other words, what is rational capital allocation for a particular firm may be capital destruction in the aggregate. The ouroboros is no longer fed by ROIC, but by the coercive laws of competition."

> "The Neo-Dollar, that is to say the system of relations between USDT, its carriers, and its spread, will have far greater uses."

> "We invert the assumption that the AI trade is about selling intelligence. Instead, we believe that intelligence is the giveaway, a loss leader that draws in users and data and derives margins from being the conduit."

> "As intelligence becomes commoditized and widely available, value migrates toward the assets that remain scarce. By definition, value accrues to a much narrower stratum. Owned by the few, kept by the even fewer."
