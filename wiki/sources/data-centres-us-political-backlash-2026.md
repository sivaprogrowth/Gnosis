## Abstract

Public opposition to data centres in the United States has surged dramatically between 2025 and 2026, turning what was once a niche land-use question into a defining issue of the midterm election cycle. Governors who previously championed AI infrastructure — including those of Illinois, Texas, and Pennsylvania — have reversed course, imposing moratoriums, audits, and community-veto mechanisms as polls show nearly two-thirds of Americans now oppose data-centre construction locally. The backlash is rooted less in concerns about AI itself than in fears about electricity prices, water consumption, and traffic, many of which analysts say are overstated. Despite foreign-influence narratives pushed by some politicians, the opposition appears largely organic, and the article assesses whether financial incentives or political dealmaking can reverse it before investment simply migrates to more permissive states.

## TL;DR

- A dramatic political reversal has occurred: US governors and localities that once courted data centres are now banning or restricting them, driven by voter backlash ahead of the 2026 midterms.
- Local grievances about electricity bills, water use, and traffic dominate opposition — not AI policy itself — and many of those concerns are empirically overstated.
- Investment is likely to flow to more permissive states rather than stop, but rising costs and political friction are a real headwind for the industry.

## Key claims

- Opposition to local data-centre construction reached 63% in an *Economist*/YouGov poll from late August 2026, up from majority support just over a year prior.
- American counties considered 409 moratoriums or similar measures in the first eight months of 2026 — nearly seven times the total for all of the previous year.
- In more than half a dozen states, including virtually every midterm battleground, more than a fifth of counties have passed at least one moratorium.
- A Fox News poll found 75% of data-centre opponents cite local concerns (energy use, water bills, traffic) as their primary reason; only 11% point to AI itself.
- The Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory estimates data centres consumed 4.4% of US electricity in 2023 and could account for 9.5%–15.3% by 2030.
- The Electric Power Research Institute estimates that data-centre growth actually *reduced* average electricity prices by 6% between 2019 and 2024 by spreading fixed grid costs.
- An Echelon Insights poll found opposition could be reversed by informing respondents of a single local benefit (e.g., lower property taxes); a University of Virginia poll found a median acceptable payment of $10,000 per resident to support a local facility.
- [[companies/openai]] reported that a Chinese influence operation targeting data-centre sentiment generated "little or no observable engagement" among Americans, undermining claims that foreign actors are driving the backlash.
