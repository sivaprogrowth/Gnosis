## Abstract

This *Economist* graphic-detail piece tracks the measurable growth of AI-generated content across five fields—books, legal filings, academic papers, software apps, and music—using recent empirical research and platform data. It finds that AI has dramatically increased output volume in each domain since the release of ChatGPT-3.5 in November 2022, with AI tools enabling more participation (e.g., self-filed lawsuits, indie apps) but also flooding platforms with low-quality material. The article notes a persistent gap between people's revealed preferences (often indistinguishable from or even preferring AI output in blind tests) and their stated preferences (favouring the "human touch"). It concludes that by lowering the skills and effort required for creation, AI may ultimately lower the perceived value of all content.

## TL;DR

- AI-generated content has surged across books, law, academia, apps, and music since late 2022, with volume increases ranging from 2× to 7.5×.
- Blind tests show people often cannot distinguish AI from human output, yet AI-labelled content still receives worse reviews on platforms like Amazon.
- The sheer quantity of AI content is creating systemic strain—clogging courts, overwhelming peer-review, and potentially devaluing all content.

## Key claims

- Amazon e-book releases rose from ~100,000/month before November 2022 to ~300,000/month by end of 2025, with AI chatbots largely responsible for the increase (per Reimers & Waldfogel, Cornell/Minnesota).
- Civil lawsuits filed without a lawyer in America doubled to 41,000 between 2023 and 2025; 18% of self-filed complaints in 2026 contained AI-flagged language (per Shah & Levy, MIT/USC).
- Self-filed AI-assisted lawsuits have the same success rate as pre-chatbot pro-se cases, suggesting AI genuinely helps litigants win claims.
- ArXiv paper rejection rates more than doubled since early 2023; 57% of papers published in 2025 showed AI-influenced language, up from 12% in 2023.
- iOS App Store releases exceeded 100,000/month (up from under 50,000 the previous May), coinciding with the release of coding agents such as [[entities/claude-code]] and Codex.
- Deezer estimates 75,000 AI-generated songs are uploaded per day (up from 10,000 in January 2025), constituting 44% of all new tracks on the platform.
- 97% of Deezer survey respondents could not hear the difference between AI-generated and human-made music.

## Key passages

> "AI has lowered the skills and effort required for some work. That, in turn, might lower the value of all content."

> "In a sample of 1,600 complaints, 18% of those filed in 2026 included language flagged as AI-generated. These self-filed cases still have the same success rate as before the launch of chatbots, suggesting that AI is helping more people to win claims."

> "AI music now makes up a staggering 44% of all new tracks uploaded to the platform."

> "Similarly, blind tests have found that people often prefer AI-generated text to human writing. In an in-house experiment, our colleagues at *The Economist* struggled to pick which Bartleby column was written by our human columnist versus his AI agent."

> "But ask most people for their thoughts on AI content and they will probably say they prefer the human touch … Books that appear to be authored by AI have fewer and worse reviews on Amazon."
