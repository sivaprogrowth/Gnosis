## Abstract

This January 2023 *Economist* article surveys the competitive landscape among corporate AI research labs in the wake of [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]'s ChatGPT, which attracted one million users in five days after its November 2022 launch. It traces the historical arc of corporate R&D—from Bell Labs and Xerox PARC to the shift away from basic science—and argues that generative AI is reversing that trend by enabling rapid translation of research into products. The piece benchmarks ChatGPT against [[companies/google|Google]]'s LaMDA on maths, reading comprehension, and dating advice, finding no clear winner, and explains why AI knowledge diffuses so quickly across labs. It concludes that the race's outcome will hinge on organisational structure, early-mover data advantages, and whether scale continues to confer supremacy.

## TL;DR

- No single AI lab holds an insurmountable lead; leading researchers estimate rivals are at most two to six months apart.
- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]'s early-mover advantage may compound through user data and "reinforcement learning from human feedback," but big tech and Chinese state-backed labs are catching up fast.
- Scale has dominated generative AI so far, but techniques to fine-tune smaller models may erode big tech's compute advantage over time.

## Key claims

- ChatGPT reached 1 million users within five days of its November 2022 launch, making it one of the fastest consumer-product launches in history.
- Microsoft committed $10 billion to [[companies/openai|OpenAI]] and plans to embed ChatGPT-like capabilities across its software product suite.
- [[companies/baidu|Baidu]] reportedly planned to integrate a chatbot into its search engine by March 2023, signalling broad competitive pressure in China.
- Chinese labs lead the computer-vision subdiscipline, with the top five computer-vision teams in the world reportedly all Chinese; BAAI's Wu Dao 2.0 was claimed to be the world's largest natural-language model at the time.
- AI knowledge diffuses rapidly because researchers publish freely, present at conferences, and move between organisations—no lab is ahead by "more than two to six months," according to [[companies/facebook|Meta]]'s Yann LeCun.
- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]]'s lean structure and few revenue streams to protect may give it more latitude to release products publicly, generating user data that reinforces model quality via RLHF.
- Epoch, a non-profit research institute, estimated that large language models could exhaust high-quality internet text by 2026 at current training rates.
- Generative-AI startups collectively raised $2.7 billion across 110 deals in 2022, suggesting venture capitalists believe value will not accrue solely to large incumbents.

## Key passages

> "In five days after its unveiling in November the artificially intelligent chatbot, created by a startup called OpenAI, drew 1m users, making it one of the fastest consumer-product launches in history."

> "Reckons Yann LeCun, Meta's top AI boffin, 'Nobody is ahead of anybody else by more than two to six months.'"

> "OpenAI, a small firm with few revenue streams to protect, may find itself with more latitude than rivals to release products to the public. That in turn is generating tonnes of user data that could make its models better ('reinforcement learning from human feedback', if you must know)—and thus attract more users."

> "Epoch, a non-profit research institute, estimates that at current rates, big language models will run out of high-quality text on the internet by 2026 (though other less-tapped formats, like video, will remain abundant for a while)."

> "As Mr Ha of Stability AI points out, there are ways to fine-tune a model to a specific task that 'dramatically reduce the need to scale up'. And novel methods to do more with less are being developed all the time."

> "The capital flowing into generative-AI startups, which last year collectively raised $2.7bn in 110 deals, suggests that venture capitalists are betting that not all the value will be captured by big tech."
