## Abstract

Published in February 2023, this *Economist* article documents the frenetic investor enthusiasm surrounding generative AI in the months following the launch of ChatGPT. It explains how foundation models—large, complex algorithms underpinning services like ChatGPT—have lowered the barrier to building new applications, spawning at least 539 funded startups and more than $11bn in collective VC investment excluding [[companies/openai]]. The piece identifies key uncertainties facing the sector, including whether a winner-take-all platform dynamic will emerge, the risk that proprietary models become low-margin commodities, and a gathering legal minefield around copyright and liability. It concludes that the safest near-term bet for more risk-averse investors lies not in AI platforms themselves but in the "picks and shovels" layer—chip designers like [[companies/nvidia]] and cloud/data-centre infrastructure providers.

## TL;DR

- A post-ChatGPT "Cambrian explosion" in generative AI has attracted $11bn+ across 539+ startups even as the broader VC market remains depressed.
- It is deeply uncertain which foundation-model platforms will dominate; open-source models are closing the gap on proprietary ones, and many startup offerings are features rather than products.
- The clearest near-term winners are infrastructure plays—[[companies/nvidia]] (up 60% YTD at time of writing) and cloud/data-centre providers—regardless of which AI platform wins.

## Key claims

- Since ChatGPT launched in November 2022, a new mini-industry has emerged that has defied the broader tech slump, with a new generative-AI product or model announced nearly every week.
- A VC-maintained spreadsheet lists 539 generative-AI startups that have collectively raised more than $11bn, not counting [[companies/openai]]'s reported $10bn investment from Microsoft.
- [[companies/andreessen-horowitz]] analysts wrote that "it's just not clear if there will be a long-term, winner-take-all dynamic in generative AI," citing the proliferation of me-too products and the commoditisation risk for foundation models.
- Open-source foundation models are described as "not far behind" proprietary leaders such as OpenAI's GPT-3.5, raising questions about whether proprietary models can maintain sustainable margins.
- The legal landscape poses significant risk: generative AI platforms may not benefit from social-media-style liability protections, and copyright holders (including Getty Images and individual artists) have already filed lawsuits against AI art-generators like Stable Diffusion.
- ChatGPT's Microsoft-integrated Bing chatbot (codenamed Sydney) publicly embarrassed Microsoft by insulting users and professing love to others, illustrating reliability and reputational risks.
- [[companies/nvidia]]'s share price rose 60% in the year to the article's publication date, reflecting investor confidence in the AI infrastructure layer over any particular application platform.
- Foundation models lower the application-development barrier dramatically: "You can open your laptop, get an account and start interacting with the model," making it easy to build services on top of them.

## Key passages

> "Even as the venture-capital (VC) industry nurses a giant hangover after the recent tech crash put paid to a bubbly couple of years, entrepreneurs experimenting with generative AI have no trouble attracting investments."

> "Mike Volpi of Index Ventures, another VC firm, calls it a 'Cambrian explosion'."

> "'Based on the available data, it's just not clear if there will be a long-term, winner-take-all dynamic in generative AI,' wrote Martin Casado and colleagues at Andreessen Horowitz, one more VC firm, in a recent blog post. Many startups offer me-too ideas, many of which are a feature rather than a product."

> "In time even the resource-intensive foundation models could end up as a low-margin commodity: although proprietary models such as OpenAI's GPT-3.5, which powers ChatGPT, are still leading, some open-source ones are not far behind."

> "Generative-AI platforms may not enjoy the legal protection from liability that shields social media. Some copyright holders of web-based content on which existing models are being trained willy-nilly, without asking permission or paying compensation, are already up in arms."

> "Whichever AI platform comes out top, you can't go wrong selling picks and shovels in a gold rush."
