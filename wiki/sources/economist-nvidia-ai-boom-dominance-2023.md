## Abstract

This *Economist* leaders piece examines [[companies/nvidia|Nvidia]]'s extraordinary rise on the back of the AI boom, arguing that the company's dominance in specialist AI chips, high-performance networking, and software ecosystems has placed it in a structurally enviable position analogous to [[companies/microsoft]] and Apple at the peaks of their respective platform waves. The article notes that Nvidia holds over 80% of the specialist AI chip market and 78% of the AI networking market, underpinned by its CUDA software platform and a deliberate strategy of cultivating developer ecosystems. However, it cautions that lasting dominance is not assured, citing competitive threats from chipmakers such as [[companies/amd]], cloud-computing giants [[companies/amazon]] and Alphabet designing in-house chips, open-source software eroding CUDA's lock-in, and government regulation—including US export restrictions to China—as credible risks. The piece concludes that Nvidia's integrated bundle of chips, networking, and software gives it a durable near-term advantage, even if the longer-run competitive picture is uncertain.

## TL;DR

- [[companies/nvidia]] commands ~80% of the specialist AI chip market and ~78% of AI networking, locked in by its CUDA software ecosystem—a rare triple-layer moat.
- Near-term prospects remain strong, but cloud hyperscalers ([[companies/amazon]], Alphabet) developing their own AI chips represent the most serious long-term threat.
- Regulation and US export controls on chips to China have already dented Nvidia's sales and could crimp future growth.

## Key claims

- Nvidia's market capitalisation briefly crossed $1 trillion in late May 2023, driven by AI-related demand.
- The company holds over 80% of the market for specialist AI chips and 78% of the high-performance AI networking market.
- Nvidia's 2019 acquisition of Mellanox was central to cementing its networking supremacy.
- CUDA, Nvidia's AI software platform, creates strong developer lock-in by running exclusively on Nvidia hardware; early free access to researchers deepened this advantage.
- [[companies/amd]], Intel, and startups are actively targeting both Nvidia's chip and networking businesses.
- Open-source and proprietary software alternatives to CUDA are under active development by rivals.
- US government export restrictions on high-performance chips to China dented Nvidia's revenues in Q3 2022.
- The article draws an explicit parallel between Nvidia's integrated ecosystem strategy and those of [[companies/microsoft]] and Apple during their respective platform-dominance phases.

## Key passages

> "Nvidia is in an enviable position. Its core business is designing high-performance chips. At first it sold these to video-game enthusiasts. The chips were also highly efficient at training AI models, and a new, booming market emerged."

> "Today it holds over 80% of the market in specialist AI chips."

> "CUDA, its AI platform, is popular with programmers and runs only on the company's chips. By, for instance, giving free access to its chips and software to some AI researchers, the firm focused on encouraging developers to use its software long before its competitors set out to woo them."

> "The biggest challenge, though, may come from Nvidia's own customers. The cloud-computing arms of both Amazon and Alphabet are designing their own AI-tailored chips. Both have the scale and the deep pockets to become fearsome rivals."

> "Nvidia's chief advantage lies in its ability to package these up and create an attractive ecosystem. That sounds a lot like Microsoft and Apple."
