## Abstract

This *New Yorker* piece is a first-person satirical monologue written in the voice of Elon Musk, announcing an imaginary "Tesla Body" product that applies Silicon Valley product-launch rhetoric to the human body itself. The piece skewers Musk's techno-solutionism, his public comments on reproduction and population growth, his self-aggrandisement, and the subscription-service business model by pushing each conceit to absurdist extremes. Through deadpan send-ups of features like self-walking, mandatory *Rick and Morty* eye content, protein-powder nutrition, and embedded pre-fertilised "Elon embryos," the author lampoons both Musk's stated worldview and the broader trend of tech billionaires positioning themselves as redesigners of fundamental human experience. The satire also targets gender politics, wealth inequality (accessories priced at hundreds or thousands of dollars), and Musk's public comments about being a prolific father.

## TL;DR

- A *New Yorker* satirical piece mocks Elon Musk by imagining him pitching a "Tesla Body" that replaces human biology with subscription-gated, Musk-branded hardware and services.
- The piece lampoons techno-solutionism, billionaire narcissism, and predatory SaaS pricing by applying them literally to the human body.
- Each satirical feature (self-walking, mandatory streaming content for eyes, pre-loaded Musk embryos) corresponds to a real Musk public stance or business behaviour.

## Key claims

- The article frames Musk's public persona as one that positions him as the singular arbiter of human improvement, parodied through him declaring himself the only person worthy of having a penis.
- Musk's stated concern about population decline and his own large family are satirised via a system in which he holds a monopoly on all human sperm and reproduction requires submitting an application to him.
- The subscription-and-upsell model of [[companies/spacex]] and Tesla is mocked through absurdly priced add-ons: $595 per ski pole for turning, a $3,095 alarm clock, and $9,415/month roadside assistance that is legally mandatory.
- The piece targets Musk's self-identification as a feminist by having the narrator declare his only concession to women is cargo-pant-style skin pockets.
- Techno-solutionism is satirised through "solutions" (self-walking, protein-powder nutrition) that are more restrictive and dangerous than the problems they replace.
- The piece mocks Musk's fondness for brand ecosystems ([[companies/spacex]], Tesla) by imagining a closed body hardware/software stack with no interoperability.
- Size and exclusivity gates ("fits women's sizes 0–8 and any man shorter than me") satirise how tech products nominally marketed as universal in practice exclude most people.

## Key passages

> My name is Elon Musk, and I am the C.E.O. of Tesla and the father of approximately one per cent of the current population of Earth and a hundred per cent of the future population of Mars.

> You will simply set a speed for your feet, and they will walk forward forever until your Body stops when it encounters a hard surface.

> The Roadside Assistance service is nine thousand four hundred and fifteen dollars per month. If you don't buy it, you go to jail.

> I'm going to say what I'm sure you're already thinking: no one should have a penis but me. A penis is a luxury and a privilege that only I have proved myself worthy of.

> I will therefore embed pre-fertilized Elon embryos in every Tesla Body. All you have to do is take care of your Body, and, one night, a tiny Elon or Elonia will pop out of its own little door!

> The Body will be on the market soon, and it will be available to almost everyone. It fits women's sizes 0-8 and any man shorter than me. I am the tallest man in the world!
