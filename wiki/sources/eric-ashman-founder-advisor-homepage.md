## Abstract

Eric Ashman's personal advisory website presents him as a consultant helping startup founders navigate the challenges of growth, scaling, and enterprise value creation. The site offers three core service lines — a Startup Strategy Sprint, a Pivot to Profitability® Accelerator, and general Advisory Services — aimed at founders facing near-term milestone pressure or uncertain paths to the next funding round. A blog section covers recurring startup themes including liquidation preferences, product-market fit, marketing efficiency, and media literacy for founders. Case studies reference past work with companies such as Group Nine, Thrillist/JackThreads, and Text 100. The site also promotes a free ebook on the five essential principles of raising venture capital.

## TL;DR

- Eric Ashman is a founder-facing advisor offering structured frameworks for startup strategy, profitability pivots, and runway optimisation.
- His content emphasises financial literacy (cap tables, liquidation preferences), evidence-based decision-making, and scepticism of startup media narratives.
- Case studies span digital media (Group Nine, Thrillist) and PR (Text 100), suggesting a background in consumer internet and media-adjacent startups.

## Key claims

- Liquidation preferences, not headline valuations, determine how cash is actually distributed at exit — founders must understand their cap table's waterfall.
- Product-market fit should be evaluated across three dimensions: desirability, feasibility, and viability, with SMART Goals set against each.
- The startup press optimises for clicks on headlines, making selective storytelling a distortion risk for founders benchmarking their own progress.
- Marketing budget inflation is frequently caused by a small number of identifiable and correctable mistakes tied to poor efficiency metrics.
- A culture of innovation requires institutionalising experimentation and explicitly embracing failure as a path to building great products and enterprise value.
- The "Pivot to Profitability® Accelerator" is positioned for founders when "chasing growth at all costs is no longer sustainable" and the path to the next round is unclear.
- Data-driven leadership advice is anchored by the Jim Barksdale (former Netscape CEO) maxim: "If we have data, let's look at the data. If all we have are opinions, let's go with mine."

## Key passages

> Helping founders navigate the challenges of growth, scaling and building value.
