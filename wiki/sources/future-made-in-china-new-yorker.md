## Abstract

This long-form *New Yorker* piece surveys the accelerating technological and geopolitical rivalry between China and the United States, arguing that Beijing's decades of state-directed industrial policy, scientific investment, and AI deployment are converging at the precise moment that Washington is retreating from research funding, international alliances, and regulatory coherence. Drawing on interviews with investors, scientists, factory managers, military officers, and diplomats in Beijing, the article traces how China has moved from imitating Western technology to leading in electric vehicles, robotics, biotech, solar energy, and AI—while simultaneously grappling with a property crash, demographic collapse, and authoritarian overreach. The piece contends that the likely outcome of the rivalry is not a clean Chinese victory but a period of global "un-order," and closes with the warning that whichever country can most honestly face its own problems—rather than engineering alternate realities—holds the greater long-term advantage.

## TL;DR

- China's state-led AI and industrial policy is producing real technological leads in EVs, robotics, biotech, and AI models, not just propaganda.
- Trump-era cuts to science funding, alliances, and regulatory credibility are accelerating a brain drain to China and an erosion of U.S. soft power.
- Neither superpower is positioned to cleanly dominate; the more likely outcome is global "un-order"—a Hobbesian scramble among all major powers.

## Key claims

- China now produces at least 70% of the world's drones, EVs, lithium-ion batteries, and solar cells, and deploys more industrial robots than the rest of the world combined.
- The Chinese startup DeepSeek released an AI model in 2024 that stunned Silicon Valley by performing competitively at a fraction of the cost of American equivalents.
- Chinese biotech firms now generate nearly a third of the world's new drug candidates, and a Chinese firm's experimental cancer drug outperformed Merck's Keytruda in 2024.
- Nearly 20,000 scientists of Chinese descent left the U.S. between 2010 and 2021; Trump-era China Initiative prosecutions and subsequent funding cuts have accelerated the exodus.
- China's one-child policy produced a fertility rate of approximately one birth per woman—the lowest since records began in 1949—creating a demographic crisis that no incentive program has reversed.
- China's property market collapse left an estimated 90 million empty homes and triggered a swing in public sentiment "from optimism to pessimism," with only ~28% of citizens expecting living standards to improve.
- The article argues that the successor to the American-led international order is less likely to be a Chinese order than a period of "un-order," as every country hedges, arms, subsidizes, and hoards.
- AI surveillance tools in China—emotion-recognition kiosks, student "alert" systems, and remote vital-sign scanners—are being marketed commercially and exported globally via the Belt and Road Initiative.
