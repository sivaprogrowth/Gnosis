## Abstract

This McKinsey report, drawn from the 2026 B2B Pulse Survey of nearly 4,000 buyers and sellers across 13 countries plus in-depth executive interviews, argues that AI adoption in B2B sales is widespread but value capture remains rare because companies apply AI to isolated tasks rather than rewiring end-to-end commercial workflows. The authors introduce five "impact journeys"—market opportunity mapping, go-to-market model design, account management, pricing, and sales enablement—as the building blocks of a new commercial operating model powered by [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]]. Growth champions differentiate themselves by being three times more likely to have increased AI investment by double digits and by fundamentally redesigning workflows rather than layering tools on top of existing complexity. The report prescribes four organisational shifts—a business-led road map, a redesigned talent and operating model, a data and technology spine, and a rigorous adoption and scaling discipline—to convert agentic AI from experimentation into durable margin improvement.

## TL;DR

- Most B2B companies have deployed AI but fewer than 10% have scaled it in any given function; value comes from rewiring end-to-end "impact journeys," not adding point tools.
- Growth champions use [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]] across five commercial workflows—opportunity mapping, GTM model, account management, pricing, and sales enablement—to compound gains rather than keep them isolated.
- Organisational change (talent redesign, data governance, and change management) is as critical as the technology; for every $1 spent deploying AI, companies may need to spend $3 on change management.

## Key claims

- High-growth B2B companies are **three times more likely** to have increased AI investment by double digits YoY (71% vs. 25% of peers).
- Fewer than **10% of organisations** have scaled AI in any given function, creating a paradox of widespread adoption but limited value capture.
- Financial-services companies that rewired prospecting and relationship-management workflows with agentic AI achieved **3–15% higher revenues per relationship manager** and **20–40% lower cost-to-serve ratios**.
- AI-powered next-best-experience programmes have reported **5–8% revenue uplift** and **20–30% lower cost-to-serve ratios** for companies that implemented them.
- Implementing agentic AI in even one of the five impact journeys can free up an additional **10% of seller time**.
- A B2B services company that used AI for smart pricing saw a **10% uplift in earnings** by optimising discount variance rather than simply raising prices.
- AI-driven sales coaching at a large construction company produced a **4.5 percentage point increase in conversion** among reps who used the tool.
- Inconsistent information across teams is now the **number-one reason buyers switch suppliers**, ahead of inability to access knowledgeable representatives.
- **65–85% of B2B pricing executives** expect to adopt gen AI or agentic AI in pricing within one to three years, up from just 10–30% today.

## Key passages

> "What sets growth champions—those growing faster than their peers—apart is their ambition to rewire key workflows end to end. High-growth companies are three times more likely to have increased AI investment by double digits in 2026 year over year—71 percent versus 25 percent."

> "AI is a business transformation topic, not a technical initiative. AI is about bringing people along and fostering a culture of experimentation. Otherwise, you still conduct business the old ways with just a new gadget."
> — Monique Buch, chief commercial officer at [[companies/covestro]]

> "For salespeople to be effective in their roles, they're going to have to manage agents. The role of the account executive is still building relationships, driving timelines, and getting deals across the line, but increasingly it's going to be a person working with an agent at every step."
> — Shane Paladin, chief customer and revenue officer at [[companies/equinix]]

> "Our research suggests that for every $1 spent deploying AI, companies may need to spend $3 on change management. Yet most organizations invert this ratio."

> "The five impact journeys in this report are not a menu of use cases. They are the building blocks of a new operating model: identifying the right opportunities, growing accounts with better offers, improving go-to-market execution, pricing more effectively, and helping more sellers perform like the best."

> "AI is also helping us understand which products are responsive to sales rep promotion versus those that would do just as well via marketing campaigns. That means our reps are becoming knowledgeable about eight to ten key products versus having limited knowledge of 65 different ones."
> — Jason McKinney, SVP of commercial sales at Dechra
