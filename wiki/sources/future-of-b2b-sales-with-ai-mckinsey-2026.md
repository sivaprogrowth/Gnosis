## Abstract

Published by McKinsey's Growth, Marketing and Sales Practice in July 2026, this report draws on a 2026 B2B Pulse Survey of nearly 4,000 buyers and sellers across 13 countries to argue that most B2B companies are adopting AI without capturing material value from it. The central thesis is that breakout growth requires "rewiring" core commercial workflows—called "impact journeys"—end-to-end around [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]], rather than layering point solutions on top of existing processes. The report identifies five priority impact journeys (market opportunity mapping, GTM model design, account management, pricing, and sales enablement) and outlines four organisational shifts needed to make the transformation stick. Failure to make these structural changes, the authors argue, means AI merely automates existing complexity rather than creating new value.

## TL;DR

- High-growth B2B companies are 3× more likely to have increased AI investment by double digits YoY, and they differentiate by redesigning workflows end-to-end around agentic AI—not just deploying enterprise tools.
- Five "commercial impact journeys" (reach the right opportunities, GTM model, account management, pricing, and sales enablement) are the highest-value targets for agentic rewiring.
- Capturing value requires four organisational shifts: a business-led road map, a redesigned talent and operating model, a trusted data/technology spine, and rigorous adoption and scaling discipline.

## Key claims

- Fewer than 10% of organisations have scaled AI in any given function, creating a paradox: adoption is widespread but value capture is limited.
- Financial services firms that rewired prospecting and relationship-management workflows with agentic AI achieved 3–15% higher revenues per relationship manager and 20–40% lower cost-to-serve ratios.
- AI-powered next-best-experience programmes have delivered 5–8% revenue uplift and 20–30% lower cost-to-serve ratios in GTM orchestration deployments.
- In the 2026 B2B Pulse Survey, inconsistent information across teams is now the number-one reason buyers switch suppliers, ahead of inability to access knowledgeable representatives.
- 65–85% of B2B pricing executives expect to adopt gen AI or agentic AI in pricing over the next one to three years, up from just 10–30% today.
- Implementing agentic AI in even one of the five impact journeys can free up an additional 10% of seller time.
- A construction company deploying an AI coaching platform achieved a 4.5 percentage point increase in conversion for reps who used the tool; sellers—not buyers—initiated discount conversations 70% of the time, and conversion was 40% lower when reps led with brand rather than buyer-need discovery.
- A B2B services company that applied AI-driven smart pricing saw a 10% uplift in earnings by optimising discount variance across hundreds of customer and deal parameters.

## Key passages

> "What sets growth champions—those growing faster than their peers—apart is their ambition to rewire key workflows end to end. High-growth companies are three times more likely to have increased AI investment by double digits in 2026 year over year—71 percent versus 25 percent."

> "This is the current AI paradox in B2B sales. Adoption is increasingly widespread, but value capture is limited. Most companies have focused on rolling out enterprise-wide gen AI tools. These tools can improve employee productivity, but their economic impact is often diffuse. The bigger prize comes from applying agentic AI to specific workflows—in this case, the end-to-end sales process—to create measurable value."

> "AI is a business transformation topic, not a technical initiative. AI is about bringing people along and fostering a culture of experimentation. Otherwise, you still conduct business the old ways with just a new gadget." — Monique Buch, Chief Commercial Officer, Covestro

> "For salespeople to be effective in their roles, they're going to have to manage agents. The role of the account executive is still building relationships, driving timelines, and getting deals across the line, but increasingly it's going to be a person working with an agent at every step." — Shane Paladin, Chief Customer and Revenue Officer, Equinix

> "We're reimagining the role of the sales manager from performance inspector to sales coach. Historically, managers spent much of their time reviewing pipeline reports, analyzing performance metrics, and manually scoring a small sample of sales calls. Today, AI can listen to every customer interaction, evaluate it against both sales skill and product knowledge models, and automatically surface coaching opportunities at both the individual and organizational level." — VP of Revenue Operations, global B2B technology company

> "AI reduces administrative tasks for territory managers so they can instead focus on learning what their customers really need... AI is also helping us understand which products are responsive to sales rep promotion versus those that would do just as well via marketing campaigns." — Jason McKinney, SVP Commercial Sales, Dechra
