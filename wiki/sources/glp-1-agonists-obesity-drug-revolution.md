## Abstract

This *Economist* briefing argues that a new class of injectable drugs — GLP-1 agonists, principally semaglutide (Wegovy/Ozempic) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro) — represent a genuine breakthrough in the treatment of obesity, a condition now affecting over a billion people and projected to cost the global economy $4 trillion annually by 2035. The article explains the biological and evolutionary reasons conventional dieting fails for most people, traces the chequered history of weight-loss pharmacology, and details how GLP-1 agonists achieve average weight losses of 15–20% in clinical trials by mimicking satiety hormones and acting on the brain's hunger centre. It weighs the drugs' significant commercial potential — analysts project a $150bn-plus market by 2031 — against drawbacks including side-effects, lifetime dependency, and high costs that currently limit access. If insurers and governments can negotiate pricing, widespread adoption could save hundreds of billions annually in avoided obesity-related healthcare and lost productivity.

## TL;DR

- GLP-1 agonist drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide) produce 15–20% average body-weight loss in trials, far exceeding anything diet or exercise achieves for most people.
- Obesity is projected to affect half of all people over five by 2035, costing $4 trillion/year — making effective treatment one of the largest healthcare opportunities in history.
- The drugs require lifelong use and currently cost ~$1,300/month in the US, but a $150bn+ market is expected by 2031 as competition and generics eventually reduce prices.

## Key claims

- The [[World Economic Forum|World Obesity Federation]] projects that 4 billion people — half of everyone over five — will be overweight or obese by 2035, at an annual economic cost of $4 trillion (roughly 2.9% of global GDP).
- GLP-1 agonists were originally developed to treat diabetes and work by mimicking post-meal hormones, slowing gastric emptying, acting on the hypothalamus, and promoting fat breakdown.
- Novo Nordisk's semaglutide (Wegovy) achieved an average 15% body-weight loss at a 2.4mg weekly dose over 68 weeks; Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro) achieved ~20% in trials.
- Analysts at Jefferies project the GLP-1 agonist market will exceed $150bn by 2031, comparable to the entire global oncology drug market.
- Biology, not willpower, explains most obesity: evolutionary adaptations slow metabolism when calories are reduced, raise hunger hormone ghrelin, and cause the body to "remember" and fight to regain lost weight.
- Obesity carries a 45–65% heritable genetic component, and environmental factors — ultra-processed food abundance, sedentary work — compound genetic predisposition.
- Discontinuing the drugs causes patients to regain roughly two-thirds of lost weight within a year, making lifelong treatment likely necessary, analogous to statins for cholesterol.
- GLP-1 agonists taken for diabetes have already reduced strokes and heart attacks by 14%, all-cause mortality by 12%, and hospitalisations for heart problems by 11% in observed cohorts.
