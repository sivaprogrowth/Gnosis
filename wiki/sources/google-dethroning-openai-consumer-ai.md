## Abstract

Reporting from Google's annual I/O developer conference, *The Economist* argues that Google is positioned to overtake [[entities/chatgpt|OpenAI's ChatGPT]] as the dominant force in consumer AI. Google unveiled a new suite of AI agents powered by its Gemini 3.5 Flash model — including background-task agents and in-Search "information agents" — that bring agentic AI to the combined 3bn+ users of [[entities/google-search|Google Search]] and the [[entities/gemini|Gemini app]]. The piece also interrogates the financial strain this compute-intensive ambition places on Alphabet, whose capital expenditure is projected at $190bn in 2026, and examines three monetisation levers — efficiency gains, usage caps, and advertising — that Google is exploring to sustain the push.

## TL;DR

- Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash and new agent lineup give it a credible shot at surpassing OpenAI in consumer AI reach, leveraging its 3bn+ Search users.
- Alphabet's market cap has nearly hit $5trn, but AI token consumption (3.2 quadrillion/month) is driving capex to eye-watering levels.
- Google is quietly introducing usage caps and deeper ad integration into AI responses to offset ballooning compute costs.

## Key claims

- Google's [[entities/gemini|Gemini]] app already reaches 900 million monthly users; Google Search reaches more than 3 billion, giving Google an unmatched distribution advantage over OpenAI.
- The Gemini 3.5 Flash model is described by Google as four times faster than other frontier models, directly challenging OpenAI's and Anthropic's coding and consumer agents.
- A new agent called **Gemini Spark** can perform background tasks — such as scanning emails or organising group trips — even when the user's device is idle.
- "Information agents" embedded in [[entities/google-search|Google Search]] will monitor ongoing events (sports, shopping sales, markets) on a user's behalf.
- After Google launched Gemini 3 in November 2025, OpenAI's Sam Altman issued an internal "Code Red" to accelerate ChatGPT improvements, signalling competitive alarm.
- Alphabet's token consumption has risen from 480 trillion to 3.2 quadrillion per month year-on-year, driving capex up sixfold versus four years ago (to ~$190bn in 2026).
- Google is testing three monetisation strategies for its AI services: (1) improving per-token efficiency, (2) imposing usage caps (with higher limits for subscribers), and (3) inserting ads alongside AI responses in Search and launching AI product explainers next to ads.
- Gemini subscribers were notified post-event that usage caps would apply — suggesting Google is moving toward tiered consumption models even as it broadens access.

## Key passages

> "It now looks as though it may steal the consumer AI crown from OpenAI, maker of ChatGPT, as well."

> "Some will appear in the Gemini app, used by 900m people every month. Others will be embedded directly in Google Search, used by more than 3bn. The company is, in sum, bringing agents to the masses."

> "The release of Gemini 3.5 Flash, which Google says is four times faster than other frontier models, and the new suite of agents is likely to raise fresh questions about what OpenAI is doing with its flagship chatbot."

> "The number of tokens… consumed by its services has risen to 3.2 quadrillion a month, up from 480trn a year ago. Each token requires computing power, and therefore money, to generate, which is why Google's capital expenditure this year will be up to $190bn, six times as much as four years ago."

> "Google reckons that the greater detail in AI queries will appeal to marketers. Although it has not yet incorporated ads into its Gemini app, it has been interspersing them into the AI responses its search service now spits out."

> "Mr Pichai noted at the conference that some companies are 'already blowing through their annual token budgets—and it's only May.' Consumers are not 'tokenmaxxing' to anything like the same extent."
