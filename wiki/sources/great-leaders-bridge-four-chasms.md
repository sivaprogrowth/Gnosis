## Abstract

Writing in *Livemint*, Raghu Raman — former CEO of the National Intelligence Grid and author of *Everyman's War* — distils a framework for organizational success drawn from an encounter with an iconic corporate leader. The framework identifies four "chasms" that every organization must bridge regardless of size or industry: Confusion to Clarity, Competence to Capability, Concern to Confidence, and Criticism to Celebration. Raman argues that visionary leaders are distinguished not by their ability to formulate strategy but by their ability to simplify, institutionalize, and inspire — translating lofty intent into executable action. The piece draws on military analogies and Gallup research to ground each chasm in observable organizational failure modes, making the case that leadership is ultimately about inspiration, not fault-finding.

## TL;DR

- Great leaders bridge four structural chasms: Confusion→Clarity, Competence→Capability, Concern→Confidence, Criticism→Celebration.
- Strategy fails not at conception but at translation — when the "why" is never communicated to those who must execute the last 100 metres.
- A leader's primary job is to inspire; competence, strategy, and problem-solving can be outsourced, but inspiration cannot.

## Key claims

- **Confusion to Clarity**: Gallup surveys identify lack of clarity as a primary driver of employee disengagement; strategic intent routinely gets lost in translation between senior leaders and frontline executors.
- Vague, jargon-laden strategy documents are common because translating vision into simple, non-jargonized steps is laborious and therefore often skipped.
- **Competence to Capability**: Elaborate multi-panel recruitment processes tend to produce homogenous, stereotypical hires by surgically removing gut-feel, chemistry, and ideological alignment from selection.
- Toxic organizational politics erodes positive energy and drives out talented people who refuse to participate; removing it is the direct responsibility of strategic leadership.
- **Concern to Confidence**: Leaders must convert healthy concern into confidence in their people's training, values, and judgment — including their moral courage to deviate from SOPs when necessary.
- **Criticism to Celebration**: Reviews are too often conducted as inquisitions; fault-finding is the auditor's job, not the leader's.
- A leader's irreplaceable responsibility is to inspire and institutionalize that inspiration across the organization; everything else can be sourced externally.

## Key passages

> "No matter how brilliant the general's plan may be, it is the havildar who has to execute the last 100 metres. If the havildar doesn't get it, the brilliance of the plan is irrelevant."

> "Fire in the belly, ideological and psychological alignment, chemistry, gut-feel, intuition, etc, are surgically removed from the hiring process. But often, these are the very aspects of leadership and decision making needed at strategic levels."

> "Removing this venom from teams is the direct responsibility of the strategic leadership. However many don't, because it is distasteful and confrontational."

> "If a platoon-level attack is underway, the general must retain composure because he is confident of the ability of leaders in the chain of command to handle the operation. Any perturbation displayed by him could have undermined that chain of command."

> "A leader's primary job is to inspire. She may be a great problem solver, brilliant strategist or a highly competent professional. All that is naught if she cannot inspire and institutionalize that inspiration, because strategy, competence and solutions can be outsourced but inspiring the organisation is the responsibility of its leaders."
