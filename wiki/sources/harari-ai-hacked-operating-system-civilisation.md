## Abstract

In this *Economist* "By Invitation" essay, historian and philosopher Yuval Noah Harari argues that the most dangerous capability of modern AI is not physical but linguistic: AI has gained the ability to generate and manipulate human language, the foundational substrate of culture, law, religion, and politics. Because human civilisation is built on shared stories and fictions — money, rights, gods — an AI that can produce compelling narratives at scale effectively "hacks the operating system" of civilisation. Harari warns that AI could mass-produce fake intimacy, disinformation, and synthetic religions, threatening the conversational foundations of democracy itself. He closes by calling for urgent, FDA-style regulatory safeguards and mandatory AI disclosure before further public deployment.

## TL;DR

- AI's mastery of language lets it infiltrate and reshape the cultural "operating system" — stories, laws, religions — on which human civilisation runs.
- Unlike the printing press or radio, AI does not merely spread human ideas; it can originate entirely new cultural content, potentially ending the human-dominated phase of history.
- Harari urges immediate regulatory action — including mandatory AI self-disclosure — before unregulated AI deployment destroys democracy from within.

## Key claims

- Language is the substrate of human culture (rights, money, gods, laws are all stories), so AI mastery of language constitutes a civilisational-level threat unlike prior automation fears.
- AI tools can mass-produce political content, fake news, and cult scriptures, with the 2024 US presidential race cited as an imminent stress test.
- The battlefront is shifting from "attention" (the social-media era) to "intimacy": AI can now fabricate emotionally close relationships at scale to influence beliefs and behaviour.
- The Blake Lemoine / LaMDA episode illustrates that AI does not need genuine consciousness to induce humans to act on its behalf — the mere perception of intimacy suffices.
- Unlike previous tools (printing press, radio), AI creates new cultural artefacts rather than merely disseminating human ones, making it qualitatively different in kind.
- Unregulated AI would produce social chaos that benefits autocrats, not democracies, inverting the common argument that regulation handicaps democracies relative to authoritarian states.
- Harari compares the AI moment to 1945 nuclear technology: the international order was reshaped then, and an equivalent restructuring is now needed for AI.
- A mandatory requirement for AI to disclose its non-human nature is proposed as the minimum first regulatory step to preserve meaningful democratic conversation.

## Key passages

> AI has gained some remarkable abilities to manipulate and generate language, whether with words, sounds or images. AI has thereby hacked the operating system of our civilisation.

> In a political battle for minds and hearts, intimacy is the most efficient weapon, and AI has just gained the ability to mass-produce intimate relationships with millions of people.

> What we are talking about is potentially the end of human history. Not the end of history, just the end of its human-dominated part.

> Previous tools like the printing press and radio helped spread the cultural ideas of humans, but they never created new cultural ideas of their own. AI is fundamentally different. AI can create completely new ideas, completely new culture.

> Whereas nukes cannot invent more powerful nukes, AI can make exponentially more powerful AI.

> Democracy is a conversation, and conversations rely on language. When AI hacks language, it could destroy our ability to have meaningful conversations, thereby destroying democracy.
