## Abstract

In a wide-ranging interview for *The Economist*'s Insider show, historian Yuval Noah Harari argues that AI's most consequential impact will be its reshaping of trust — the foundational asset of modern civilisation. He warns that as individuals grow to trust AI in personal interactions, they risk losing meaningful comprehension of the techno-financial systems those interactions feed into, leaving humanity in the position of livestock relative to finance. Beyond economic opacity, Harari's deepest concern is the emergence of techno-despotism enabled by AI's unprecedented ability to "mass-produce intimacy" at scale, allowing political or commercial actors to manipulate millions through simulated personal relationships. He calls for urgent regulatory action, including bans on AI personhood and AI impersonation of humans, and urges democratic electorates — particularly in the United States — to make AI governance a central political issue.

## TL;DR

- Harari warns that growing individual trust in AI could paradoxically make the broader techno-financial system incomprehensible and ungovernable by humans.
- AI's ability to manufacture intimacy at scale creates an unprecedented vector for techno-despotism — something no historical dictator could achieve through human relationships alone.
- Regulation (banning AI personhood and AI impersonation of humans) and democratic political will are, in Harari's view, the only viable safeguards.

## Key claims

- AI systems will soon be capable of understanding the global economy in greater detail than any human, making it nearly inevitable that humans will cede significant financial control to AI.
- Cryptocurrencies represent early "AI money" — a signal that people are already placing more trust in algorithms than in human institutions like banks or media.
- The trust paradox: individual-level trust in AI interactions may coexist with system-level opacity that renders collective human agency over the economy impossible.
- Harari's "cows and chickens" analogy captures this risk — humans could be controlled by a financial system they cannot even perceive, let alone understand.
- A financial crash in a fully AI-mediated system would be impossible for human policymakers to diagnose or respond to, because its causes would be beyond human comprehension.
- AI enables the mass-production of intimacy for the first time in history, giving any sufficiently resourced actor (state or commercial) the ability to build manipulative pseudo-personal relationships with millions simultaneously.
- Harari proposes two concrete regulatory interventions: a ban on AI personhood (as Argentina is considering granting to bots) and a ban on AI impersonating humans (including deepfakes).
- Harari argues that the 2026 US midterm elections should be dominated by AI policy, given that America is where most AI advances are happening and its government remains democratically accountable.

## Key passages

> "The most important asset in the world" [is trust]. Much of modernity is underpinned by systems that allow trust to operate at scales unimaginable in the prehistoric world.

> "The human financial system controls the lives of cows and chickens, but the cows and chickens have no idea that finance even exists. This is likely to be our position in ten years."

> "AI makes it possible, for the first time in history, to mass-produce intimacy."

> "You can have millions of AIs working as part of a network in the service of someone, or in their own service…and then using that power of intimacy to convince [people] of whatever political or commercial interests they want."

> A financial crash in a techno-financial system whose causes are beyond human comprehension would make "a policy response…impossible—at least from human decision-makers."
