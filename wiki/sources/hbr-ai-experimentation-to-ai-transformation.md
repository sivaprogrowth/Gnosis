## Abstract

Published in *Harvard Business Review* (April 2026), this article addresses the gap between widespread generative AI adoption and meaningful bottom-line improvement. The authors — Arjun Dutt, Gene Rapoport, Aaron "Ronnie" Chatterji, Gawesha Weeratunga, and Harrison Satcher — argue that most organisations are stuck in experimentation mode rather than achieving true transformation. The piece is positioned as a practical guide for leaders seeking to translate AI pilots into enterprise-wide performance gains. It spans topics of corporate strategy, process management, and [[concepts/executive-ai-fluency|executive AI fluency]].

## TL;DR

- Generative AI has moved rapidly from novelty to boardroom priority, but widespread adoption has not yet translated into proportional bottom-line results for most companies.
- The article argues that organisations must move beyond experimentation to achieve genuine AI-driven [[concepts/implementation-wave|transformation]].
- Practical frameworks are needed to bridge the gap between isolated AI pilots and company-wide performance improvement.

## Key claims

- Generative AI has "sprinted from novelty to boardroom priority in record time," signalling unusually fast enterprise uptake.
- Despite broad adoption, many companies are not realising bottom-line improvement commensurate with AI's capabilities.
- The core challenge is transitioning from scattered experimentation to structured, scalable transformation.
- Success requires moving beyond proof-of-concept pilots toward enterprise-wide process and strategy integration.
- Digital transformation and process management disciplines are central to closing the gap between AI experimentation and AI transformation.
- Performance indicators (KPIs/metrics) need to be reoriented around AI-driven outcomes rather than activity metrics.

## Key passages

> "Generative AI has sprinted from novelty to boardroom priority in record time."

> "Despite the widespread adoption of this technology, not every company is realizing bottom-line improvement commensurate with its capabilities."
