## Abstract

Generative AI is fundamentally restructuring B2B buying journeys by shifting discovery, evaluation, and recommendation into AI-mediated environments that sellers neither own nor can easily observe — what the authors term the "dark funnel." Drawing on case studies from pharmaceuticals (GSK), industrial manufacturing (IMI), and banking, the article argues that traditional go-to-market levers such as sales representatives, channel partners, and owned media are being partially displaced by how AI tools synthesize and surface content. The piece introduces a 4C framework — Coordination, Citability, Credibility, and Calibration — as the strategic response, and warns that B2B leaders have been slow to adapt despite mounting evidence that the shift is durable and accelerating.

## TL;DR

- Gen AI tools are compressing and reshaping the B2B purchase journey, with 75% of U.S. tech buyers now completing purchases in 12 weeks or less (vs. 11 months in 2024), driven by AI-synthesized discovery rather than traditional sales channels.
- The "dark funnel" — AI-mediated influence that remains largely invisible to sellers — means that content structure, machine-readability, and third-party citability now determine competitive positioning as much as sales investment does.
- The 4C framework (Coordination, Citability, Credibility, Calibration) offers a practical path for organizations to engineer AI-mediated presence and monitor it through generative listening systems.

## Key claims

- Over 40% of U.S. physicians now use OpenEvidence daily; it handled 20 million queries in January 2026 — up from 2.6 million in December 2024 — signalling durable workflow integration, not experimentation.
- IDC predicts that 62% of traditional B2B demand generation will be AI-led by 2028.
- McKinsey's 2025 B2B Pulse Survey found only 19% of respondents are implementing gen AI use cases for B2B buying and selling, indicating a widespread strategic gap.
- [[concepts/generative-engine-optimization|Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)]] is replacing SEO as the dominant content-discovery mechanism, requiring content to be engineered for LLM ingestion rather than human reading.
- GSK's 2025–26 audit tested 6,000 prompts across nine healthcare-practitioner decision nodes and found that brand AI visibility varied dramatically by prompt specificity — a brand ranking first for a broad query dropped to fourth for a narrower, commercially critical one.
- Content structured behind paywalls or as non-machine-readable PDFs (e.g., downloadable files vs. embedded web content) risks being excluded from LLM training and retrieval, with direct patient-safety implications when clinical guidelines are involved.
- A Digitas UK pilot found that more than 80% of LLM sources for B2B fintech payment solutions originated directly from the brands themselves (e.g., [[companies/stripe|Stripe]], Adyen, PayPal, Visa), suggesting that content-rich category leaders benefit disproportionately from AI-mediated discovery.
- Nascent research suggests Reddit and YouTube may currently carry outsized influence on LLM answers, prompting companies like IMI to restructure their video content strategy toward short, authoritative formats.

## Key passages

> "A purchasing manager may bypass product sites and sales representatives, relying instead on AI‑synthesized specs. A low‑impact press release can become a persistent data point in AI training sets. And a competitor's framing can become the industry's default if it is more retrievable and easier for AI to distill."

> "Yet, traffic originating from gen AI tools remains largely invisible to companies (we've come to call it the 'dark funnel'), leaving sellers with limited insight into how, where, and to what extent their content is influencing buyer decisions."

> "AI systems rely on structured, trustworthy, machine-readable data, so the way we publish and organize information must be engineered for LLM ingestion, not just human reading. In simple terms, it's about controlling the input to control the output." — IMI executive

> "We know what a high-quality publication looks like for human readers. Now we need to understand what a good publication looks like for the API and the LLM. What are the basics to ensure? What metadata is missing? And what potential Q&A documents should we produce?" — Christof B. Wyss, GSK

> "Customers don't probe the answers deeply. Through this LLM audit, IMI aims to safeguard brand visibility, strengthen commercial pull-through over time, reduce misinformation risk, and build long-term influence by being early to shape how LLMs understand the category." — Emma Fox, IMI

> "Industrial projects work with specifications. Today it's much easier to find alternatives, compare features and prices, and equip yourself to push the conversation with B2B suppliers even if you don't have the knowledge." — Marcella Colombino, IMI
