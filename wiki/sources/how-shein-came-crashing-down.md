## Abstract

This *Economist* article examines the dramatic fall of Shein, the Chinese ultra-fast-fashion e-retailer that was once valued at $100bn by private investors. After years of hypergrowth driven by data-driven trend-spotting, algorithmic design, and cheap Chinese manufacturing, Shein's revenue growth has stalled (just 1% year-on-year in Q1 2026) and the company is now operating at a loss. The article identifies three compounding causes: intensified competition from copycat platforms including Temu, [[companies/amazon|Amazon]]'s Haul, and [[companies/tiktok|TikTok Shop]]; the closure of the de minimis tax loophole in the US and EU; and higher tariffs on Chinese goods under the second Trump administration. Shein is now preparing to list on the Hong Kong stock exchange at a valuation of just $27bn — roughly a quarter of its 2022 peak.

## TL;DR

- Shein's revenue growth collapsed from 21% (2024) to 1% (Q1 2026), and the company swung from $3.4bn net profit to an operating loss.
- The closure of the US/EU de minimis rule and Trump-era tariffs on Chinese goods were the primary structural blows to its business model.
- Shein is listing in Hong Kong at ~$27bn, down from a $100bn private valuation, after failed IPO attempts in New York and London.

## Key claims

- In 2022, Shein was valued at $100bn by private investors including General Atlantic; its planned Hong Kong IPO in September 2026 prices it at just $27bn.
- Shein's net profit of $3.4bn in 2024 has turned into an operating loss by mid-2026.
- The company offers more than 2 million distinct clothing items and adds 4,700 new items per day, serving 280 million customers worldwide (excluding China).
- Temu (launched by PDD in 2022), [[companies/amazon|Amazon]]'s Haul (2024), and [[companies/tiktok|TikTok Shop]] have all emerged as direct competitors, with TikTok Shop overtaking Shein in US sales.
- The removal of the de minimis duty exemption in the US and EU, combined with higher tariffs on Chinese goods, caused Shein's US revenue share to fall from ~30% in 2023 to ~23% in early 2026.
- Shein has begun diversifying its manufacturing base, including a partnership with the retail arm of [[companies/reliance-retail|Reliance Retail]] in India to produce clothing for local and international markets.
- Shein's New York IPO was abandoned due to congressional pushback over alleged forced labour in its supply chain; a London listing was approved by UK regulators but not blessed by the Chinese government, leading to the Hong Kong pivot.
- The valuation collapse has forced Shein to pay settlements to investors who suffered large losses during the prolonged IPO process.

## Key passages

> Only a few years ago Shein looked unstoppable. Its novel business model—using oodles of data and clever algorithms to spot fashion trends and generate new designs, which its network of Chinese suppliers stitch for a pittance—proved a hit in Western markets.

> In 2024 it generated $39bn in revenue, up by 21% from the year before. In 2025 sales grew by only 8%, to $42bn, and in the first quarter of 2026 they increased by a mere 1%, year on year. In 2024 the company's net profit reached $3.4bn. It is now operating at a loss.

> Perhaps an even bigger reason for Shein's slowing sales and deteriorating profitability is the closing last year of a tax loophole in America and the European Union known as the de minimis rule, which had allowed packages below a certain value to be imported free of duties.

> The percentage of global revenue it generates from the country fell from nearly 30% in 2023 to around 23% in the first three months of this year.

> Shein has spent years sounding out various potential stock markets. Its original plan, to list in New York, was abandoned amid pushback from American lawmakers over the alleged use of forced labour in the firm's supply chain (which Shein denies).

> The collapse in Shein's valuation since it began efforts to go public has forced it to pay settlements to investors who have experienced precipitous losses.
