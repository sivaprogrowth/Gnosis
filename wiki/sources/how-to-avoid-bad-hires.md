## Abstract

This article presents a seven-step framework for avoiding bad hires, a costly and pervasive problem acknowledged by three-quarters of employers. The author argues that bad hires result from a broken hiring process and can cost between $17,000 and $240,000 per incident, with ripple effects on productivity, morale, and work quality. The framework covers everything from writing compelling job descriptions and conducting rigorous reference checks to designing structured interview stages and applying [[google]]'s Rule of 4 — the finding that four interviews predict new-hire performance with 86% confidence. The piece is primarily aimed at startup founders and hiring managers seeking a repeatable, signal-rich hiring process.

## TL;DR

- Bad hires stem from process failures, not just candidate shortcomings; a structured multi-stage process dramatically reduces the risk.
- Distinguishing between *skills* (specific abilities) and *competencies* (behaviours and tendencies) is essential to evaluating whether a candidate will actually succeed on the job.
- [[google]]'s Rule of 4 — evaluating candidates across Role Knowledge, Critical Thinking, Communication, and Cultural fit — provides 86% confidence in predicting new-hire performance.

## Key claims

- Three-quarters of employers report having made at least one bad hire, with costs ranging from $17,000 to $240,000 per incident.
- Reference checks should be treated as substantive assessments, not formalities, drawing on current or previous managers to evaluate knowledge, skills, and work history.
- Job descriptions should function as marketing material — highlighting success factors and brand personality — rather than dry lists of tasks and responsibilities.
- Setting success factors means envisioning what a dream hire will have *delivered* at the one-year mark, quarter by quarter, rather than specifying a to-do list.
- Skills (e.g., copywriting, programming) and competencies (e.g., adaptability, strategic planning) are distinct evaluation dimensions and both must be explicitly listed for each role.
- Interview exercises and case studies — typically 2–3 hours of candidate effort — provide a window into how a candidate thinks and works under realistic conditions.
- A five-stage interview funnel (qualifying call → first interview → second interview → exercise → third interview) with clearly assigned DRIs ensures each stage yields distinct hiring signals.
- Google's research found that four interviews are sufficient to predict new-hire performance with 86% confidence, and additional interviews yield diminishing returns.

## Key passages

> Bad hires are the result of a broken hiring process.

> ¾ of employers say they've hired the wrong person for a position. And to make matters worse, the average cost of a bad hire ranges from $17,000 to $240,000.

> Instead of creating a to-do list of tasks and responsibilities, focus on the end results the business needs from the candidate in order for them to "win" at your company.

> Skills are the highly specific abilities that an employee needs to perform their job… Competencies are knowledge, behaviors, or tendencies that lead an individual to be successful in a given activity.

> Google found 4 interviews were enough to predict a new hire's performance with 86% confidence.

> Each step of the hiring process should give you the signal you need to know whether their skills, values, and needs line up with your role.
