## Abstract

AI-powered employee monitoring systems are growing in adoption across tech and white-collar industries, tracking metrics ranging from sales results to keyboard strokes and screen-sleep frequency, often without workers' knowledge due to weak disclosure laws. The article, drawing on interviews with monitoring-system makers and workers who game them, argues that employees must now strategically manage their *appearance* of productivity — not just their actual output — to survive in an era of AI-driven performance evaluation. Key tactical advice includes meticulous calendar hygiene, targeting a "sweet spot" of roughly 60–80% measured activity, skepticism toward software-based mouse jigglers in favour of physical devices, and calibrating AI tool usage to avoid both under-adoption and wasteful overuse. The piece uses a pending lawsuit by former Meta employees — who allege AI systems were used to select workers for layoffs — as a high-profile illustration of the stakes.

## TL;DR

- AI workforce-monitoring tools now cross-reference calendar data, activity levels, mouse movement, and AI token spend to score employee productivity, often invisibly.
- Employees should target ~60–80% measured activity, keep calendars meticulously updated, and avoid both mouse-jiggler software (detectable) and over-reliance on AI tools (seen as wasteful).
- A lawsuit alleging [[companies/facebook|Meta]] used AI to conduct discriminatory layoffs underscores that the consequences of poor monitoring scores can be severe.

## Key claims

- Many U.S. states do not require employers to disclose when employee-monitoring software is in use, meaning workers may be surveilled without their knowledge.
- Employee monitoring has intensified beyond the remote-work era and continues to escalate even as office attendance returns.
- Former [[companies/facebook|Meta]] employees allege in a lawsuit that Meta used a "constellation of internal artificial-intelligence systems" to select workers for the ~10% workforce reduction announced in May 2026; Meta maintains humans make final termination decisions.
- Calendar integration is a key advancement in modern monitoring: platforms such as Insightful cross-check online status against scheduled meetings to distinguish legitimate offline activity from unproductive absence.
- Insightful CEO Ivan Petrovic states that most companies using his platform target 60–80% of employee time being "utilized for work" — meaning some downtime is implicitly tolerated and expected.
- Artificially inflating measured activity to ~90%+ can itself appear suspicious to monitoring algorithms.
- A Visier survey of 1,000 U.S. workers found that 48% admitted to exaggerating their AI usage to appear relevant; this strategy is now backfiring as companies scrutinise AI token spending for waste.
- Physical mouse-movement devices (hardware jigglers) are increasingly preferred over software equivalents because corporate cybersecurity systems are more effective at blocking software-based solutions.

## Key passages
