## Abstract

This Mint interview with Sanjiv Mehta, CEO and MD of Hindustan Unilever Limited (HUL), covers his decade-long tenure during which HUL's market capitalisation quadrupled from ₹1.33 trillion to ₹5.98 trillion. Mehta addresses a wide range of strategic topics including the rationale for royalty payments to parent Unilever Plc, HUL's "Winning in Many Indias" cluster-based market model, the company's digital transformation journey (including the Shikhar app and Livewire analytics platform), and its competitive positioning against rivals such as [[companies/reliance-industries|Reliance Retail]], P&G, Nestle, and ITC. The interview is notable for Mehta's argument that India's low per-capita FMCG consumption (~$45 vs. China's 3x and Philippines' 4x) represents massive headroom for growth, and that competition, far from being a threat, consistently brings out the best in HUL.

## TL;DR

- India's per-capita FMCG spend (~$45) is a fraction of peer economies, giving HUL enormous room to grow without competing as a zero-sum game.
- HUL's digital transformation—spanning the Shikhar retailer app, Livewire data platform, AI hub, and algorithmic media optimization—is now a core competitive moat.
- Royalty payments to Unilever are justified by access to global R&D (700+ scientists in Bangalore), technology, brand IP, and buying scale that HUL could not replicate independently.

## Key claims

- HUL's market cap quadrupled over Mehta's 10-year tenure (2013–2023), from ₹1.33 trillion to ₹5.98 trillion.
- India's per-capita FMCG consumption is ~$45, compared to Indonesia (2x), China (3x), and the Philippines (4x), indicating massive untapped headroom.
- The royalty arrangement with Unilever (increasing by 80 bps over three years to 3.45%) is framed as fair because it grants access to global R&D, brand IP, and scale that would cost more to replicate locally.
- HUL's "Winning in Many Indias" model segments India into 15 consumer clusters, each with distinct strategies, enabled by the Livewire data analytics platform—which was subsequently rolled out globally by Unilever.
- The Shikhar app (HUL's online ordering platform) has been adopted by 1.1 million retailers, with digital demand capture exceeding 25% of total orders.
- HUL maintains an AI hub that tracks global consumer trends, models product prototypes digitally, and uses simulated computers to replace physical factory test runs, dramatically reducing lead times.
- General trade still accounts for ~75% of the FMCG channel mix; even after 10 years, Mehta expects it to remain the largest channel—but in a highly digitized form, with distributors shifting toward financing and market development roles.
- Surf Excel became HUL's first billion-dollar brand, with purpose-led marketing ("daag achhe hain") cited as a key driver of brand equity.

## Key passages

> "India is not a zero-sum game. If someone else grows, that doesn't mean I'll decline. We have enough space in India at $45 per capita FMCG consumption. Compare this with Indonesia, which is 2x; China, which is 3x; the Philippines, which is 4x. In India, the headroom to grow is massive."

> "The cost of all these R&D centres doesn't come in my P&L; it goes to Unilever and comes to me as royalty. So, people don't understand how a multinational absorbs it."

> "Today, it's not my gut feeling which tells me how much media to spend. It's all science. And the more data we have, the more powerful it becomes."

> "We have two philosophies—high tech and high touch. They go together. The distributor in the market knows the retailer's history. The distributor will not only give credit but will also be involved in market development."

> "A good competitor has always been good for HUL. It brings out the best in us."

> "Great brands and great companies are made by great people. And distinctive capabilities, which are hard to replicate, gives the competitive edge."
