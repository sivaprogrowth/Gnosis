## Abstract

This article from *The Economist* profiles Hyrox, a hybrid fitness-racing format that combines running with eight functional strength stations and has grown from 650 participants at its Hamburg debut in 2017 to over 2 million competitors across six continents in the 2026–27 season. The piece examines the social, competitive, and lifestyle dimensions that drive Hyrox's appeal — including its integration with travel culture, influencer communities, and a dedicated dating app and bank card — situating it within a broader record-breaking boom in gym-club memberships across America, Britain, and Europe. The article also explores the format's accessibility advantages over rivals like CrossFit, its aspirations toward Olympic inclusion in 2032, and the tension between surging demand and expensive, scarce race tickets. Co-founder Moritz Fürste, an Olympic-medal-winning field hockey player, expresses confidence that supply constraints will ease and that the format has enduring lifestyle-brand potential.

## TL;DR

- Hyrox has exploded from a single 650-person race in Hamburg (2017) to 2 million+ participants globally in 2026–27, making it one of the fastest-growing mass-participation fitness formats in the world.
- Its appeal blends competitive fitness with social and travel experiences, supported by a strong influencer presence and ancillary lifestyle products.
- Co-founder Moritz Fürste aims for Olympic inclusion in 2032, comparing Hyrox's stickiness to tennis and golf as a sport people return to repeatedly.

## Key claims

- Hyrox describes itself as the "fastest-growing mass-participation fitness concept in the world," growing from 650 participants in 2017 to over 2 million in the 2026–27 season across six continents.
- The format consists of eight 1 km running laps interleaved with eight functional-fitness stations (ski erg, row, burpee broad jumps, kettlebell carry, sled push/pull, lunges, wall balls), with competitive times ranging from ~60 to ~90 minutes.
- The hashtag #hyrox has accumulated over 500 million views across Instagram and [[companies/tiktok|TikTok]], and Peloton offers dedicated Hyrox training classes.
- Fitness-club memberships across America, Britain, and Europe have reached record highs, providing structural tailwind for Hyrox's growth.
- Hyrox is perceived as more accessible than CrossFit — which peaked in the 2010s — because it avoids complex Olympic weightlifting movements, broadening its appeal to older and recreational athletes.
- Race tickets cost upwards of £150, and with travel, hotels, and gym-training costs, participation is expensive; a Hyrox-themed European cruise charges £2,200 per cabin.
- Moritz Fürste hopes Hyrox will be included in the 2032 Olympics and draws an analogy to tennis and golf as sports with high repeat-participation rates once adopted.

## Key passages

> "Hyrox claims to be the 'fastest-growing mass-participation fitness concept in the world'. At the first race, in Hamburg in 2017, some 650 people took part. In the 2026-27 season more than 2m will compete across six continents."

> "The hype is partly explained by a boom in gym culture: men and women are pumping iron more than they used to. Across America, Britain and Europe, fitness-club memberships have reached a record high."

> "Hyrox is kinder on the body than other fitness races—such as CrossFit, which peaked in popularity in the 2010s—because it does not require complex weightlifting. This may explain why it appeals to retired athletes as well as hobbyists."

> "'Hyrox is not just a race or an event,' one acolyte proclaims online. 'Hyrox is a lifestyle.'"

> "'It's like tennis and golf,' he observes: 'Once it becomes your sport, you'll keep coming back.'"
