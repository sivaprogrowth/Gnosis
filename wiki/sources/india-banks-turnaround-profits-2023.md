## Abstract

India's banking sector, long associated with rampant bad-loan concealment and high-profile fraud, has undergone a remarkable turnaround and is now generating some of the highest profits in the world. The recovery traces back to Raghuram Rajan's 2015 asset-quality review at the Reserve Bank of India, which forced painful write-downs, and to subsequent bankruptcy reforms under Narendra Modi's government that accelerated liquidation of failing firms and consolidated 27 state-owned banks into 12. Non-performing loans in corporate lending peaked at 16% in 2018 but are forecast to fall below 2% by early 2024, while credit growth has rebounded from 3% in 2017 to 18% today. Despite the impressive headline numbers, investor scepticism about state-owned banks persists, given India's history of short-lived reforms and the government's continued outsized influence over public-sector lenders.

## TL;DR

- Indian banks—both state-owned and private—are now among the world's most profitable, with return-on-equity figures rivalling JPMorgan Chase.
- The turnaround is rooted in a forced asset-quality review (2015), bankruptcy law reform (2016), and a massive state-funded recapitalisation of public lenders.
- Investors remain cautious about state banks because government interference and short leadership tenures risk repeating the cycle of politically driven bad lending.

## Key claims

- Canara Bank, Union Bank of India, and IDBI posted net earnings growth of 87%, 61%, and 49% year-on-year respectively; ICICI, Kotak Mahindra, and HDFC rose 37%, 28%, and 19%.
- India's private banks generate a return on equity of ~15% and state banks ~11%, compared with JPMorgan Chase's 14% global benchmark.
- During the first half of the 2010s, loan-evergreening was rampant—especially in state banks—with politically connected borrowers shielded from write-downs.
- Raghuram Rajan's 2015 asset-quality review at the Reserve Bank of India triggered the initial wave of write-downs in energy, steel, and telecoms, costing him a second term.
- Non-performing loans peaked at 16% of corporate lending in 2018 and are forecast by Crisil to fall below 2% by early 2024.
- Modi's government consolidated 27 state banks into 12 (announced 2019) and injected ₹2.6 trillion (~$31bn) in recapitalisation funds over three years.
- State banks have written off $91bn in bad loans over five years—roughly equal to their combined worth, per Boston Consulting Group.
- Private-sector banks (HDFC, Kotak Mahindra, ICICI) trade at triple book value, while many state banks still trade at a fraction of book value, reflecting lasting investor scepticism.
