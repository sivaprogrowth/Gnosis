## Abstract

Walter Russell Mead, writing in the *Wall Street Journal* from New Delhi in March 2023, argues that India's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) is the single most important foreign political party from the standpoint of American national interests — and the least understood. The BJP, rooted in the Hindu nationalist Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) social movement, combines pro-market economics with traditionalist values and populist rhetoric, and has presided over India's rise as a leading economic and strategic power. Mead contends that as U.S.-China tensions escalate, India — and by extension the BJP — is the linchpin of any viable American Indo-Pacific strategy. Based on firsthand interviews with top BJP and RSS leaders including Yogi Adityanath and Mohan Bhagwat, Mead concludes that the movement is at a crossroads, seeking to position itself as the establishment of a rising global power while balancing its grass-roots base.

## TL;DR

- The BJP is the most strategically consequential foreign political party for U.S. interests because India is central to countering rising Chinese power in the Indo-Pacific.
- The BJP/RSS movement is widely misunderstood in the West; it blends Hindu cultural nationalism with pro-market economics and has grown from a fringe movement into one of the world's most powerful civil-society organisations.
- Senior BJP and RSS leaders signalled to Mead a desire for deeper international engagement and economic development, though how those signals translate to the grass roots remains uncertain.

## Key claims

- The BJP, in power since 2014 and heading for a third consecutive victory in 2024, sits at the helm of Indian politics at a moment when India is emerging as both a leading economic power and a strategic linchpin alongside Japan in the Indo-Pacific.
- American efforts to balance rising Chinese power are likely to fall short without Indian partnership, making the BJP's ideology and trajectory of direct relevance to U.S. foreign policy.
- The BJP is analytically comparable to the Muslim Brotherhood (rejection of Western liberal priorities while embracing modernity), the Chinese Communist Party (ambition to lead a billion-plus nation to superpower status), and Israel's Likud Party (pro-market economics combined with populist and traditionalist politics).
- The RSS has grown from a fringe of marginalised intellectuals into what Mead calls "perhaps the most powerful civil-society organization in the world," with rural and urban development programs, religious education, and civic activism mobilising hundreds of millions.
- Critics raise legitimate concerns: journalists critical of the ruling coalition face harassment; religious minorities report mob violence and hostile measures such as broadly drafted anticonversion laws.
- The BJP has achieved notable political successes in India's predominantly Christian northeastern states, and its Uttar Pradesh government claims strong support among Shia Muslims — complicating simple narratives of Hindu majoritarian exclusivity.
- Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath and RSS spiritual leader Mohan Bhagwat both told Mead that the priority is economic investment and growth, and that religious minorities should not face discrimination — signalling a potential mainstreaming of the movement's leadership.
- Understanding the BJP and RSS is presented as equally important for business leaders and investors engaging with India economically as it is for diplomats and policymakers managing the strategic relationship.

## Key passages

> "India's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party is, from the standpoint of American national interests, the most important foreign political party in the world. It may also be the least understood."

> "Like the Muslim Brotherhood, the BJP rejects many ideas and priorities of Western liberalism even as it embraces key features of modernity. Like the Chinese Communist Party, the BJP hopes to lead a nation with more than a billion people to become a global superpower. Like the Likud Party in Israel, the BJP combines a basically pro-market economic stance with populist rhetoric and traditionalist values."

> "From a fringe of mostly marginalized intellectuals and religious enthusiasts, the RSS has become perhaps the most powerful civil-society organization in the world."

> "I did get the impression that the leadership of a once-marginalized movement wants to position itself as the natural establishment of a rising power and is looking to engage deeply and fruitfully with the outside world without losing touch with its social and political base."

> "The invitation to engage with the BJP and RSS is one that Americans can't afford to reject. As tensions with China rise, the U.S. needs India as both an economic and political partner."
