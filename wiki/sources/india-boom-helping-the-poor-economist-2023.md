## Abstract

This *Economist* article uses vehicle sales data as a proxy to interrogate whether India's headline economic boom is translating into welfare gains for ordinary citizens. While car sales have surged 16% since the pandemic began — partly driven by SUV demand — two-wheeler sales (the vehicle of the masses) remain 15% below 2019 levels, illustrating a "two-speed" economy. The article draws on household surveys, labour-market data, and rural wage figures to argue that poverty reduction has stalled and may have reversed, with the [[companies/world-bank]] estimating that lockdowns pushed 56 million Indians into extreme poverty. It concludes that structural recovery is only tentatively beginning, and that government claims of a universally prosperous *Amrit Kaal* remain premature.

## TL;DR

- India's GDP growth leads the world, but the gains are accruing overwhelmingly to wealthier segments; two-wheeler sales and rural wages tell a bleaker story for the poor.
- The [[companies/world-bank]] estimates pandemic lockdowns pushed 56 million Indians into extreme poverty, and recovery has been slow and uneven.
- Early 2023 data hints at a tentative turn — rural wages picking up, bank deposits rising, two-wheeler sales creeping up — but the evidence is fragile.

## Key claims

- India's GDP grew 4.4% year-on-year in Q4 2022, and the IMF expects it to be the fastest-growing major economy in 2023, accounting for 15% of global growth.
- Car sales have risen 16% since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, but two-wheeler sales (the vehicle of the majority) are down 15% since 2019.
- Only 6% of India's poorest households (income below ₹100,000/year) believe their families are better off than a year ago, per a CMIE survey of 44,000 households.
- The [[companies/world-bank]] estimates that pandemic shutdowns pushed 56 million Indians into extreme poverty.
- Real wages in rural areas have stagnated while annual inflation reached 6.5% in January 2023; rural food costs have risen 28% since 2019 and onion prices by 51%.
- Take-up of India's rural employment guarantee programme (MGNREGS) remains above pre-COVID levels, and CMIE data show unemployment averaging over 7% for two years.
- Labour-force participation rates have fallen since the pandemic, with many workers who left cities during lockdowns not having returned.
- The governing BJP's framing of *Amrit Kaal* — an auspicious period of universal prosperity — is contested by data showing deeply uneven recovery.

## Key passages

> "To many, India's automobile boom symbolises the country's superfast economic rise. On February 28th new figures revealed that India's gdp grew by 4.4% year on year in the last quarter of 2022, down from 6.3% in the previous quarter."

> "Purchases of two-wheelers, such as scooters and motorcycles, have sputtered since covid hit, and are down by 15% since 2019. These are the vehicles of the masses: half of households own a two-wheeler; fewer than one in ten own a car."

> "Only 6% of India's poorest households—those bringing in less than 100,000 rupees ($1,200) a year—believe their families are better off than a year ago."

> "The World Bank estimates shutdowns pushed 56m Indians into extreme poverty. Since then inflation has further eroded purchasing power: real wages in rural areas, where most of the poor live, have stagnated, and annual inflation jumped to 6.5% in January."

> "Rural food costs have risen by 28% since 2019; onion prices by an eye-watering 51%."

> "A lot more improvement will be needed, however, for claims of _Amrit Kaal_ to ring true."
