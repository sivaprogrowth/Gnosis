## Abstract

This *Economist* article examines whether AI is actually destroying jobs in India's approximately 6-million-strong IT sector. Contrary to the prevailing hype, evidence of large-scale AI-driven job losses is limited: headcount at major listed IT consultancies has dipped modestly from a post-pandemic hiring boom rather than collapsed, and global capability centres (GCCs) have grown strongly from 1.4m to 2.4m workers since 2019. AI is, however, accelerating two pre-existing shifts: the movement of technology functions from outsourced "context" work to in-house "core" work, and the growing disadvantage faced by entry-level graduates whose basic-skill roles are most automatable. The article argues that while India's IT industry has survived AI so far, the technology is reshaping the talent mix in ways that deepen inequality between experienced engineers and an increasingly large pool of underemployed graduates.

## TL;DR

- India's big IT consultancies have shed some headcount since ChatGPT, but the decline reflects a post-pandemic hiring correction more than AI displacement.
- Global capability centres (GCCs) are booming, adding 1m workers since 2019, as firms insource AI-critical functions rather than outsource them.
- AI is hollowing out entry-level IT roles while sharply increasing demand — and salaries — for workers with AI/ML skills.

## Key claims

- Around 6 million Indians work in IT (Nasscom), but they represent only ~1% of India's workforce, making accurate sampling difficult.
- Headcount at Nifty IT index companies fell from 1.71m in 2023 to 1.66m by mid-2026, yet remains 9% above the pre-pandemic trend.
- GCC employment rose from 1.4m (2019) to 2.4m (2026), lifting GCCs' share of aggregate IT employment from 35% to 40%.
- [[companies/anthropic]], Costco, and L'Oréal are among recent new GCC entrants to India.
- AI is accelerating the shift from "context" outsourcing to "core" insourcing: functions that provide competitive advantage must now be internal.
- Naukri data shows overall IT job demand down 3% year-on-year, while demand for AI/ML-skilled workers is up 25%.
- Entry-level computer-science salaries in IT consulting have been frozen at ~310,000 rupees (~$3,200/year) for nearly 20 years.
- Graduates specialising in AI programmes at top Indian universities can earn roughly twice as much as peers in generic computer-science tracks.

## Key passages

> "Before the breakthroughs that made chatbots functional, tech types quipped that AI stood for 'actually Indians', as seemingly whizzy tech was really the work of humans in Hyderabad or Bangalore."

> "Aggregate headcount is still 9% above the pre-pandemic trend, and the consultancies are pulling back after a hiring boom."

> "The number employed in global capability centres (GCCs), captive back offices handling everything from research and IT to HR and finance, has risen from 1.4m in 2019 to 2.4m this year."

> "'What AI changes is the mix of talent we need,' says Vinod Kumar, chief people officer at Amtech, an American software company with a GCC in Bangalore. There is less demand for those workers with basic skills to do repetitive work. Instead there is a focus on engineers who can work effectively with AI."

> "Entry-level salaries for computer-science graduates in IT consulting have been frozen at around 310,000 rupees ($3,200) a year for nearly 20 years."

> "AI has not eaten India's software services yet. But it is eyeing it hungrily."
