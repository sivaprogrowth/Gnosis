## Abstract

This newsletter instalment from *The India Notes* is a qualitative interview profile of "Girish," a Tamil Nadu–origin software engineer who has migrated to Bangalore with his wife and two-year-old daughter. Published as part of the "What's Cooking?" series exploring food behaviour in urban Indian millennial households, the piece documents three interlocking behaviour patterns: a toddler who drives culinary variety, a tiny kitchen that forces ad hoc, just-in-time grocery purchasing, and a wife whose hyper-selective non-vegetarian preferences effectively dictate the household's food-delivery choices. The interview illuminates how city migration disrupts established joint-family food routines and pushes households toward on-demand delivery services such as BBDaily, Swiggy, and Zomato. Transcripts were lightly rephrased using ChatGPT while attempting to preserve authenticity.

## TL;DR

- Migrating from a joint-family home in Chennai to a nuclear household in Bangalore radically changed Girish's grocery cadence—from monthly bulk buys to ad hoc, just-in-time ordering via BBDaily and Instamart.
- The household's food-delivery platform loyalty shifted from Zomato Pro to Swiggy One after a customer-support dispute, highlighting how trust and bundled perks (Dineout, Instamart) drive platform stickiness.
- A toddler's unpredictable appetite and a partner's narrow non-vegetarian preferences are the dominant decision-making forces in the household—not the primary earner's own tastes.

## Key claims

- Girish's household switched from monthly grocery stocking to an ad hoc model after moving to a smaller Bangalore flat with limited kitchen storage, relying primarily on BBDaily for next-day delivery.
- The toddler's food variety needs—she tired of repetitive South Indian dishes—create ongoing parental labour, including a 60–75-minute nightly feeding ritual that pushes Girish's own dinner past 10 pm.
- Moving to a nuclear setup in a new city caused the couple to nearly stop dining out (only 5–6 times in two years), primarily because the child's crankiness makes restaurant visits stressful for the mother.
- Girish's wife, despite being non-vegetarian, orders from only one or two trusted restaurants (e.g., Mani's Dum Biryani) due to a high authenticity bar set by her mother's home cooking.
- Platform switching from Zomato Pro to Swiggy One was triggered by a customer-service failure and Zomato's policy change on food-quality responsibility, not by price alone.
- Discount stacking—Dineout (15%), CRED app, HDFC credit card—is an active strategy: Girish estimates saving ₹600–700 on a ~₹2,600 restaurant bill.
- Girish's personal food-ordering triggers are emotional (work frustration) or functional (staying awake for late-night work), not cravings-driven; he mostly defers to his wife's preferences.
- Health motivation is aspirational but unactionable: Girish cites seeing founders like Paras Chopra (Wingify) and Nitin (Zerodha) tweet about health as inspiration, but has not yet formed a concrete plan.

## Key passages

> "We have a tiny kitchen, so we don't buy a lot of groceries at once… After a month or two, we stopped buying groceries monthly and switched to an ad hoc basis since our family did not require as much food, and we wanted to avoid waste."

> "My wife is quite particular about her non-vegetarian food preferences. She especially enjoys dishes prepared by her mother, which has led to her being quite selective about what she eats from outside. Despite being a non-vegetarian, she's almost like me in her pickiness, only opting for one or two restaurants that meet her taste and value expectations."

> "After experiencing some issues with Zomato's customer support and their decision to take responsibility for food quality no longer, I became frustrated and stopped using Zomato Pro. I then switched to Swiggy One, which I found comparatively better."

> "There are two main instances when I feel the urge to order food. First, it occasionally happens when I'm particularly frustrated or upset with work, maybe once or twice a month. Second, there are times when I need to stay up all night to complete something important."

> "Seeing people like Paras Chopra from Wingify and Nitin from Zerodha taking reasonable steps towards their health on Twitter motivated me to do the same. Although I don't have a solid plan yet, I want to start by eating only when I'm truly hungry, not out of habit or when my mind is stressed."

> "She doesn't eat mutton and only orders chicken dishes from a handful of trusted restaurants, such as Mani's Dum Biryani. For the past year, my wife has been ordering Chicken Lollipop Biryani from Meghna, finding it a good value for around 300 Rupees."
