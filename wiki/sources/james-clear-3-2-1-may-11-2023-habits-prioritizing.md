## Abstract

This is the May 11, 2023 edition of James Clear's weekly 3-2-1 newsletter, in which Clear shares three original aphorisms, two curated quotes, and one reflective question. The issue focuses on the compounding power of average effort sustained over long time horizons, strategies for making habits more enjoyable, and a two-frame prioritization model (10 years vs. 1 hour). Supporting quotes from physician-YouTuber Ali Abdaal and actress Joan Crawford add texture on early-morning productivity and the art of hosting diverse gatherings. Together the edition advances Clear's broader thesis — drawn from *Atomic Habits* — that small, consistent, enjoyable actions compound into meaningful outcomes.

## TL;DR

- Exceptional results often come from average effort repeated for an above-average duration, not from heroic bursts.
- Prioritization collapses into two useful time frames: 10-year ambitions (worked on in 1-hour daily increments) and single-session tasks completed fully.
- Most decisions are not worth optimizing; identifying and mastering the single highest-leverage action renders most others irrelevant.

## Key claims

- Nearly any habit can be made more enjoyable; asking "What would this look like if it were fun?" is a practical design question.
- Long-time-horizon projects (business-building, fitness, relationships) require consistent 1-hour work sessions rather than sporadic large efforts.
- The 1-hour unit of work is valuable specifically because it ends with something *finished*, avoiding the drag of perpetual half-work.
- Early-morning hours carry disproportionate productivity value: "An hour before 9 is worth 2 after 5" (Ali Abdaal, citing a medical school professor).
- Good parties are made great by deliberate diversity of guests — across profession, age, and background — rather than homogeneous social circles.
- The most powerful prioritization question is identifying the single action that makes 1,000 other choices irrelevant, then moving quickly through everything else.
- Average effort compounded over an above-average time frame is a sufficient formula for exceptional outcomes.

## Key passages

> "Sometimes all you need for exceptional results is average effort repeated for an above-average amount of time."

> "Make your habits fun. This doesn't mean each habit will be the most fun thing in your life, but nearly any habit can be made more fun than it is currently. Ask yourself, 'What would it look like if this was fun?' … Find the most enjoyable version of each habit you do."

> "10 years or 1 hour. Those are the two time frames worth prioritizing. … How do you work toward the 10-year things? In 1 hour increments. … The key is that you finish with something accomplished, not with half-work still waiting to be completed."

> "An hour before 9 is worth 2 after 5." — Ali Abdaal (quoting a medical school professor)

> "The best parties are a wild mixture. Take some corporation presidents, add a bearded painter, a professional jockey, your visiting friends from Brussels, a politician, a hairdresser, and a professor of physics, toss them all together, and try to get them to stop talking long enough to eat!" — Joan Crawford, *My Way of Life*

> "What's the one action that moves the needle more than 100 other actions? What's the one choice that renders 1000 other choices irrelevant? Most things are not worth optimizing. Master the big moves and move quickly and peacefully through the other stuff."
