## Abstract

This *Economist* article argues that Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO of [[companies/nvidia|Nvidia]] is a significant blow to Silicon Valley's "accelerationist" movement — those who advocate for unrestricted, breakneck AI development and oppose regulation. Huang was the movement's most credible industry spokesperson, maintaining close ties with the White House and vocally opposing AI "doomers." His departure, combined with midterm election losses for accelerationist-aligned politicians and [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]]'s high-profile pro-regulation IPO, has left the movement on the back foot. The article also examines knock-on effects for the open-source AI community, which relied heavily on Huang's political and financial backing.

## TL;DR

- Jensen Huang's resignation removes the accelerationist movement's most prominent and credible industry voice.
- Nvidia's future support for open-source AI — including its $12.9bn planned acquisition of [[companies/hugging-face|Hugging Face]] — is now uncertain under interim CEO Colette Kress.
- Some Silicon Valley founders privately welcome a slower pace, arguing it would let AI demonstrate real value without utopian hype.

## Key claims

- Jensen Huang was the accelerationist movement's "most credible spokesperson," publicly attacking "doomers" and lobbying against AI regulation.
- Fellow accelerationists — including Elon Musk ([[companies/spacex|SpaceX]]), Martin Casado ([[companies/andreessen-horowitz|Andreessen Horowitz]]), and Garry Tan (Y Combinator) — have called publicly for Huang's return.
- The accelerationist camp suffered setbacks in the midterm elections, which will bring many AI-sceptic legislators to power.
- [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]]'s landmark public listing, combined with the company's consistent calls for AI regulation, represents another symbolic defeat for accelerationists.
- Nvidia under Huang invested heavily in open-source AI, including backing Reflection, Thinking Machine Labs, and its own Nemotron model line, and agreed to acquire [[companies/hugging-face|Hugging Face]] for $12.9bn.
- Interim CEO Colette Kress has signalled continued commitment to an "open-model ecosystem" but is "rigorously evaluating" future investments — a notably more cautious posture.
- The open-source AI community faces a dual threat: loss of Huang's advocacy and growing legislative pressure to ban American firms from using free Chinese AI models.
- Some operators — contrasted with "people spending their day on X" — are privately ready for deceleration, believing it would let AI prove genuine business value.

## Key passages

> For the "accelerationists" in Silicon Valley—who are eager to ensure artificial intelligence continues developing at breakneck speed—the fall of Jensen Huang is a heavy blow. Nvidia's boss was the movement's most credible spokesperson.

> Mr Huang publicly bemoaned their talk of "science-fiction outcomes" and hinted at unspecified "ulterior reasons" for spreading fear; of Dario Amodei, Anthropic's boss, he said they disagreed on "almost everything."

> In July Mr Huang came out against that effort, with Nvidia and more than 230 other organisations signing a letter urging the government to avoid "premature restrictions."

> Whether Nvidia "will continue to aggressively promote the development of this ecosystem" in Mr Huang's absence is an "open question", says Anastasios Angelopoulos, boss of Arena, a platform for ranking ai models.

> People who are "actually busy running companies, not spending their day on X, don't really believe in that".
