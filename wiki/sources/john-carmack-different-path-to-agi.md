## Abstract

In this exclusive interview with *Dallas Innovates*, John Carmack—veteran game developer, rocket engineer, and former Oculus CTO—outlines his approach to building artificial general intelligence (AGI) through his startup Keen Technologies, which raised $20 million in 2022. Carmack argues that the mainstream AI industry's convergence on large-scale, compute-heavy approaches may be taking an unnecessary detour, and that AGI's core algorithm is likely a modest program of a "few tens of thousands of lines of code" rather than a massive engineering effort. He assigns a ~60% probability to meaningful AGI arriving by 2030 and positions himself as a deliberate "random test point" in the solution space, independent of the groupthink he perceives at [[companies/openai]], [[companies/google-deepmind]], and peer institutions. The interview spans his intellectual journey into AI, his theory of the problem's scope, his frugal research philosophy, and his vision of AGI as universally accessible "cloud-based artificial human capital."

## TL;DR

- Carmack gives AGI a ~60% probability of arriving by 2030 and ~95% by 2050, and is betting a decade of independent research on it via Keen Technologies.
- He believes AGI's core code will be a small program (tens of thousands of lines), not a massive engineering effort, and is deliberately avoiding the compute-scaling race.
- His strategy is to be a "random test point" outside mainstream groupthink—drawing on systems engineering, older research, and cross-domain synthesis rather than following the dominant academic pipeline.

## Key claims

- Carmack estimates AGI has a ~60% chance of emerging by 2030 and ~95% by 2050, a figure he has only slightly revised upward after three years of dedicated research.
- He believes AGI's source code will amount to a "few tens of thousands of lines"—comparable in scale to existing deep-learning model implementations—because the human genome's brain-relevant information is itself only ~40 megabytes.
- The dominant failure mode of past AI research was over-reliance on symbolic AI and "sophistry of philosophical symbol manipulation," while the breakthrough of the last decade came from deep learning's ability to perform perception tasks.
- Current AI systems (voice assistants, image generators, LLMs) represent valuable "off-ramps" but are not on the critical path to general intelligence, because they are brittle and specialized.
- The key missing ingredients for AGI are consciousness, associative memory, goal-directed planning, and a unified synthesis of reinforcement learning, supervised learning, and unsupervised learning.
- Carmack is deliberately frugal—spending well below his $20 million raise—preferring to identify a "half-dozen key insights" before committing to expensive GPU compute scaling.
- He frames his independent position as analogous to a random-initialization strategy in machine learning: exploring a different attractor basin while large labs converge on the same gradient.
- His near-term target is not superintelligence but a "toddler-equivalent"—a conscious, learning entity that developmental psychologists and engineers could then study and train.

## Key passages

> "Some people have raised billions to pursue this. And while that's interesting in some ways, and there are signs there are extremely powerful things possible now in the narrow machine-learning stuff, it's not clear those are the necessary steps to get all the way to artificial general intelligence."

> "So I asked Ilya Sutskever, OpenAI's chief scientist, for a reading list. He gave me a list of like 40 research papers and said, 'If you really learn all of these, you'll know 90% of what matters today.' And I did. I plowed through all those things and it all started sorting out in my head."

> "One of the things I say—and some people don't like it—is that the source code, the computer programming necessary for artificial general intelligence, is going to be a few tens of thousands of lines of code."

> "I am positioning myself as one of these random test points, where the rest of the industry is going in a direction that's leading to fabulous places, and they're doing a great job on that. But, because we do not have that line of sight—we're not sure that we're in the local attractor basin where we can just gradient descent down to the solution for this—it's important to have some people testing other parts of the solution space as well."

> "My hope is that I can spend several years working through some of these things, building small things that I think point in the right directions. And then, throw some scale at it and push an entire lifetime of information and experience through this and see if it comes out with something that shows that spark."

> "What I keep saying is that as soon as you're at the point where you have the equivalent of a toddler—something that is a being, it's conscious, it's not Einstein, it can't even do multiplication—if you've got a creature that can learn, you can interact with and teach it things on some level."
