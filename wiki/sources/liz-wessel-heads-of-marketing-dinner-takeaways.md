## Abstract

Liz Wessel shares synthesised takeaways from a private dinner she hosted for Heads of Marketing at fast-growing US startups. The thread surfaces practitioner consensus across several pressing go-to-market topics: the nascent but promising state of AI Engine Optimisation (AEO), channel strategy, evolving sales development roles, and changing hiring profiles in marketing. The observations are grounded in real peer exchange rather than analyst reports, making them a useful leading indicator of where B2B marketing practice is heading in 2026.

## TL;DR

- AEO is still very early—only ~50% of marketing leaders at fast-growing startups are even using an AEO tool.
- Events remain the #1 lead-generation channel; focusing on a single social channel outperforms spreading effort across many.
- Inbound SDR roles are being automated away by AI, while BDRs and "product archetype" PMM hires are growing.

## Key claims

- Only 50% of attendees were using an AEO tool; early success was seen with Gauge and Profound, though both require significant effort and specialist skills.
- Swag that features a fun company mascot (e.g., a stuffed animal) is more memorable than traditional giveaways, especially when selling to senior buyers who are parents.
- In-person events ranked as the clear #1 source of high-quality leads, with outbound as a secondary channel.
- Owning a single social channel deeply is more effective than attempting a presence across YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok, X, etc.
- LinkedIn organic content is considered the top channel for employer brand.
- OneScreen is the recommended broker/middleman for out-of-home (OOH) advertising such as billboards.
- BDR headcount is growing, but the inbound SDR role is being eliminated as AI automates inbound qualification.
- Marketing Ops is increasingly requiring data science or SQL skills as AI absorbs traditional operational work.
- Field/conference marketing roles (>2 years experience) are hard to fill because of the demanding travel lifestyle.
- The "product archetype" PMM hire—a former practitioner or ex-customer from the buyer's world—is becoming a standard role at fast-growing startups.

## Key passages

> AEO is still SO early. Only 50% of people were using an AEO tool -- of those seeing some early success, 1 was using Gauge, and 2 were using Profound (both said it takes a lot of effort / team skills, but had impact once used correctly).

> For those selling to senior folks who are likely to be parents, one common thread was how helpful it is to have a fun / playful company mascot... that you can incorporate into swag (like a stuffed animal) that you can give prospects to give to their kids. Suddenly, your company's brand is living in the form of a stuffed animal in your prospect's home.

> Everyone agreed that going all in on one channel (youtube vs linkedin vs tiktok vs X etc) is most effective vs trying to be great at one. Just own one.

> BDRs aren't going away. Most seemed to be doubling down. That said, the inbound SDR role, on the other hand, is going away as AI automates it.

> The role of Mktg Ops is increasingly best suited to people with data science or SQL backgrounds, since AI has automated much of the traditional role.

> Almost everyone is hiring "product archetype" roles: former practitioners from the buyer's world (sometimes even a fmr customer themself) who become a PMM.
