## Abstract

This BBC News Arabic feature profiles Mohamed Salah's journey from a remote Egyptian village to global football icon, tracing his career from Al Mokawloon Al Arab through Basel, Chelsea, Fiorentina, Roma, and finally Liverpool FC. The article examines not only his on-pitch achievements — 257 Liverpool goals, a Champions League, a Premier League title, and multiple domestic honours — but also the disciplined physical and mental regime that sustains his elite performance. Beyond football, it explores Salah's cultural significance: his role as a symbol of Muslim identity in British public life, a reduction in hate crime attributed to his presence, and his status as a national inspiration in Egypt through charitable work in his home village of Nagrig. As Salah prepares to leave Anfield, the piece argues his legacy is inseparable from who he is off the pitch as much as on it.

## TL;DR

- Salah's rise from a nine-hour daily bus commute to training in Cairo to becoming Liverpool's all-time top scorer is rooted in relentless self-improvement and a disciplined recovery and nutrition regime.
- His Muslim identity and humble public persona have made him a rare figure credited with increasing social cohesion and reducing anti-Muslim hate crime in Liverpool.
- Speculation over his next club (Saudi Pro League, MLS, Turkey, Italy) frames his departure from Anfield as the end of one of English football's most remarkable chapters.

## Key claims

- Salah scored 156 goals in his first five seasons under Jürgen Klopp, winning the Champions League, Premier League, three domestic cups, the Super Cup, and the Club World Cup.
- He is among the fastest players in Premier League history, with an official Opta top speed of 36.64 km/h recorded in 2021.
- As an "inverted winger," Salah attacks from the right flank and cuts inside to shoot with his stronger left foot, while also undertaking intensive training to develop his right foot.
- His recovery routine includes at least twice-daily gym sessions, ice baths, yoga, Pilates, and use of a personal hyperbaric oxygen chamber at home.
- Salah meditates 15–20 minutes daily and plays regular chess to maintain mental clarity and decision-making ability.
- His visibility as a devout Muslim at Anfield is credited by commentators with attracting more Muslim fans to the club and contributing to a measurable reduction in hate crime in Liverpool.
- In 2023, Liverpool reportedly rejected a $200m offer from Saudi Pro League club Al-Ittihad for Salah.
- The Mohamed Salah Charity Foundation in Nagrig supports orphans, divorced and widowed women, and the poor and sick in his home community.

Just keep focused, train hard and I'm sure you will play again and be great.'"

> "From the first day I left Egypt for Basel's assessment, I told myself I had to become a different player. I wanted to do a great achievement, to attract attention, and to become a player people love and follow."

— Mohamed Salah, BBC Sport
