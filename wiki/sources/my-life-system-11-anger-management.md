## Abstract

This short personal essay — the eleventh instalment in an ongoing "My Life System" series — reflects on the author's evolving relationship with anger. Drawing on formative observations of his father's temper, repeated incidents with his teenage son Abhishek, and the steadying influence of his wife Bhavana, the author argues that anger is essentially a demonstration of power that generates resentment rather than results. He concludes that calm communication is more impactful than shouting, that displaced frustration is usually the real culprit, and that journaling serves as a practical tool for dissipating momentary anger without suppression.

## TL;DR

- Anger demonstrates power but produces resentment; the same message delivered calmly lands with greater impact.
- Most anger is misdirected — rooted in one's own unreal expectations, imprecise instructions, or personal failings.
- Writing thoughts in a notebook provides closure and dissipates anger without bottling it up.

## Key claims

- Shouting at people who cannot answer back creates helplessness and resentment, not compliance or improvement.
- Angry words are progressively discounted by the recipient; calm delivery retains greater persuasive force.
- The author observes that his disproportionate reactions to his son were typically displaced frustrations originating elsewhere.
- Self-control is identified as a critical learnable trait, developed through reflection and spousal accountability.
- Journaling acts as a surrogate conversation — forcing reflection and providing emotional closure.
- The root cause of most anger can be traced back to the angry person themselves: unreal expectations, imprecise instructions, or fury at one's own mistakes.
- Anger directed outward is rarely warranted; anger directed inward (as honest self-assessment) is the more appropriate response.

## Key passages

> "I have realised that getting angry doesn't really get you anything. It is just a demonstration of power and it ends up creating resentment in the other person."

> "The same thing which is said by shouting can also be said calmly to the other person and the impact is likely to be much greater. After a point, the angry words are just discounted by the person at the receiving end."

> "In almost all cases, I was upset at something else and I found Abhishek to be the victim whom I could unleash my inner frustrations on."

> "I use my notebook to write down my feelings and get closure. I find that writing out my thoughts helps clear the mind; it is almost like talking to someone else."

> "More often than not, we will find that the root cause is perhaps of our own making – unreal expectations, imprecise instructions, or simply, fury at our own follies."
