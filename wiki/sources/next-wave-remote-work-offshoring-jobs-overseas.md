## Abstract

The article argues that the pandemic-era normalization of remote work has catalyzed a new wave of corporate offshoring, in which companies—having learned that skilled work can be done from anywhere—are now moving jobs abroad to cut costs and address persistent U.S. labor shortages. Unlike the previous era of manufacturing globalization, this wave targets high-skilled service roles such as software developers, accountants, risk analysts, and HR professionals. Economists like Stanford's Nicholas Bloom predict 10–20% of U.S. service-support jobs could migrate overseas within the next decade, heralding what Bloom calls "the era of service sector globalization." The trend benefits skilled workers in countries like India, Mexico, Georgia, and Armenia, while posing risks to U.S. office workers and commercial real estate.

## TL;DR

- Companies are offshoring skilled service jobs—not just manufacturing or call-centre roles—as a direct consequence of remote-work infrastructure built during the pandemic.
- Stanford economist Nicholas Bloom estimates 10–20% of U.S. service-support jobs could move overseas within a decade.
- Latin America, the Caucasus, and traditional hubs like India are all benefiting, with hiring platforms like Deel and CodersLink reporting explosive demand growth.

## Key claims

- The pandemic proved to employers that highly skilled roles (engineering, finance, HR, legal) can be performed entirely remotely, removing a psychological barrier to offshoring.
- As of August 2022, 7.3% of U.S. senior managers surveyed by the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta reported moving more jobs abroad due to remote work.
- Deel, an international HR platform, saw hiring through its platform more than double in 2022.
- U.S. immigration backlogs and visa shortages are pushing firms toward hiring abroad rather than importing foreign talent.
- Nicholas Bloom frames 2020 onwards as "the era of service sector globalization," analogous to manufacturing globalization from 1980–2019.
- New offshoring destinations beyond India are emerging, including Tbilisi (Georgia) and Yerevan (Armenia), partly fueled by Russian emigres with high-skill profiles.
- Q2, an Austin-based fintech, moved ~90 jobs to Mexico over one year; approximately 20% of its total workforce is now abroad.
- Rising offshoring is an additional headwind for U.S. office landlords already weakened by remote-work-driven demand collapse.
