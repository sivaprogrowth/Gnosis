## Abstract

This *Economist* Schumpeter column uses [[companies/novo-nordisk|Novo Nordisk]]'s experience with its GLP-1 obesity drugs Ozempic and Wegovy as a case study in managing a sudden, explosive demand surge. The piece argues that Novo's century-long specialisation in diabetes gave it a structural head start in the adjacent obesity market, even as the company stumbled on supply and distribution. Drawing four "golden rules" from Novo's story, the article contends that deep domain focus, accurate market identification, disciplined capacity expansion, and long-term access planning are the keys to thriving in a pharmaceutical gold rush. The column also situates Novo alongside [[companies/eli-lilly]] as co-frontrunner in what could become the biggest-selling drug class in history.

## TL;DR

- [[companies/novo-nordisk|Novo Nordisk]]'s century of diabetes specialisation gave it a structural head start in GLP-1 obesity drugs, even though it mis-timed the demand surge for Ozempic and Wegovy.
- The article distils four lessons: know your domain deeply, know your real market, control capacity deliberately, and plan for long-term access and goodwill.
- The combined market cap of Novo Nordisk and [[companies/eli-lilly]] already exceeds that of AstraZeneca, Moderna, and Pfizer combined, signalling how large the obesity-drug opportunity is perceived to be.

## Key claims

- Ozempic's serendipitous success as a weight-loss drug led to Wegovy becoming the first FDA-approved obesity drug in years, triggering a rapid doubling of Novo Nordisk's market value to ~$326 bn as of early 2023.
- Narrow specialisation — often criticised by analysts — gave Novo Nordisk its competitive advantage; Michael Porter identified Denmark's insulin-exporting prowess as a national competitive advantage as early as 1990.
- Demand for obesity drugs spiked far sooner than for Novo's other products, partly driven by social-media influencers on TikTok, depleting inventories and depriving patients of needed medication.
- A contracted filling site in Europe malfunctioned during the demand surge, exposing the risks of outsourced capacity; Novo responded by bringing two additional filling sites online and doubling capital expenditure for two consecutive years.
- New active-ingredient factories in the US and Denmark take five years to build and cost up to $2.5 bn each, creating a durable barrier to entry that rewards steady investment over frenzied expansion.
- Americans earning less than $15,000 a year carry the highest BMIs, according to a Jefferies survey, making affordability and insurer coverage a political and strategic risk for Novo if left unaddressed.
- [[companies/eli-lilly]]'s Mounjaro was expected to seek FDA approval for obesity in 2023, setting up a two-horse race analogous to the covid-19 vaccine competition.
- GLP-1-related drugs are being explored beyond obesity and diabetes for cardiovascular and other related diseases, suggesting obesity may underpin another 100-year business cycle for Novo.

## Key passages

> "In America alone, 110m people like Mr Ingram, many on low incomes, suffer from obesity. They need help getting into shape. Novo Nordisk is their new port of call."

> "The combined market capitalisation of Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly easily eclipses that of AstraZeneca, Moderna and Pfizer put together."

> "One of the beauties of the firm, whose founders first made insulin in Denmark in the 1920s, is specialisation… That industrial focus gave Novo a head start on obesity."

> "This served as a reminder of the dangerous distractions of the hype cycle. So now the firm is going back to basics. It is focusing on customers with a body-mass index (BMI) over 30… It is working with doctors to ensure that they prescribe the drug correctly."

> "Companies as clever as Amazon learned during the pandemic that excessive faith in a 'new normal' leads to overcapacity."

> "To avoid a political backlash, it is important that those who need them most can access them. In order for obesity drugs to extend to other diseases, such as cardiovascular ones, it will be crucial to maintain goodwill."
