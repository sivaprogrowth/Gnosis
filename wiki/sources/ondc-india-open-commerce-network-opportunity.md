## Abstract

This article by Susmit Patodia (Capital Director, Antler India) provides an extensive analysis of India's Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) — a government-backed set of open protocols designed to unbundle and democratise e-commerce in the same way UPI transformed digital payments. The report argues that ONDC's core innovation lies in decoupling the four stages of commerce (discovery, assortment, fulfillment, and governance) and making them interoperable across millions of buyers, sellers, logistics providers, and technology players. Unlike traditional platform e-commerce — which the author characterises as "winner-take-all" — ONDC is designed to catalyse hundreds of niche unicorns rather than a handful of decacorns, and could add $60–80 billion to India's economy by 2030. The piece combines strategic analysis with direct quotes from network participants including ONDC's CEO and COO, and founders of startups building across every layer of the network.

## TL;DR

- ONDC is an open, interoperable protocol stack that unbundles commerce into discovery, assortment, fulfillment, and governance — enabling any player to participate in any layer without platform lock-in.
- It faces a harder cold-start challenge than UPI (63 million SMBs vs. ~50 banks) but, once past the inflection point, could self-sustain e-retail penetration far beyond the current 5–7%.
- The network is expected to catalyse hundreds of niche unicorns, democratise business intelligence for SMEs, and enable flow-based lending — representing an $80B+ economic opportunity by 2030.

## Key claims

- India's e-retail penetration sits at just 5–7%, despite years of platform e-commerce growth, because incumbent platforms became monolithic and locked participants in.
- ONDC is built on two core principles: **unbundling** (breaking commerce into discrete, independently operable steps) and **interoperability** (open protocols so any participant can plug into any step).
- ONDC is open-source *and* open-access — unlike UPI, which is open-source but requires bank tie-ups; no permission or clearance is needed to build a fully functional ONDC application.
- The cold-start problem is ONDC's biggest near-term challenge: seller-side apps that onboard and digitise merchants are the critical unlock, since broad catalog supply must precede buyer demand.
- Breaking data silos via open network data will give brands direct access to buyer intelligence and enable flow-based lending for SMEs that lack traditional collateral.
- The Namma Yatri autorickshaw app — built on the Beckn Protocol (the same protocol foundation as ONDC) — reached ~6,000 rides/day, 2 lakh users, and 35,000 drivers within three months with little to no marketing spend, validating the decentralised model.
- ONDC's API Specifications are published as a Digital Public Good under a Creative Commons licence, with the explicit goal of enabling cross-border interoperability between similar national networks globally.
- The winners in an ONDC world will be "profit-first founders" who can operate at the intersection of digital protocols and physical logistics execution ("bits and atoms").

## Key passages

> ONDC is a common language of commerce: ONDC is not a platform killer or even an e-commerce website, it is a set of protocols that enables various players across a commerce transaction across the country to talk to each other and fulfill a transaction.

> What ONDC can do for commerce can go beyond what UPI did for payments. ONDC scope is much broader, enabling both products and services. There is tremendous room for innovation and value creation for startups, product builders, on-the-ground operators, as well as for large and conventional players and investors to come together and reimagine one of the most fundamental aspects of our society.
>
> — T. Koshy, Chief Executive Officer, ONDC

> Within three months of launching, Namma Yatri is doing close to 6000 rides a day with over 2 lakh users and 35,000 auto drivers, all with little or no marketing spends. This is an initiative that was taken up and driven by the auto-rickshaw drivers and shows the power of unbundling driven adoption.
>
> — Sujith Nair, CEO & Co-Founder FIDE

> If UPI and Aadhaar are the digital equivalents of building public infrastructure like roads, then building ONDC is akin to building a national scale open-city of commerce where buyers, sellers, transporters, and everyone else can come together and interact.
>
> — Nitin Sharma, Partner at Antler

> ONDC is a population scale network where more than $100B of commerce is expected to happen with more than 500 million customers and 15 million sellers.
>
> — Manoj Gupta, Founder and CEO, Plotch.ai

> Joining the ONDC network will be the best strategic bet for these businesses to not just sustain but flourish amidst the ever-increasing competition in the e-commerce landscape.
>
> — Saahil Goel, Co-founder & CEO, Shiprocket
