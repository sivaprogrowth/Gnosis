## Abstract

This *Economist* article examines the growing phenomenon of "overemployment" — tech workers secretly holding two (or more) full-time remote jobs simultaneously — framed through the case study of an anonymous data scientist called Matt. The piece argues that remote work is the key enabler: the share of tech workers fully remote remains 60% higher than in other sectors, removing the in-person oversight that previously made dual employment impractical. Workers cite boredom, insufficient workload, financial motivation, and job-security concerns as drivers, while generative AI tools like [[companies/openai|ChatGPT]] are both a future threat to this practice (by automating menial tasks) and a present enabler (by helping overemployees complete work faster). The article frames overemployment as a power rebalancing, with workers reclaiming leverage in a historically employer-favoured relationship.

## TL;DR

- Remote work has enabled a cohort of tech workers to hold two or more full-time jobs simultaneously without employers' knowledge.
- Low workload demands (Matt's first job required only ~8 hours/week) and AI coding assistants make juggling jobs feasible.
- Overemployees view dual employment as a financial safety net and a way to reclaim power in an asymmetric labour market.

## Key claims

- The share of tech workers fully remote is approximately 60% higher than in other sectors, creating the conditions for covert dual employment.
- America's Bureau of Labour Statistics has recorded 4–6.5% of the US workforce holding multiple jobs at any point over the past 30 years; Census Bureau estimates run higher, rising from 6.8% (1996) to 7.8% (2018).
- Mid-career software engineers reportedly apply for more junior positions at a second employer to "underpromise and overdeliver" with minimal effort.
- Online communities on [[companies/reddit|Reddit]] dedicated to "overemployed" workers share tactics such as attending two simultaneous video calls, faking connectivity issues, or playing pre-recorded audio to mask divided attention.
- Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) can, according to one overemployee, complete approximately 90% of a software engineer's work, lowering the effort required to maintain dual jobs.
- The same AI advances may eventually make overemployment harder by replacing the lower-skill tech tasks that currently leave workers with excess capacity.
- Overemployees frame the practice as a power-rebalancing strategy: dual income provides flexibility and negotiating leverage against potential layoffs or unreasonable demands.

## Key passages

> "Without managers physically looking over their shoulders, some of them are two-timing their employers. Mid-career software engineers report applying for more junior positions so that they can 'underpromise and overdeliver', with minimal effort."

> "The tasks required by his first job, working remotely as a data scientist for a medium-sized tech firm, were not particularly challenging, taking him only eight hours a week."

> "The rise of generative artificial intelligence like ChatGPT may in time make double-jobbing harder by replacing some menial tech tasks. Until then, coasters can themselves use clever chatbots to help structure computer code, write documents and even conduct preliminary research."

> "ChatGPT cannot replace the work of a software engineer, says one overemployee, but it gets you 90% of the way there."

> "The employee-employer relationship has historically favoured the employers, who wield more clout because they can typically choose from more workers than workers can among companies. Matt thinks of his ruse as taking back some control."
