## Abstract

This article by Rajesh Jain introduces a series on modern marketing, anchored by foundational quotes from [[peter-drucker|Peter Drucker]] and [[theodore-levitt|Theodore Levitt]] that distinguish marketing from selling and position it as the defining function of any business. It contrasts traditional marketing — centred on media-driven demand creation and physical distribution — with modern marketing's complexity: digital channels, search engines, social media, influencers, direct-to-consumer models, and ratings/reviews. The article frames the modern marketer's challenge as a series of "OR Conundrums" (ORCs): forced choices between competing priorities arising from constraints on budget, time, and resources. The series intends to examine each such trade-off, acknowledging that while the ideal answer is often "AND," real-world limits demand decisive choices.

## TL;DR

- Modern marketing has become far more complex than traditional media-and-distribution marketing, adding digital channels, influencers, marketplaces, and ratings into every part of the value chain.
- Marketers routinely face "OR Conundrums" (ORCs) — binary strategic trade-offs forced by limited budget and time — that are easy to state but hard to execute well.
- The article frames marketing, following Drucker and Levitt, as the core purpose of business: creating conditions for a customer to choose to exchange money for value.

## Key claims

- Peter Drucker argued that marketing and innovation are the only two basic functions of a business; everything else is a cost.
- Theodore Levitt distinguished marketing from selling by orientation: selling serves the seller's need to convert product into cash, whereas marketing serves the buyer's needs.
- Traditional consumer marketing centred on demand creation through memorable ads in print or TV, paired with physical distribution to fulfil that demand.
- Over the past 25 years, digital transformation has changed every part of the marketing value chain, from outreach (search, email, social, influencers) to distribution (marketplaces, D2C).
- Ratings and reviews now play a meaningful role in the purchase decision alongside traditional and digital advertising.
- Modern marketing is more customer-oriented, customisable, device-agnostic, and adaptable to trends compared with traditional product-centric approaches.
- The author identifies at least ten recurring OR Conundrums for B2C/D2C marketers, including: existing vs. new customers, [[build-vs-buy|build or buy]], broadcast vs. personalise, growth vs. profit, and SEO vs. paid search.
- The preferred answer to each ORC is "AND," but budget, time, and resource constraints force trade-offs that are often obvious in theory yet difficult in execution.

## Key passages

> "The soul of a business is marketing. Whether it is B2B or B2C, marketing is about conveying the value provided by the product, which in turn makes a customer decide that an exchange of money in return for the product is a transaction that's worth doing."

> "The difference between marketing and selling is more than semantic. Selling focuses on the needs of the seller, marketing on the needs of the buyer." — Theodore Levitt

> "Because the purpose of business is to create a customer, the business enterprise has two–and only two–basic functions: marketing and innovation. Marketing and innovation produce results; all the rest are costs." — Peter Drucker

> "While traditional marketing's focal point is the company's product or service, modern marketing is more customer-oriented… Since modern marketing uses a digital platform, it's more customisable. It can also easily adapt to change, so it has no problem keeping up with the latest marketing trends."

> "In this series, I will consider the modern marketer's ORCs (OR conundrums). Of course, the preferred option is an AND but because of limits on budget, time and other resources, marketing – like life – is about choices and trade-offs."
