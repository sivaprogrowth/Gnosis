## Abstract

This is the opening tweet (1/16) of a thread by Sridhar Ramaswamy (@RamaswmySridhar) arguing that the AI industry is making a structurally unusual bet: locking in multi-year compute purchases while monetising the resulting capacity by selling inference on a per-token basis. Ramaswamy frames this risk structure through analogies to several established industries — banks, commodity merchants, airlines, utilities, and cloud providers — each of which illuminates a distinct dimension of the underlying financial and operational exposure. The thread promises to unpack what these industry parallels reveal about the AI compute-to-inference business model.

## TL;DR

- The AI industry commits capital to compute over long horizons but earns revenue in tiny, variable increments (per token sold).
- Five industry analogies — banking, commodity trading, airlines, utilities, and cloud — are proposed as lenses for understanding this risk.
- The mismatch between fixed long-duration asset spend and variable short-duration revenue is the core structural tension.

## Key claims

- The AI industry's business model can be characterised as "buy compute for years, sell intelligence by the token."
- This creates a structural mismatch between capital expenditure timelines and revenue realisation timelines.
- Banks offer one analogy — likely around duration/maturity mismatch and leverage.
- Commodity merchants offer another analogy — likely around inventory risk and price volatility of a raw input.
- Airlines offer another analogy — likely around high fixed-cost infrastructure with perishable, variable-demand capacity.
- Utilities offer another analogy — likely around regulated or semi-regulated capital-intensive infrastructure with usage-based billing.
- Cloud providers offer another analogy — likely as the most direct precedent, having navigated similar capex-vs-consumption tensions.
- The framing implies that understanding AI industry risk requires drawing on financial and operational models from multiple legacy sectors simultaneously.
