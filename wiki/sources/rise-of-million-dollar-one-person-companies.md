## Abstract

AI tools are enabling a new class of solo entrepreneurs to launch and scale companies to millions of dollars in revenue without hiring any employees. Data from [[companies/stripe|Stripe]] shows that solo operators generating over $1 million in revenue on its platform doubled between 2023 and 2025, while those crossing $10 million nearly tripled. A Harvard Business School study of 50,000 startups found that AI-focused firms operate with 25% fewer employees on average. The article profiles several solo founders and economists to explore how AI lowers the barrier to launching a business while simultaneously raising questions about net effects on employment and competitive moat.

## TL;DR

- [[companies/stripe|Stripe]] data shows solo operators earning $1M+ doubled in two years, with AI absorbing coding, customer support, marketing, and administrative tasks.
- AI dramatically lowers the cost and complexity of starting a business, but competitive moats are thin because the same tools are available to anyone.
- The macro labor-market effect is uncertain: fewer employees per firm, but potentially far more firms.

## Key claims

- The number of solo operators on Stripe's platform generating over $1 million in revenue doubled between 2023 and 2025; those crossing $10 million nearly tripled.
- Among 50,000 startups studied by Harvard Business School researchers, AI-focused startups employed roughly 25% fewer people than comparable non-AI startups.
- New business applications in the information sector have seen nearly a 45% increase over the past year (per Census Bureau data analyzed by Bank of America Institute), while planned hiring rates in that sector have fallen the sharpest of any measured industry.
- [[companies/anthropic|Anthropic]]'s usage-based pricing meant one solo founder was initially losing money on customers; he switched to free open-source models from China to manage costs.
- A San Francisco accelerator for solo founders attracted 4,500 applicants for 10 slots — nearly five times its first-cycle volume — signalling a surge of solo-founder interest.
- Founders warn that easy AI access cuts both ways: if launching is easy, so is copying, undermining competitive differentiation.
- A soft hiring environment and post-pandemic burnout are cited alongside AI capability as motivators for solo entrepreneurship, though not all attempts succeed.
