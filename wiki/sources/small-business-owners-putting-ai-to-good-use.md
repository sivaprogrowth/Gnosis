## Abstract

This *Wall Street Journal* article profiles small-business owners who are deploying generative AI tools — primarily [[companies/openai|ChatGPT]] and Google's NotebookLM and Gemini — to perform tasks that would otherwise require additional staff or expensive external hires, such as financial analysis, customer-service email handling, website development, and marketing content creation. Drawing on a U.S. Chamber of Commerce report finding that 58% of small businesses now use generative AI (up from 40% in 2024 and more than double two years prior), the piece argues that small businesses are experimenting pragmatically and independently, without the formal AI strategy teams that large corporations deploy. The article highlights both the productivity gains (one bar manager instead of two, no junior engineers needed) and ongoing challenges (AI hallucinations requiring human review). It matters as a ground-level snapshot of how [[concepts/ai-transformation]] is playing out at the smallest scale of the economy.

## TL;DR

- Generative AI adoption among U.S. small businesses has surged to 58% in 2025, more than doubling in two years, driven by practical, self-directed experimentation.
- Small-business owners are using AI as a de facto CFO, customer-service rep, web developer, and marketing team — reducing headcount in the process.
- [[concepts/ai-hallucination-legal-filings|AI hallucinations]] remain a real operational risk; one cooking-school owner spent months manually reviewing AI email replies before trusting 90% to go out automatically.

## Key claims

- 58% of approximately 3,800 small businesses surveyed by the U.S. Chamber of Commerce use generative AI as of August 2025, up from 40% in 2024 and more than double the figure from two years prior.
- Mike Salvatore (Heritage Hospitality Group, Chicago) uses ChatGPT for cost-of-goods analysis every three weeks instead of once or twice a year, and credits AI with eliminating the need for a second bar manager and an event planner role.
- Salvatore feeds point-of-sale and QuickBooks data into Google's NotebookLM, which generates a podcast-style business briefing shared with his management team.
- Manville Chan (Story of Ramen, San Francisco) built a two-model pipeline — Google Gemini for interpreting incoming customer emails and [[companies/openai|ChatGPT]] for drafting replies — that now auto-sends roughly 90% of responses after a learning curve of months.
- Chan's system initially hallucinated significantly (e.g., agreeing to host house-call classes the school does not offer), underscoring that AI accuracy requires iterative prompt refinement.
- Jeff Taxdahl (Thread Logic, Minneapolis suburb) used AI-generated code to modify a rigid hosting-platform template, avoiding the cost of hiring a developer.
- Dustin Bruzenak (Modern Logic, Minneapolis) converts a biweekly podcast into a full multi-channel marketing campaign using AI, eliminating the need for a dedicated marketer and junior engineers.
- Small businesses operate without formal AI strategy teams, making them "scrappier" and more experimental in adoption, according to a Google vice president running the Grow with Google initiative.
