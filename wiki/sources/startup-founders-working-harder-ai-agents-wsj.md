## Abstract

This Wall Street Journal article examines how the rise of capable [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] — systems that autonomously plan and execute multistep tasks such as pulling data and writing code — is paradoxically increasing the workload of startup founders rather than reducing it. The piece profiles founders like Aditya Sharma who are pulling all-nighters to keep pace with the output and demands of their generative-AI bot crews. It explores a cultural shift in early-stage startups where human founders must remain perpetually available to supervise, direct, and integrate the work of AI systems operating at speed. The article raises questions about the nature of human-AI collaboration and the sustainability of this new mode of work.

## TL;DR

- [[concepts/agentic-ai|AI agents]] capable of multistep autonomous tasks are accelerating startup output but also dramatically increasing the supervision burden on founders.
- Rather than replacing founder effort, generative-AI bot crews are creating a new kind of always-on work culture in early-stage companies.
- The article frames this as a new meaning of "working yourself to the bone" — one driven by keeping up with machines rather than markets.

## Key claims

- Startup founders are working longer and more irregular hours as a direct consequence of managing AI agent workflows.
- Aditya Sharma, a startup founder, stayed up until 6 a.m. managing a crew of generative-AI bots executing multistep tasks.
- Generative-AI agents are described as systems that plan and execute multistep tasks including pulling data and writing code.
- The growing capabilities of AI agents are redefining what it means to be a high-effort founder, shifting the bottleneck from human execution to human oversight.
- The human founder role is evolving toward continuous supervision and direction of autonomous AI systems rather than direct task execution.
