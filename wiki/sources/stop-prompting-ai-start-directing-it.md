## Abstract

The article argues that the most valuable output of professional work is not faster analysis or better summaries, but genuine insight — novel connections and reframings that change how a problem is understood. The authors contend that while conversational AI (prompt-and-response) can surface competing interpretations, [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]] — configured and directed rather than conversed with — enables a qualitatively deeper form of discovery because it can hold large volumes of data and sustain analytical orientations across entire datasets. This demands a distinct professional skill the authors call *directing intelligence*: knowing how to design AI systems for insight and how to make sense of what they reveal. The piece draws on two research streams: a qualitative study of AI-assisted abductive discovery and an organisational theory framing of algorithmic systems as assemblages shaped by data access, configured capabilities, and distributed agency.

## TL;DR

- Conversational AI (prompting) is bounded by what humans think to ask; [[concepts/agentic-ai|agentic AI]] sustains analysis across full datasets without losing the thread, enabling deeper insight discovery.
- Professional expertise creates cognitive blind spots — agentic AI can probe the edges of those frames in ways that conversations cannot.
- The critical new skill is *directing intelligence*: designing agentic systems for insight rather than merely prompting or automating them.

## Key claims

- The most valuable professional output is genuine insight — a new way of seeing a problem or a pattern nobody has named — not faster analysis or better summaries.
- Professional expertise is double-edged: it enables effective problem-framing but simultaneously constrains what practitioners look for, making certain insights structurally hard to reach.
- Conversational AI can surface competing interpretations and expose gaps, but remains bounded by what the human supplies and thinks to ask.
- [[concepts/agentic-ai|Agentic AI]] systems — configured and directed — can hold more data and sustain analytical orientations across entire datasets, enabling discovery moves of greater depth than prompted conversations.
- The authors identify four pathways by which AI generates surprising insights during analytical work (from their qualitative study published in *Strategic Organization*, 2026).
- Organisations can be understood as algorithmic assemblages shaped by what data is made accessible, what capabilities are configured, and how agency is distributed.
- *Directing intelligence* is a distinct professional skill, separate from both prompting and automation, centred on designing systems for insight and interpreting their outputs.

## Key passages

> The most valuable thing a professional produces is not a faster analysis or a better summary. It is insight: a genuinely new way of seeing a problem, a connection that changes how they understand a situation, or a pattern that nobody has named.

> What makes this kind of discovery hard is that expertise, the very thing that makes professionals effective, also makes certain kinds of insight difficult to reach. The frame that lets them see a problem clearly also shapes what they look for — and what they stop looking for.

> Unlike a prompted conversation that is bounded by what a human supplies and thinks to ask, an agentic system holds more data and sustains analytical orientations across entire data sets without losing the thread. The discovery moves are the same; the depth is not.

> Using agentic AI this way demands a professional skill different from both prompting and automation: knowing how to design systems for insight and how to make sense of what they reveal. We call it *directing intelligence*.
