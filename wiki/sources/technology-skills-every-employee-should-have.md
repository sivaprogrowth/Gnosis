## Abstract

This Wall Street Journal article surveys the core technology competencies that employers now expect from all office workers, not just technical specialists. Drawing on interviews with educators, employers, staffing firms, and analysts — including voices from Columbia Business School, ZipRecruiter, [[gartner|Gartner]], and [[microsoft|Microsoft]] — the piece argues that the bar for baseline digital proficiency has risen sharply. Workers must go beyond surface-level familiarity with productivity suites and demonstrate competency in data visualisation, project management platforms, robotic process automation, basic coding, and AI-assisted workflows. The article is notable for grounding its claims in concrete tool rankings from LinkedIn Learning, Udemy, and Credly certification data.

## TL;DR

- Employers now expect proficiency in data visualisation (Power BI, Tableau), advanced spreadsheets, project management software, and AI tools — not just Word and Excel basics.
- No-code automation (e.g., [[microsoft|Microsoft]] Power Automate) and light coding skills (Python, SQL) are increasingly valued even for non-technical roles.
- AI tools like ChatGPT and [[microsoft|Microsoft]] Copilot are rapidly becoming standard workplace utilities, and workers should seek 80%+ efficiency gains by applying them to repetitive tasks.

## Key claims

- The average office worker uses 11 applications on the job, and 17% use 16 or more apps, according to [[gartner|Gartner]]'s 2022 Digital Worker Survey.
- [[microsoft|Microsoft]] Power BI and [[salesforce|Salesforce]]-owned Tableau are the leading data-visualisation programs; Power BI Essential Training was the 13th most-popular LinkedIn Learning course in 2022.
- Excel remains foundational: it was LinkedIn Learning's No. 2 course and accounts for six of the top 10 office-productivity skills on Udemy.
- Advanced communication platform features — such as Microsoft Teams channels, Slack integrations, and mass email management — are distinct skills employers expect beyond basic meeting participation.
- Project-management tools such as Asana, Trello, ClickUp, and Smartsheet are among the top skills added to LinkedIn profiles, and project management is the fifth most popular tech certification per Credly.
- Robotic process automation (RPA) with no-code tools like [[microsoft|Microsoft]] Power Automate — the fastest-growing office-productivity skill on Udemy in 2022 — lets workers automate repetitive tasks without deep programming knowledge.
- Python and SQL are recommended even for non-engineers; over half of Columbia Business School students used Python in class at time of writing.
- AI tools such as ChatGPT, Stable Diffusion, and [[microsoft|Microsoft]] Copilot (powered by [[openai|OpenAI]]'s GPT-4) are lowering the barrier to AI adoption; workers are advised to target 80% efficiency gains on high-frequency tasks.

## Key passages

> "There is no debate as to whether technology is not just an enabler, but is really a driver of disruption, of change, of value, within organizations. And as a result, [there] needs to be some core knowledge that people need to bring with them, even if they're not going to be technologists themselves."
> — Costis Maglaras, Dean, Columbia Business School

> "People just have higher expectations now when they receive a document or a deck. They want it to tell a compelling story. They want it to have a professional look and feel."
> — Julia Pollak, Chief Economist, ZipRecruiter

> "The name of the game right now is efficiencies. What do we need to do to be more efficient? Which means the automation of a lot of different things."
> — Mike Steinitz, Senior Executive Director, Robert Half

> "Think about things that you do often, and apply AI to that task to see if you can find 80% efficiency gains on something that you don't want to be an expert in. Like, I don't want to be an expert email writer."
> — Allie K. Miller, AI investor and adviser

> "When we look at Microsoft Teams specifically, people are using chat and meetings, no problem. What they're not doing is using the most advanced services, such as channels for discussing specific topics or projects within a team."
> — Joe Mariano, Analyst, [[gartner|Gartner]]
