## Abstract

"Thinks 814" is a curated link-and-excerpt newsletter post by Rajesh Jain that pulls together five short readings spanning geopolitics, entrepreneurial conviction, AI's impact on content creation, the evolving scope of design, and leadership tensions for CEOs. The Indonesia excerpt argues the country is dramatically under-appreciated by the American public despite its outsized strategic, demographic, and economic importance — especially as a fulcrum of U.S.-China competition. The AI-content piece warns that when AI drives content-creation costs to zero, power will increasingly concentrate among companies with gatekeeper-free acquisition channels. The design and leadership excerpts round out the issue with calls for broader systemic thinking in both fields.

## TL;DR

- Indonesia is strategically critical (Straits of Malacca, nickel/cobalt reserves, largest Muslim-majority nation) yet largely ignored by the American public.
- AI-driven content costs approaching zero will concentrate power in companies that own direct consumer acquisition and will deepen "addiction" dynamics.
- Effective CEOs must manage feedback loops, long-horizon thinking, and sounding-board use even while avoiding fear and panic.

## Key claims

- Indonesia has seven times as many Muslims as Saudi Arabia and is the world's largest producer of nickel, making it a pivotal resource and geopolitical actor.
- China's energy supply routes pass through the Straits of Malacca, which Indonesia effectively dominates, raising the country's strategic value in U.S.-China competition.
- Entrepreneurial success stories share a common thread of "conviction" — the persistent belief that sustained focus and effort will eventually pay off.
- When AI automates content creation costs to zero, power accrues to companies with novel, gatekeeper-free acquisition methods.
- AI content tools will exacerbate the "addiction" dynamic already required of consumers for businesses to win.
- Design must travel "upstream" — into legal decisions, resource allocation, and institutional culture — not just downstream into fabrication and prototyping.
- The Post-it note became the replicable aesthetic symbol of [[concepts/design-thinking]], enabling its mass spread through organisations like IDEO.
- CEOs must actively maintain upward feedback channels, use internal and external sounding boards, and build talent depth to navigate downturns and create new market opportunities.

## Key passages

> "It's hard to think of another country as big and important as Indonesia that is so completely ignored by the American public… Little wonder that Indonesia has become a fulcrum of U.S.-China competition."

> "The one word I consistently wrote down in my notepad as I heard each person speak was 'conviction'… When the going gets tough we have two choices – quit or stay the course. Our level of conviction determines which route we go."

> "When AI automates content creation costs to zero, the effects will be far-reaching. More and more power will accrue in those companies that have novel acquisition methods that do not rely on any gatekeeper… These tools will only exacerbate that dynamic."

> "From the continuous reuse of materials in a 'circular' economy, through a shift in architecture's focus to adaptive reuse, to the redesign of food away from an unsustainable focus on meat, we must reshape not just objects but also the culture and institutions that create them."

> "Have you created an environment where your team and employees are readily giving you feedback and sharing news that you may not want to hear? Are you thinking beyond the near term and preparing the organization for potential difficulties without creating fear and panic?"
