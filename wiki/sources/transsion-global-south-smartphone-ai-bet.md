## Abstract

This *Economist* article profiles Transsion, a Chinese company that dominates smartphone sales across the global south — particularly Africa, India, and South-East Asia — by offering low-cost, locally tailored devices through an extensive distributor network. After years of rapid growth following its 2019 Shanghai IPO, Transsion now faces stagnating revenue, intensifying competition from rivals such as [[companies/samsung-electronics]] deploying agentic AI, and sharply rising memory-chip costs. In response, the company is diversifying beyond handsets into wearables, appliances, and electric vehicles, while making a major AI bet — earmarking two-fifths of a fresh $390m Hong Kong listing for AI models and applications. The article argues that Transsion's next act will determine whether its formula for the global south can survive a more expensive, AI-driven hardware era.

## TL;DR

- Transsion is the volume leader in global-south smartphones (170m handsets in 2025) but faces slowing sales and a ~50% share-price decline since April 2024.
- Rising memory-chip costs and competition from AI-enabled rivals like [[companies/samsung-electronics]] are squeezing the business.
- Transsion is diversifying into EVs and consumer electronics while betting $156m of fresh capital on AI models and applications.

## Key claims

- Transsion sold roughly 170 million handsets in 2025, about two-thirds as many as [[companies/apple]], with over half of all phones purchased in Africa being Transsion devices.
- The company's revenue grew 170% from 2019 to 2024, but has stagnated since; its share price has fallen roughly 50% since April 2024.
- Memory chips accounted for nearly half of Transsion's product costs in early 2026, up from one-fifth in 2023, forcing price increases that reduced shipment volumes.
- In Brazil, [[companies/samsung-electronics]] shipments rose 10% year-on-year in H1 2026 while Transsion's Infinix brand fell 24%, according to Counterpoint Research.
- Transsion operates across 100+ countries via 2,900 distributors and manufactures ~40% of phones in six owned factories (four in China, one in Bangladesh, one in Ethiopia).
- Non-handset products (wearables, appliances, energy storage, electric vehicles) made up 16% of revenue in H1 2026, double the share from two years prior.
- Transsion raised $390m in a Hong Kong stock listing and plans to use two-fifths (~$156m) on AI models and applications; a fifth of employees are already in R&D.
- The company is collaborating with Chinese research institutions on visual foundation models and voice recognition.
