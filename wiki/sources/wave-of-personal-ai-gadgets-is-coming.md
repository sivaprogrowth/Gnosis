## Abstract

This article, from *The Economist*'s "The World Ahead 2027" issue, argues that the dominant paradigm of AI interaction — typing into a chatbot — is giving way to a new generation of physical gadgets designed to weave AI into daily life. Major tech players including [[companies/amazon|Amazon]], [[companies/meta|Meta]], [[companies/openai|OpenAI]], and [[companies/apple|Apple]] are racing to introduce devices such as smart glasses, key-fob voice assistants, badge-worn cameras, and hockey-puck-shaped controllers to displace the smartphone as the primary personal computing interface. The article also highlights a parallel shift toward running AI models locally on-device (the network "edge"), enabled by open-weight models such as Qwen, Llama, and GLM, as a cheaper, faster, and more private alternative to cloud-based inference. The author argues 2027 will be a pivotal year for both AI hardware form factors and the edge computing trend.

## TL;DR

- Tech giants are launching a wave of AI-native gadgets — glasses, key fobs, badges, and puck-shaped devices — explicitly designed to challenge the smartphone as the primary personal device.
- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]] (with Jony Ive) and [[companies/meta|Meta]] are both releasing key-fob-style voice AI devices in late 2026/2027, while [[companies/apple|Apple]] is reportedly developing a badge device and AI-enabled earbuds.
- Local ("edge") AI inference using open-weight models is becoming a credible alternative to cloud AI, trading set-up friction for speed, privacy, and zero API cost.

## Key claims

- [[companies/amazon|Amazon]]'s Alexa+ and [[companies/meta|Meta]]'s smart glasses (co-developed with EssilorLuxottica) are the first major consumer AI gadgets already on the market.
- Meta's Muse Charm — a key-fob device providing voice access to the Muse personal AI agent — is expected to launch in December 2026.
- [[companies/openai|OpenAI]] has partnered with designer Sir Jony Ive to produce a hockey-puck-shaped, display-less AI device that controls appliances, plays music, and answers questions.
- Sam Altman explicitly positions OpenAI's device as an antidote to the attention-extracting nature of smartphones.
- [[companies/apple|Apple]] is reportedly developing a badge-like AI device with camera and microphone to act as supplementary senses for an iPhone running an upgraded Siri.
- Current AI gadgets still rely almost entirely on remote data-centre inference; local edge AI is a nascent but growing alternative.
- Open-weight models (Qwen, Llama, GLM) already allow capable desktop machines to run AI locally, rivalling cloud models for some tasks — fast, private, and free to run.
- 2027 is framed as the decisive year for observing whether edge AI and new device form factors gain meaningful traction.

## Key passages

> "Tech firms think that, for consumers, the future lies not with text interfaces but with gadgets that weave AI into daily life. Ideas about what form such a device might take vary—a badge, a key fob, a smart speaker or a pair of glasses. But all hope to dethrone the smartphone as the main personal device."

> "Sam Altman, Openai's boss, has contrasted the device with smartphones and their endless demands for attention. Using his firm's gadget, he says, should feel like 'sitting in the most beautiful cabin by a lake'."

> "Where possible, handling AI requests on devices close to the user (the so-called network 'edge') can be cheaper and quicker."

> "Running models locally is fast, private and free (because the models are 'open-weight' ones such as Qwen, Llama or GLM)."

> "With new AI devices and local AI models, the edge of the network is worth watching in 2027."
