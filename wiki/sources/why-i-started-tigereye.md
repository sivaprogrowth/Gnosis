## Abstract

In this personal essay, Tracy Young — co-founder and CEO of TigerEye — explains the motivations behind her new company, a stealth-stage B2B sales software venture. She draws on her experience as CEO of PlanGrid and her subsequent work at Y Combinator, where she observed the acute scarcity of female enterprise founders, a gap made worse by the COVID-19 pandemic's disproportionate impact on women in the workforce. Young argues that founding TigerEye is an act of activism: normalising women in enterprise CEO roles and demonstrating that effective leadership is not gendered. A secondary motivation is fixing broken enterprise software — specifically building sales tools that help companies with the best products compete against those with the largest sales teams.

## TL;DR

- Tracy Young founded TigerEye as both a business bet (better sales software for enterprises) and a deliberate act of gender-equity activism.
- She argues the best product does not automatically win in enterprise; companies with the largest sales teams do — TigerEye aims to fix that.
- Shared domestic responsibility and workplace time flexibility are foundational cultural commitments at TigerEye, not afterthoughts.

## Key claims

- Female founders secured only 2% of U.S. venture capital in 2021, and enterprise tech has even fewer women in top executive roles than consumer or health tech.
- COVID-19 accelerated the exit of millions of women from the workforce, widening pre-existing gender disparities.
- Young frames founding TigerEye as personal activism: she believes a world where ≥50% of decision-makers are women would be materially better.
- Role models are essential for entry into previously male-dominated fields; Young credits Karen Hansen and Julia Hartz as her own catalysts.
- The key lesson from PlanGrid was that the best product does not win in enterprise — the largest sales team does.
- After PlanGrid was acquired by [[companies/salesforce|Autodesk]], integrating enterprise point solutions slowed the team down, revealing a gap in business software quality.
- TigerEye's operating model requires shared equal childcare at home and time-flexible, meeting-light work culture as structural prerequisites for women in leadership.
- Young believes the next generation of workers ("TikTok generation") will refuse to continue using legacy enterprise solutions, creating a market opening.

## Key passages

> "Founding TigerEye is activism for me. Being a great founder and a great leader is not dependent on gender. But when the leadership of an entire industry is predominantly male, the world is simply missing out."

> "The most surprising lesson I learned from building PlanGrid was that the best product doesn't necessarily win in enterprise, but those with the largest sales team will."

> "The only way I can be successful as a co-founder, CEO and mother of three is having a partner who shares 50 percent of the child care duties with me. I cannot stress enough how important equality is in the workforce and in the household."

> "I took that anger and focused on building again."

> "I would have never become a construction engineer and I would have never become a founder had I not had role models like Karen Hansen (a builder) and Julia Hartz (a founder). I looked at them and thought, 'I'd like to do that too.'"
