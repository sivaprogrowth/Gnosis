## Abstract

This article is a partial transcript of a conversation between Patrick and Ben Thompson on Colossus, revisiting themes from an earlier discussion that centred on aggregation theory, now updated to the drastically changed AI landscape. The central argument advanced by Ben Thompson is that a decisive US victory in the "AI race" — particularly one yielding military and national-security superiority — would be deeply dangerous rather than desirable. Thompson grounds this concern in game-theory reasoning: a dominant US AI position would rationally incentivise adversaries such as China to take catastrophic pre-emptive actions, such as destroying TSMC. He identifies a fundamental disconnect between this view and much of the rhetoric emanating from Silicon Valley, which he implies oversimplifies or ignores these second-order geopolitical consequences.

## TL;DR

- Ben Thompson argues that a US "win" in the AI race — especially military supremacy — would be geopolitically destabilising, not triumphant.
- The game-theoretically optimal response of a disadvantaged China could be a catastrophic strike (e.g., on TSMC), making dominance inherently dangerous.
- Thompson sees Silicon Valley rhetoric (particularly from one unnamed AI lab) as dangerously disconnected from this strategic reality.

## Key claims

- A scenario in which the US achieves decisive AI-driven military superiority is described as "very problematic" and "very dangerous for the world," not a win condition.
- Game-theory analysis of US AI dominance suggests China's rational response could be to destroy TSMC, eliminating the global semiconductor supply chain.
- Thompson considers this particular game-theory problem unusually clear-cut, not "convoluted and complex."
- AI is not expected by Thompson to straightforwardly "fix" manufacturing or other real-world problems, limiting the scope of what winning the AI race actually delivers.
- There is a "fundamental disconnect" between Thompson's analysis and the dominant rhetoric coming out of Silicon Valley, attributed implicitly to a specific AI lab.
- The prior Thompson–Patrick conversation focused on [[concepts/aggregation-theory|aggregation theory]]; this conversation signals how much the strategic landscape has shifted since then.

## Key passages

> "I think it would be very problematic for the US to win."

> "What is the game theory optimal response of China? To blow up TSMC. Game theory can get very convoluted and complex. To me, this one actually isn't that complicated."

> "There is a fundamental disconnect that I have with a lot of the rhetoric coming out of Silicon Valley — coming out, I think, of one of the labs in particular — where if we get to a place where we have a meaningful superiority in terms of a military and national security perspective, I think that's very dangerous for the world."
