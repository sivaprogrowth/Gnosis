## Abstract

Written from MWC 2023 in Barcelona, Joanna Stern cuts through the trade-show buzzword haze to assess three technology bets being made by the mobile industry as the smartphone market records its largest-ever single-quarter unit decline (18% in Q4 2022, per IDC). The article profiles executive views from Qualcomm's Cristiano Amon and Samsung's Patrick Chomet on on-device generative AI, mixed-reality headsets, and the Internet of Things. Stern offers skeptical but open-minded personal takes on each theme, noting that while the direction of travel is plausible, most pitches remain solutions in search of a problem. The piece is a useful snapshot of where industry consensus sat in early 2023 before the generative-AI wave fully crested.

## TL;DR

- The smartphone market is in structural decline, prompting the mobile industry to bet on three successor platforms: on-device generative AI, mixed-reality headsets, and IoT/connected devices.
- Qualcomm's "hybrid AI architecture" aims to run models like Stable Diffusion locally on-device, reducing latency and cloud dependency.
- Mixed reality and IoT (including "digital twins") attract the most executive enthusiasm but remain early and use-case-poor.

## Key claims

- IDC recorded an 18% decline in smartphone shipments in Q4 2022—the largest single-quarter drop ever—and an 11% annual fall.
- Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon argues the next growth phase lies in IoT and connected cars, not smartphones per se.
- Qualcomm demonstrated Stable Diffusion running on a Snapdragon-powered phone entirely offline in under 15 seconds, illustrating its "hybrid AI architecture" combining cloud and on-device processing.
- [[companies/samsung-electronics]] is actively integrating generative-AI tools from [[companies/microsoft]] and [[companies/google]] across its phone operating system and services, and is co-developing a mixed-reality headset with both companies and Qualcomm.
- HTC's Vive XR Elite, shipping March 2023, is positioned as a lighter, more comfortable mixed-reality device; CEO Cher Wang envisions full workdays spent in blended virtual/physical environments.
- The "digital twin" concept—pairing physical objects (e.g., aircraft) with live virtual simulations—was a recurring theme at MWC 2023, with wearables floated as a future source of personal digital twins for health simulation.
- Stern concludes that generative AI on phones is genuinely promising but immature, while mixed reality remains "a solution in search of a problem" until headsets shrink and battery life improves.

## Key passages

> Smartphones really are groaning. Unit shipments recently marked the largest-ever single-quarter decline, according to analyst firm IDC, with an 18% drop in the 2022 holiday quarter. Shipments also fell 11% for the year.

> On a phone powered by one of Qualcomm's latest Snapdragon chips, I typed "impressionist painting of an interview at a conference." In under 15 seconds, I had an AI-generated image created by Stable Diffusion, a text-to-image model developed by Stability AI. The magic trick? The phone did it all without a connection to the internet.

> Qualcomm refers to it as "hybrid AI architecture," which combines cloud and on-device processing.

> Sure, a headset can offer a more immersive 3-D experience than any phone or laptop. But the technology continues to be a solution in search of a problem. The work applications are intriguing, but the headsets have to shrink in size—while getting better battery life—to make them devices we really want to wear.

> Mr. Chomet talked about a future where "we have a distributed set of devices that are most adapted to the human senses—eyes and ears and maybe smell." The phone will be the center of the network, but headsets, earbuds and watches will become more capable and independent.
