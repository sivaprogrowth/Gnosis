## Abstract

This *Economist* article examines the rise of fractional investment platforms among young South Koreans, who invest in unconventional assets such as music royalties, fine art fragments, and even livestock. Driven by a weak jobs market, high property prices, and a cultural appetite for early-stage investing — illustrated by the fact that 7% of [[samsung-electronics]] shareholders are teenagers — platforms like Musicow, Tessa, and Bancow have attracted millions of users. South Korea's Financial Services Commission (FSC) is navigating a regulatory tension: classifying some of these assets as securities under a framework analogous to the American Howey test, while simultaneously declaring Musicow an "innovative financial service" and greenlighting security tokens. The article cautions that fractional assets carry financial risks comparable to traditional assets, even if they are perceived as safer alternatives to crypto or equities.

## TL;DR

- Young South Koreans are piling into fractional-investment platforms offering stakes in music royalties, art, and cattle — driven by a tough jobs market and limited property access.
- South Korea's FSC is simultaneously tightening regulation (classifying some platforms' assets as securities) and encouraging growth (approving security tokens and tax exemptions on fractional profits).
- Despite the perception of safety, fractional investments carry risks equivalent to traditional assets, as some early investors are discovering firsthand.

## Key claims

- Eight in ten South Koreans in their 20s and 30s invest in stocks, cryptocurrencies, or other assets, per a 2021 survey.
- 7% of shareholders in [[samsung-electronics]] are teenagers, reflecting how young Koreans start investing early.
- Musicow, launched in 2016, has 1.2 million users trading fractional rights to music royalties, and returned nearly 9% in 2022.
- Platforms like Tessa allow investors to buy fragments of fine art for as little as 1,000 won, earning returns when paintings are rented to galleries or auctioned.
- Bancow lets users invest in calves and split the profit with the farmer when the animal is sold.
- The KOSPI dropped over 20% in 2022 and the Luna cryptocurrency crashed, making fractional-platform returns look appealing by comparison.
- The FSC has classified Musicow's assets as securities and is applying the Howey test — an American standard — to evaluate other fractional platforms.
- The South Korean finance ministry has ruled that fractional-share profits will not be subject to dividend or capital-gains taxes, while securities firms are rushing to partner with these platforms.

## Key passages

> "It's the only way to get rich and make honest money without breaking the law," says Mr Kim.
