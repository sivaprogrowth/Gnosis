## Abstract

This Forbes investigation examines the divorce case between Zoho CEO and cofounder Sridhar Vembu and his estranged wife Pramila Srinivasan, who alleges that Vembu secretly transferred the bulk of Zoho's intellectual property and shareholding to family members — primarily his sister Radha and her husband — without her knowledge or consent, potentially depriving her of her share under California's community property law. The article documents a series of corporate restructurings between 2010 and 2015 that moved Zoho's IP from the U.S. to India, resulting in Vembu holding only 5% of the India-based entity while his sister holds 47.8% and his brother 35.2%. The piece raises questions about the valuation of that IP sale ($50 million), whether it was an arm's-length transaction, and whether the restructuring was motivated by tax benefits, a pre-divorce asset manoeuvre, or legitimate business rationale. Forbes values the Vembu family's combined stake at over $4 billion, making the stakes of the divorce dispute enormous.

## TL;DR

- Pramila Srinivasan alleges Sridhar Vembu secretly transferred Zoho's IP and the majority of its shares to his sister and brother without her knowledge, potentially violating California community property law.
- Vembu counters that he never reduced his financial interest and that the restructuring was a legitimate, IRS-audited arm's-length transaction driven by business and rural-development goals.
- Forbes values the Vembu family's combined Zoho stake at over $4 billion, with Vembu himself holding only ~5% (~$225 million) while his siblings hold the vast majority.

## Key claims

- Srinivasan filed in California court alleging Vembu made "fictitious transfers or 'sales'" of community assets to family members without cash consideration and without her knowledge.
- Under California community property law, a spouse cannot secretly dispose of jointly-owned assets without the other spouse's consent; Srinivasan's attorney argues the 50-50 requirement was circumvented.
- The key IP transfer occurred around 2010–2011, when a new Indian entity (Zoho Corporation Private Limited, ZCPL) was incorporated and Zoho's U.S. intellectual property was sold to it for $50 million — but Srinivasan's filings claim there is no evidence the payment was ever made.
- Post-restructuring cap tables show Vembu's sister Radha holding 47.8% (est. $2.2 billion) and brother Sekar holding 35.2% (est. $1.6 billion), while Vembu retained only ~5% (~$225 million).
- A deposition from the siblings' paternal uncle, Ram Srinivasan, suggests Radha told him the shares are actually Sridhar's money and that she was asked by him to hold them, with the uncle speculating Vembu will reclaim them after the divorce.
- The IRS audited Zoho's IP transfer beginning in 2014 and, according to Vembu, concluded in 2017 that it was a "legitimate arm's length transaction," though Srinivasan's legal team has not received the IRS letter.
- Zoho had $922 million in revenue for the year through March 2022 and 80 million software users, underscoring how much is at stake in the valuation dispute.
- Tax incentives — including India's special economic zones that allowed tax-free software exports before 2012 — may have been a motivation for shifting IP ownership to India.

## Key passages

> "My husband of 29 years not only abandoned me and his son with special needs in 2020," Srinivasan states in a January court filing, "he decided to make fictitious transfers or 'sales' of our most valuable community asset to his family members without their paying any cash or other consideration, and without ever telling me or asking my permission."

> "The community property law in California does not allow a spouse during marriage to secretly dispose of assets without obtaining the other spouse's consent. After all 'community' property in effect means jointly owned—a duty to be transparent with your spouse and not to engage in secret transactions to try to evade the 50-50 legal requirement."

> "I never transferred any shares to anyone and my (and therefore Pramila's) financial interest in these entities never went down, so the question does not arise of me hiding anything." — Sridhar Vembu, in email to Forbes

> 'She replied that I just got sucked into it..It is Sridhar's money," Ram Srinivasan said, claiming that Radha told him that "Sridhar asked me to hold it." … "In my opinion, after his divorce is all done, the whole money will go to [Sridhar] one way or another."

> "There is a pattern of successful entrepreneurs whose spouses have supported them, and weird things happen…and the spouses get left in the lurch. It's sad." — Charles Kolstad, partner at Withers Worldwide

> "Our family was destroyed by the tragedy of autism. Pramila was a super-mom of autism, and she truly made heroic efforts… I was so depressed that I considered suicide at one point. Eventually I found my own relief to my suffering through service to rural poor in India." — Sridhar Vembu
